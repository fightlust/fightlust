Conditioned Vigilance and the 90-Day Intensive: Mastering Spiritual Endurance

This episode explores how to deepen your spiritual freedom by practicing conditioned vigilance, cultivating holy curiosity, and committing to the 90-Day Intensive. Learn how to stay strong during the 'long middle' of your journey, avoid distractions, and rewire your brain for lasting victory. Discover practical steps for daily accountability, post-fall recovery, and shifting from avoidance to adoration of Jesus.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to Chapter 29 — The Power of Persistence — where I talk about what it really means to guard your heart in the long, grinding middle of the battle. You see, going deeper into freedom isn’t just about big victories or dramatic breakthroughs. It’s about conditioned vigilance — keeping your shield raised even when the sky looks clear, even when you feel like you’ve got everything under control. It’s staying transparent with your support team, your Legion, when pride whispers, ‘You’ve made it.’ It’s refusing to let little distractions — the ‘foxes’ of our day — nibble away at the main thing God has called you to. And here’s the truth: real character isn’t forged in the flashy moments. It’s forged in the quiet, daily choice to stay focused on Jesus when no one’s watching. I also talk about holy curiosity — the idea that if you treat the Bible like a dry textbook, you’ll get bored and your mind will wander back to old habits. But if you treat it like uncharted territory, an infinite ocean of discovery, your focus stays sharp. You start asking the Holy Spirit, ‘Show me something new about Your holiness,’ and reading becomes less of a duty and more of a delight. And then there’s the 90-Day Intensive — a tool I give readers to rewire their brains, because freedom isn’t just a one-time decision. It’s a season of discipline, a commitment to three months of showing up, even when it’s hard. Today, we’re diving into why persistence matters, how to swap avoidance for adoration, and why the ‘long middle’ is where your love for the King is truly tested. Let’s get into it. Here’s the thing about the Bible—if you treat it like a dry, dusty textbook, your mind’s gonna wander right back to the old habits you’re trying to leave behind. But what if you saw it as an adventure? Like uncharted territory waiting to be explored? That’s holy curiosity. It’s not about forcing yourself to read; it’s about letting the Holy Spirit guide you to new parts of God’s character you’ve never noticed before. Ask Him questions like, “Lord, show me something about Your holiness I’ve never seen,” or “Reveal the hidden manna in this chapter today.” Suddenly, your daily reading stops feeling like a chore and starts feeling like a treasure hunt. You’re not just memorizing words—you’re discovering layers of meaning, like finding gold coins in a chest you didn’t know was there. This kind of curiosity keeps your spiritual batteries charged because you’re not just going through the motions. You’re actually excited to see what God will show you next. And when you approach Scripture this way, the old temptations lose their grip. Why? Because your mind is too busy marveling at the infinite ocean of God’s truth to get stuck in the same old ruts. It’s like swapping a boring spreadsheet for a thrilling journey—your heart stays engaged, the stagnation root gets pulled up, and reading the Bible becomes a joyful way to connect with the Author of your story. So instead of gritting your teeth through another chapter, let your imagination run wild. What secret about God’s love or power might this passage hide? The key is to stay a student of the Light, because when you’re fascinated by the Creator, the darkness doesn’t stand a chance of intriguing you anymore. The 90-Day Intensive isn’t some punishment—it’s science. Your brain needs about three months to reset after addiction, so this plan gives you a roadmap. Think of it like three acts: the first month is raw, like David who felt irritable and gray but stuck to filters and daily texts to his accountability partner. The second month? The cravings fade but you might hit a wall of numbness, where David found strength in reading Psalms out loud. By the third month, things shift—like David passing a triggering billboard without even flinching. The key is daily check-ins with your partner, tracking progress not by how you feel but by staying consistent. If you stumble? You don’t start over—you fix the hole. Maybe a skipped prayer or a phone in the bedroom? Patch it with specific fixes, like charging your phone in the kitchen. And remember, the first month might hit hard as old emotions thaw, but that’s your brain healing. This isn’t about perfection; it’s about building new habits. When Day 90 hits, you won’t just have a ‘streak’—you’ll feel like a new person, with old patterns overgrown and fresh paths solid. It’s not magic—it’s biology, discipline, and grace working together. So sign that 90-day contract, send it to your partner, and take it one day at a time. The freedom you’re after isn’t just surviving; it’s thriving, with a brain rewired and a heart refocused on what really matters. Here’s the thing about falling back into old patterns—it doesn’t just happen out of nowhere. There are warning signs, like little red lights on your dashboard, that you can learn to spot before things spiral. Think of it like driving: if your engine starts overheating, you don’t wait till it explodes; you pull over and check the coolant. Relapse works the same way. The yellow flags—those subtle shifts—are things like skipping your morning prayer time, letting pride whisper, “I’ve got this,” or drifting emotionally from your goals. These aren’t tiny sins; they’re cracks in the wall. If you ignore them, they become breaches. The key is to fix those cracks early. For example, if you notice you’re scrolling your phone when you should be reading Scripture, the fix isn’t just a generic “be better”—it’s something specific, like charging your phone in another room or setting a daily alarm to close the app. And if you do stumble? Don’t crash into shame. Use the rise protocol: Reflect on what went wrong, Investigate the root cause, Speak truth over yourself, and Equip yourself with a new boundary. The goal isn’t to punish yourself but to gather intelligence so you don’t repeat the same mistake. It’s like debugging code—you don’t rage at the error message; you trace the glitch and patch it. The real victory is in catching the drift at the yellow zone, not waiting till the red flag blares. That’s where freedom lives—not in perfection, but in learning to read your own heart and course-correct before the storm hits. So next time you feel that tiny shift—whether it’s distraction, pride, or a skipped habit—ask yourself: What’s the crack I need to seal today? Because the war’s already won when you stop waiting for the emergency and start fixing the warning signs. Here’s the thing: when you choose to fill your heart with the presence of Jesus instead of letting it stay empty, you’re not just avoiding sin—you’re adoring the King. It’s like shifting from gritting your teeth through a battle to dancing in the victory that’s already yours. The 90-Day Intensive isn’t about white-knuckling your way through temptation; it’s about rewiring your brain to crave His presence more than anything else. Start by writing out your commitment—‘I, , promise to walk this 90-day path’—and send it to your Legion partner. Every morning, text them a quick update: ‘Day 12. Still clean. Still fighting.’ It’s that simple thread of connection that keeps you from slipping back into the dark. When you feel the ‘yellow flags’—like skipping prayer or letting stress creep in—pause and adjust your course before it turns into a full-blown stumble. If you do fall, don’t throw in the towel. Ask, ‘Where did I break? Was it a place, a time, an emotion?’ Then fix the hole with one clear action—like charging your phone in the kitchen or praying through resentment. The goal isn’t just to stay pure; it’s to let His presence flood your soul until you’re so captivated by Jesus that the old ‘excitement’ of sin feels dull. This weekend, try the Glory-Soak: sit somewhere quiet, open your Bible, and spend 30 minutes just staring at His beauty. No requests, no to-do list—just letting His light fill you. When you do that, the darkness loses its grip, and your heart starts to beat in rhythm with His. You’re not just ‘trying’ anymore; you’re living in the freedom He already gave you. Say it out loud: ‘I fill my heart with Jesus. My eyes are fixed on the King. My goal is His presence, not just purity.’ Watch how those words change your focus, your energy, and your whole way of living. Victory isn’t a grind—it’s a celebration of the One who already won.
