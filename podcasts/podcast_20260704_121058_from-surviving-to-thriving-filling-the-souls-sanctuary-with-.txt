From Surviving to Thriving: Filling the Soul's Sanctuary with Kingdom Joy

This episode explores the critical shift from merely avoiding sin to actively filling the soul’s vacuum with Kingdom joy. Drawing on the principle of the 'Expulsive Power of a New Affection,' we uncover why 'not sinning' is insufficient and how heart excavation, replacement dopamine, and spiritual reoccupation lead to true freedom. Practical tools include crafting a 'Kingdom Joy-Map,' reclaiming mental real estate, and weaponizing adoration to out-pleasure the counterfeit. Discover how to transform your biology and mind from hijacked systems into instruments of God’s mission.

================================================================

In my book *fightlust*, Book 1: *The Belt of Truth*, Chapter 9 is titled *Going Deeper*—and it’s where we dig into why simply avoiding sin isn’t enough to win the battle over addiction. Picture this: a man clean for six months but still trapped, his house swept but empty. He wasn’t free because he’d swapped one problem for another—surviving the hijack, but never filling the sanctuary. That’s the danger of a vacuum. Jesus warns in Matthew 12 that an empty heart invites worse enemies back, seven times stronger. Your brain and spirit were never meant to stay hollow. The truth is, sexual sin isn’t just a bad habit—it’s a symptom. It’s your nervous system screaming for joy, connection, or purpose that you’ve been missing. Maybe you’re using porn to escape loneliness, to numb stress, or to spice up a boring life. But here’s the breakthrough: freedom isn’t about blocking the bad; it’s about chasing the infinitely better. We’re talking about filling that empty house with *Kingdom joy*—the kind that comes from creating, serving, connecting, and adoring the God who made you. It’s not willpower versus temptation; it’s learning to love Jesus so much that sin feels like a broken toy you’d never pick up again. Today, we’re unpacking how to rewire your brain, reoccupy your mind, and finally trade survival for a life that’s satisfying, creative, and free. Let’s go deeper. Here’s the thing about addiction: it’s not just about bad choices or weak willpower. Deep down, it’s your brain and heart trying to solve a problem they don’t know how to fix. Think of that guy I told you about—clean for six months but still miserable. Why? Because he stopped the sin but left his soul empty. The enemy doesn’t just want you to sin; he wants you to feel like nothing else matters. So here’s the truth: when you chase that dopamine hit, you’re not really looking for the sin itself. You’re chasing something *behind* it. Maybe you’re lonely and using a screen to feel connected. Or stressed and needing a ritual to numb the pressure. Or bored and craving a fake adventure to make life feel exciting. Those cravings aren’t random—they’re your heart screaming for real connection, peace, or purpose. The key is asking that hard question: *What was I really looking for in that moment?* Was it the thrill of a fantasy, or the ache of feeling invisible? Once you dig up the root—loneliness, stress, boredom—you stop fighting the symptom and start healing the wound. That’s where the real work begins. You don’t just need to quit the bad stuff; you need to fill the empty space with *better* stuff. Like building real relationships, finding joy in simple moments, or creating something meaningful. It’s not about white-knuckling your way through life; it’s about training your brain to crave the real, lasting satisfaction that only God and His Kingdom can give. So next time you feel that pull, ask yourself: What’s my heart really hungry for? Then go feed it with something that lasts. Here's the deal: your brain was made to chase joy, but addiction hijacks that system by flooding it with fake highs. The fix isn't just about stopping the bad stuff—it's about filling your life with real, lasting satisfaction. Think of it like your body’s hunger. If you only stop eating junk food but never taste a ripe apple or a warm meal, you’ll keep craving the empty calories. Same with your soul. You need to build a ‘Kingdom Joy-Map’—a list of low-voltage, high-reward activities that reconnect you with God’s design. Start with creation: maybe it’s walking in the woods, painting, gardening, or just watching a sunset. Then contribution: helping someone else, volunteering, or even doing a random act of kindness. Connection matters too—deep talks with a friend, laughing with your family, or joining a small group. And creativity: learning an instrument, building something, or writing. These things don’t give you a quick dopamine spike like a screen hit, but they fill you up in a way that sticks. The goal? Re-sensitize your brain to real joy so fake stuff feels flat by comparison. This weekend, write down five non-tech activities that make you feel alive. Pick two to do. Maybe it’s cooking a meal for someone, taking a hike, or spending an hour journaling. The key is to schedule them like you’d schedule a meeting—because your soul needs this fuel. Over time, these habits will rewire your brain to crave what’s good, not just what’s easy. And when old triggers hit, you’ll have real, satisfying options to pivot to instead of falling back into old traps. It’s not about perfection—it’s about building a life where your heart says, ‘This is enough,’ and your brain agrees. Here's a simple way to think about it: imagine a toddler clutching a broken, dirty toy. If you try to take it away, they scream. But hand them a shiny, brand-new toy, and they drop the old one instantly. That’s the power of a greater love. Sin feels like that broken toy in your hands—something you’re holding onto out of habit, not joy. But when you fall in love with Jesus, with His beauty, His victory, and the real joy He offers, sin suddenly looks like swill. It’s not about forcing yourself to stop; it’s about being so captivated by the King that the old thing no longer matters. The next time you feel a trigger—say, a craving or a temptation—don’t fight it head-on. Instead, pivot to praise. For 60 seconds, focus on adoring Christ. Say out loud how amazing He is, how He’s changed your life, or how much He loves you. This ‘adoration as evasion’ out-pleases the counterfeit. Your brain was made to chase joy, and when you flood it with real, Kingdom joy—like the peace of serving others, the thrill of creation, or the warmth of genuine connection—you rewire your rewards system. You’re not just avoiding sin; you’re replacing it with something infinitely better. Memorize Psalm 16:11: ‘In Your presence is fullness of joy; at Your right hand are pleasures forevermore.’ That’s your declaration. When you spend time in His presence, you’re not just surviving; you’re thriving. You’re filling the sanctuary of your heart with the real thing, and sin? It’s just a broken toy you no longer want. Here's the thing about your brain when it's finally free from the hijack of addiction: it doesn't just stop being broken—it starts becoming creative again. You see, for years, your mind was trapped in a loop where every thought, every decision, was consumed by planning the next fall, hiding the mess, and drowning in shame. It was like living in a mental maze with no exits. But when you start rewiring that system, something amazing happens. You begin to notice you can think deeper about your work, solve problems that used to feel impossible, and actually be present with your family without your mind splitting into a thousand secret tabs. What starts to resurface is this Kingdom vision—the ideas you buried under years of shame, the projects you gave up on, the mission you were made for. It’s not just about surviving anymore; it’s about leading again. You start seeing your life as a canvas, not a cage. And here’s the kicker: the Holy Spirit doesn’t just clean up the mess; He reclaims the space. He gives you this ‘sanitary wash’ that clears out the junk, so you can finally use your brain for what it was designed—creation, connection, and purpose. So, what does that look like practically? Maybe it’s picking up a paintbrush you haven’t touched in years, or writing that story you kept dreaming about, or volunteering somewhere that makes you feel alive. You’re not just filling time; you’re filling your mind with Kingdom work. And when old triggers hit? You don’t just fight them—you pivot to praise, to creativity, to something that out-pleases the old junk. You trade the saltwater of fake joy for the Spring of Life. The bottom line? Your brain isn’t just healing; it’s being reoccupied. And that reclaimed real estate? It’s not for you—it’s for the mission you were made to fuel.
