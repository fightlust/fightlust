From Frozen to Fluid: Mastering the Healing Journey and Forgiveness for Lasting Freedom

This episode walks you through the four-stage healing process—excavation, thaw, treatment, and restoration—and reveals why forgiveness is the key to unlocking spiritual freedom. You'll learn how to stop numbing pain, rewire your brain for holiness, and release the toxic grip of unforgiveness that keeps you trapped in cycles of sin. Practical steps include journaling your 'Admissions Policy,' identifying false bandages, and applying the Debt-Release Protocol to break relational chains. Whether you're stuck in microwave expectations or fear the thaw, this episode gives you the tools to move from crisis management to radiant wholeness.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 3, Shoes of the Gospel of Peace, we turn to Chapter 16 — The Healing Journey — where I talk about how healing isn’t a one-time fix but a step-by-step process. Think about it: if you broke your arm, you wouldn’t expect it to be 100% in a day. You’d follow the doctor’s plan, change bandages, and trust the slow work of bone knitting. Your soul heals the same way. But here’s the problem — our culture wants instant results. We say a prayer, expect trauma to vanish overnight, and when it doesn’t happen, we start doubting God or ourselves. That’s not how the Great Physician works. This chapter walks you through the four stages of healing: first, digging up the buried wounds you’ve numbed for years. Then, the hard part — thawing out. That means stopping the flight from pain and actually feeling the grief, anger, or shame you’ve been running from. It hurts, yeah, but that ache is proof you’re alive. Numbness is the dead; pain is the living. Once you thaw, the real work begins — letting God bind up your wounds with His patience, His truth, and His timing. And finally, restoration — not just patching cracks but becoming whole again. But here’s what I want you to hear loud and clear: this takes time. You’ve got to stay on the operating table. Today, we’re diving into why rushing your healing backfires, how to face the ‘holy pain’ of emotional thaw, and the three critical movements — forgiveness, self-mercy, and professional help — that turn your healing from a survival mission into a radiant walk. If you’re ready to trade microwave expectations for the Master Surgeon’s steady hand, stick with me. This isn’t just about feeling better — it’s about becoming unbreakable. Here’s the thing: for years, you’ve been using sexual sin like a numbing agent, right? It’s been your way to freeze out the pain you didn’t want to feel—like a cryogenic freezer for your heart. But here’s the hard truth: God can’t heal what’s frozen. To thaw your heart, you gotta stop running and actually feel the ache. I know it sounds scary because when you stop numbing, the pain gets loud. You might even think you were ‘safer’ when you were sinning. But that so-called peace was just the silence of a dead heart. The real deal is this: pain is proof you’re alive. It means the Holy Spirit is flowing into those neglected parts again. Think of it like a healing wound—yeah, it stings, but that’s how you know it’s working. The key is to stay still, let God touch those wounds, and trust He’s not re-injuring you but sealing you for good. And here’s how you start: write down your ‘Admissions Policy’—be honest with yourself and God about wanting to be made well. Then, schedule a ‘Rest Session’ where you literally hand over your shame and guilt to Jesus, feeling the weight lift. It’s not about fixing yourself; it’s about surrendering to the Surgeon who knows exactly what He’s doing. Yeah, it’ll hurt sometimes, but that’s the price of real life. You’re not broken—you’re being remade. Here's the thing about your brain: it's not stuck in the past. God made it to change, to rewire itself when you make new choices. Every time you pick Scripture over scrolling through your phone, you're doing more than checking a box—you're starving the old addiction pathways and paving new highways for freedom. Think of it like this: your brain is a city with roads. The more you drive down the same old sin routes, the smoother they get. But when you start taking detours—saying Psalm 147:3 three times a day, five minutes each, seven days in a row—you're building brand-new roads. The 3x5x7 system isn't just memorization; it's training your mind to reroute traffic from brokenness to healing. And here's the kicker: self-forgiveness isn't about letting yourself off the hook. It's about trusting that Jesus already did the heavy lifting. When you carry guilt like a backpack full of rocks, you're basically saying, 'I know God forgave me, but I'm still going to punish myself.' But that guilt isn't from Him—it's a lie that says your past defines you. The Cross says, 'Paid in full.' So when you choose to stop punishing yourself, you're not being weak. You're declaring, 'I believe the finished work of Christ is enough for me.' It's like swapping out a leaky roof for a brand-new one. Your brain starts to wire itself for hope, not shame. And the best part? This isn't some abstract idea. It's you, right now, making daily decisions to let God's Word and His grace rewrite the story playing in your head. The old patterns? They’ll fade if you keep choosing the new path. It’s not magic—it’s muscle memory for your soul. Healing isn’t a one-time fix—it’s a journey that takes time, patience, and three key moves. First, you’ve got to *give* by letting go of the right to be angry or a victim. That means forgiving those who hurt you, not because they deserve it, but because you deserve freedom. Holding onto bitterness is like drinking poison and hoping someone else dies; it traps you in a cage of resentment. Then, you’ve got to *receive*—accept the mercy Christ already gave you. Stop punishing yourself for sins He already paid for. If God says you’re free, your guilt is just you trying to run the court instead of trusting His justice. Finally, *recruit* help. Healing isn’t something you do alone. Invite Christian counselors into your life—it’s not weakness, it’s wisdom. Think of it like building a fire team: you’re stronger with backup. When you combine forgiveness, self-mercy, and professional support, you shift from just surviving to thriving. You stop being a broken person waiting for a fix and become a whole person walking in purpose. It’s messy, it’s slow sometimes, but every step forward is the Surgeon at work. Trust Him, even when the pain feels too raw. That’s where real freedom begins. Forgiveness isn’t about pretending the hurt didn’t happen or feeling warm fuzzies toward someone who messed you up. It’s a legal move you make in your heart—a way of saying, ‘I’m cutting the debt. You don’t owe me anything.’ The Greek word for forgiveness, aphiēmi, means canceling a debt or sending something away. Think of it like tearing up a bill. When someone steals from you—whether innocence, trust, or time—you start keeping a mental ledger of what they owe. But holding onto that debt traps you. You’re stuck watching their life, nursing bitterness, and carrying around that ledger like a heavy backpack. Forgiveness is you walking up to that ledger, ripping it into pieces, and tossing it. You’re not letting them off the hook; you’re letting yourself off the hook. You trust God to handle justice, and you walk free. This isn’t weakness—it’s strength. It’s the only way to stop using pain as a reason to stay stuck. A lot of people confuse forgiveness with pretending everything’s okay. But no—bad things happened. The difference is, you’re not letting those bad things define your future. You’re not letting the person who hurt you control your emotions or your choices. The Debt-Release Protocol is simple: name the debt, declare it canceled, and throw it away. Then, you turn to self-forgiveness. If you’re holding onto shame for your own mistakes, that’s part of the ledger you need to tear up too. You’re not erasing the truth—you’re choosing not to let the truth weigh you down. Say it out loud: ‘I release you. I release me.’ That’s how you unlock the door and step into freedom.
