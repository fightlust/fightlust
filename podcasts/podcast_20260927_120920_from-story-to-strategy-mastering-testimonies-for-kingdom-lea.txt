From Story to Strategy: Mastering Testimonies for Kingdom Leadership

Discover how to transform your personal journey into a precise tool for spiritual leadership using the 3-3-3 testimony structure. Learn why rules matter in sharing your story, how to honor the King, and the paradox of keeping freedom by giving it away. This episode equips you to lead others out of darkness while protecting your own heart.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we turn to Chapter 12 — The 3-3-3 Testimony. This chapter is all about turning your story into a weapon for the Kingdom. You see, when you’re sharing your journey, emotions can flood in, shame can creep back, or old habits might try to twist your truth into a tragic fairy tale. That’s why I created the 3-3-3 structure — it’s your guardrail, your safety net. Picture this: a three-minute testimony split into three parts. First, the *Mess* — your life before freedom, but keep it clean, no graphic details, just the suffocating cost of the cage. Then the *Miracle* — how Jesus stormed in, how the Legion became your army, and how the Holy Spirit rewired your heart. Finally, the *Message* — today’s reality: walking in light, mind being healed, and your destiny reclaimed. But it doesn’t stop there. You also share three *Lessons*: fighting from victory, leaning on community, and chasing heart-healing roots. And three *Rules*: honor God by not glamorizing sin, honor listeners by not trauma-dumping, and honor the absent by speaking grace, not blame. Oh, and I’ll teach you the 60-second ‘blitz’ version for those hallway moments. This isn’t just storytelling — it’s strategic. Because when you lead with this framework, you’re not just sharing pain; you’re handing someone the keys to their freedom. And here’s the kicker: true freedom isn’t kept by hoarding it. It’s multiplied when you give it away. So we’ll talk about building your squad, recruiting from the shadows, and avoiding the hero trap — because your job isn’t to save anyone, it’s to point them to the only real Savior. Let’s dive in. Here’s the deal: when you share your story, you gotta follow three rules to keep it Kingdom-focused and not just a pity party. First, honor the King by not making your sin sound cool or interesting. Sin is boring and destructive, so keep 90% of your time on the Light—on how Jesus rescued you. Don’t spend forever talking about the ‘darkness’ and skim over the victory. People need hope, not a horror show. Second, honor the listener by knowing where they’re at. If they’re new, be gentle and protect them; if they’re further along, be honest but still kind. Don’t dump your trauma on someone just because you feel like venting. Ask yourself, ‘Is this for their good or my ego?’ Lastly, honor the people who hurt you by not slandering them. You can say, ‘My dad was hard on me,’ without badmouthing him. Speak grace, not bitterness. If you can’t talk about them without anger, wait till you’re ready. These rules turn your testimony into a weapon for freedom, not a chance to gossip or play the victim. It’s about pointing to Jesus, not yourself. Keep it simple, keep it safe, and let your story be a lifeline for others. You don’t need a title or a fancy role to start leading—just look around at the people you already know. Find the guy in your ‘middle circle’ who’s tired of his own excuses, the one who feels trapped but doesn’t know how to get free. He’s the one Jesus is calling you to help. Use your 60-second blitz to share your story quickly: ‘I used to be stuck in secrecy and shame, but the King set me free. I’m walking in freedom now, and I want to stand in your foxhole with you.’ Then invite him into a fire-team—maybe two or three others—to walk through the tools together. Teach them the reject method, how to set digital boundaries, and the basics of heart healing. Leadership isn’t about waiting for permission; it’s about taking initiative. You’re not a hero or a savior—just a guy who’s one step ahead on the trail, handing back the flashlight so others can see the path. If you’re not careful, you might fall into the hero trap, thinking your strength or advice is what’s keeping them free, but your job is to point them to Jesus. Stay humble, keep your own Legion squad close for accountability, and remember: the goal isn’t to build dependents, but to build believers who can stand strong on their own. The enemy will target you harder now that you’re leading, so protect your boundaries—no mentoring alone, no blurred lines with others, and no letting anyone lean on you more than they lean on Christ. This is how you multiply freedom: by stepping into your role as a guide, not a crutch, and trusting the Commander to do the real work through you. Here’s the thing about leadership—when you start helping people, it’s easy to fall into the trap of thinking you’re the one saving them. I’ve seen it happen. You get a little ego boost from someone thanking you or looking up to you, and suddenly you start believing you’re the hero. But that’s a dead end. The real danger is called the Messiah Complex, where you think your strength or advice is what’s keeping people free. That’s not just wrong—it’s dangerous. Your job isn’t to be their savior. Your job is to point them straight to Jesus. If you try to take over, you’ll burn yourself out, and worse, you’ll trap them in a new cage where they’re following you instead of the King. I’ve learned the hard way that staying small is how the King stays big. When you give them your ‘great advice’ instead of God’s Word, you’re stealing their chance to connect with the real source of freedom. When you let them rely on you instead of Christ, you’re building dependency, not believers. And if you start enjoying the gratitude or the spotlight a little too much, you’re not leading—you’re just feeding your own ego. That’s identity vandalism in action. The fix? Keep your eyes on Jesus and your mouth focused on His victory. Invite your Legion squad to watch your back while you mentor others—never work alone. Draw hard boundaries, especially with people of the opposite sex, to protect everyone’s heart. And if someone starts clinging to you instead of the King, push them back toward Jesus, even if it’s hard. Leadership isn’t about being a hero. It’s about being a signpost, pointing the way to the Commander who already did the saving. Stay humble, stay focused, and let the King get all the glory—or you’ll end up leading people into a cage you can’t unlock. Here's the thing about leading others in their freedom journey—you gotta set some rock-solid boundaries, or you’ll end up dragging both yourself and your team into trouble. First off, never mentor someone all by yourself. Always have your own Legion squad keeping an eye on you while you help others. Think of it like hiking with a guide—you don’t want to be the only one with the map. If you’re flying solo, you’re not a leader; you’re just setting yourself up for a fall. Second, keep things strictly professional when it comes to gender. Mentoring someone of the opposite sex is a recipe for disaster, no matter how good your intentions are. The emotional stuff that comes up during healing is intense, and even a tiny crack in the wall can let in all sorts of mess. Protect your marriage and theirs by keeping that line clear. Lastly, don’t let people lean on you like a crutch. Your job isn’t to carry them—it’s to point them toward Jesus. If someone can’t make a decision without your approval, you’re not building a believer, you’re creating a dependent. Push them back to the King so they grow strong, not clingy. These boundaries aren’t about being cold or distant; they’re about keeping the focus where it belongs—on the Commander, not on you. When you stay protected, you’re free to lead with clarity and courage, and your team gets to follow without getting tangled up in someone else’s mess. Stay grounded, stay guarded, and watch the Kingdom move through your leadership.
