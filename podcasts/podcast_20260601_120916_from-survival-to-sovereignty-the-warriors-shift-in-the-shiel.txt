From Survival to Sovereignty: The Warrior's Shift in the Shield of Faith

This episode explores the critical shift from a defensive 'not-defeat' mindset to an offensive 'occupation' mindset in spiritual warfare. We'll uncover how redefining freedom as a platform for intimacy, purpose, mission, and legacy transforms your daily walk. Learn how to fix your eyes on Jesus as your ultimate focus and discover the power of setting Kingdom-centered goals to reclaim your life. Walk away with practical steps to turn your reclaimed energy into a weapon for God's Kingdom.

================================================================

In the book fightlust, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to Chapter 26 — The Goal of Freedom — where the conversation shifts from just surviving to truly thriving. This chapter asks the big question: Why are you fighting? Are you stuck in a cycle of ‘not doing the same thing again’ or are you chasing something bigger? The truth is, if you only focus on what you’re running from — like guilt or shame — you’ll burn out fast. It’s like dieting forever without ever eating real food. But what if freedom wasn’t just about stopping bad stuff? What if it was about running toward a life so full of purpose, intimacy, and impact that the old habits don’t even compare? That’s the heart of this lesson. We’re talking about redefining victory. Instead of just avoiding the cage, you’re stepping into a life designed for you by God — a life that includes deeper relationships, a mission to help others, and a legacy that outlives you. The Bible uses a powerful image here: Hebrews 12:1-2 tells us to ‘run with endurance, looking unto Jesus.’ That’s not about gritting your teeth and powering through; it’s about locking your eyes on a finish line so bright it makes the struggle worth it. And here’s the kicker — your freedom isn’t an end in itself. It’s a launchpad. Think of it like a superhero getting their powers: what do they do? They use them to protect others, fight for justice, and build something better. Today, we’re unpacking how your freedom becomes fuel for the life God made you for — and why your ‘why’ has to be bigger than just ‘not messing up.’ Let’s get into it. So here's the deal—freedom in Christ isn't just about stopping bad stuff. It's about being set free for something way bigger. Think of it like this: when you walk in purity, you’re not just checking off a rulebook. You’re unlocking four huge areas of life that only you can fill. First, intimacy. Imagine looking your spouse in the eye without hiding behind shame. No more sneaking glances at screens when you should be connecting. Real love? That’s the prize. Then there’s purpose. Every hour you used to waste scrolling? That’s now fuel for your unique gifts. You were made for more than mindless clicks—you’ve got a Kingdom assignment waiting. Third, mission. Your past isn’t just a problem to fix; it’s a message to share. You’ve been through the fire, so you can lead others out of their cages. And finally, legacy. You’re breaking cycles in your family tree. No more secrets, no more patterns of shame. You’re building a home where kids grow up seeing integrity and the Spirit’s power. The cost of fighting? It feels huge when you’re just trying to survive. But when you see freedom as a launchpad for these four things—real relationships, your calling, helping others, and a godly family tree—it’s not about sacrifice anymore. It’s strategic. Every no you say to distraction is an investment in something way more valuable. You’re not just reclaiming time. You’re reclaiming your life’s purpose. And that? That’s the kind of freedom that changes everything. So here's the deal with Hebrews 12:1-2—it’s not just about running a race or checking off a to-do list. The verse says to ‘lay aside every weight’ and ‘run with endurance, looking unto Jesus.’ But the word for ‘looking’ here is super specific. In Greek, it’s *aphorō*, which means to lock your eyes on one thing and ignore everything else. Like, you’re staring so hard at the finish line that the sidelines, the noise, the distractions—they just fade away. That’s the whole point. If you spend all your time staring at your sin—even if you’re trying to fight it—you’ll stay stuck in a ‘sin-conscious’ loop. You’re always thinking about what you’re avoiding, and that wears you out. But when you fix your gaze on Jesus—His character, His victory, His love for you—something shifts. You start becoming ‘son-conscious’ instead. You’re not just trying not to mess up; you’re remembering who you are in Him. It’s like training your brain to notice the Light so much that the darkness feels small. And here’s the kicker: purity stops being this exhausting ‘don’t do this’ thing and becomes a natural result of hanging out with Him. You’re not perfect, but you’re abiding. You’re choosing to look at the King so much that the old patterns lose their power. It’s not about white-knuckling your way through life; it’s about letting His presence be the engine that keeps you going. Every day, you’re deciding—what’s your focus? Is it the weight you’re trying to甩 off, or the Prize you’re running toward? Because when you train your eyes on Jesus, you’re not just avoiding failure. You’re chasing a relationship, a purpose, a life that’s rooted in His victory. And that? That’s where real freedom starts. Here's the deal: if you want to prove that life outside the cage is better than life inside it, you need to set clear, specific goals that show you're embracing the Light. Let me break it down. First, pick a relational goal—like sharing your heart with your spouse by a certain date and committing to weekly heart-to-heart dates without phones or distractions. That’s about building real intimacy, not just avoiding shame. Next, a spiritual goal—maybe memorize key parts of the Armor of God or start a 30-minute morning routine with the Word to anchor your soul. This isn’t just checking boxes; it’s training your focus to stay on Jesus. Finally, a mission goal: find a way to serve in your church or community using your unique skills, and look for someone else stuck in the cage you’ve escaped. These goals aren’t just tasks—they’re proof that your freedom has purpose. When you chase these things, you’re not just surviving; you’re building a life that matters. You’re turning from a victim of your past into someone who shapes their future. The enemy wants you to waste your energy on emptiness, but now you’re giving that energy a job to do. That’s how you make sure your freedom isn’t just about saying no—it’s about saying yes to something bigger. And that’s how you stay captivated by the Light instead of looking back at the dark. You know how the world judges success by money or social media likes? Well, the Kingdom of God measures life differently. It’s not about what you get—it’s about what you give. When you shift from chasing titles and trophies to helping people flourish and glorifying God, the empty high of old habits just... fades. Think about it: your energy, your time, your creativity—those things don’t just disappear when you break free. They come back full force, and if you don’t channel them into something meaningful, you’ll end up bored, restless, and vulnerable again. But if you use that reclaimed energy to serve others, to build something that lasts, to live out the unique purpose God made you for? That’s when life starts to feel real. You’re not just surviving; you’re thriving. And here’s the kicker: your purpose wasn’t invented after you got clean. God already had it ready while you were still in the struggle. Your past, your pain, your personality—it’s all been shaped into a mission only you can do. So stop trying to outdo everyone else. Focus on being a blessing, on stewarding what you’ve been given, and suddenly, the old cravings don’t matter. You’re too busy making a difference to waste time on things that drain you. That’s significance. And that’s freedom.
