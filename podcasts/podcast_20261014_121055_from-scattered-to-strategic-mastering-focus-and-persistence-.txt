From Scattered to Strategic: Mastering Focus and Persistence for Kingdom Impact

This episode guides you through transforming reclaimed time into purposeful action by auditing your schedule, identifying spiritual weights, decluttering digital distractions, and embracing persistence as the foundation of your Kingdom mission. Learn practical tools like the 'Sacred No,' the Mary and Martha strategy, and how to reframe your work as worship.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to Chapter 27 — Living with Purpose — where I explain how freedom isn’t just about reclaiming time, it’s about reclaiming direction. You see, if your recovery has given you two extra hours a day, what are you doing with them? Are you filling that space with more scrolling, more noise, more of the same old patterns? If so, you’re letting the enemy steal your freedom in a subtler way. That’s why I challenge you to do a ‘purpose-audit’ this week — look at your schedule, find the gaps where you zone out, and ask yourself: What’s my ‘Prime Time’ for morning watch and training? When can I serve others in ‘Ministry Time’? How will I build real connections in ‘Relational Time’? This isn’t about being busy; it’s about being directed. True purpose means using your willpower like a rudder to steer your life toward what matters. And here’s the game-changer: your job — that 9-to-5 you used to dread — is a battlefield where you declare victory. When you view your work as a Kingdom assignment, it stops being a source of stress and becomes a trophy of grace. But here’s the catch: the enemy won’t attack you head-on anymore. Instead, he’ll distract you with ‘weights’ — good things that drain your energy, like endless scrolling, shallow relationships, or a ‘hobby’ that eats your day. In Chapter 28, we’ll dig into how to spot these hidden thieves, clear the mental clutter, and master the ‘Sacred No’ so you can say yes to what truly matters. We’ll look at Mary and Martha’s story, why comparison kills focus, and how to protect your heart from the ‘compare-trap.’ This isn’t just time management — it’s about running the race God set before you without letting the world’s noise steal your momentum. Let’s get clear on what’s slowing you down — and set your heart free. Here’s the deal: even good things can trip you up if you don’t watch them. The Bible talks about this in Hebrews 12:1, where it says we need to lay aside every weight and sin that’s holding us back. Now, sin is obvious—like lust or lies—but weights are trickier. They’re not bad on their face, but they slow you down. Think of it like a marathon runner wearing a heavy coat and backpack. The coat isn’t illegal, but it’s going to make them lose. A weight could be a hobby that eats up your time, a social group that feels fun but leaves you empty, or work that’s become about proving yourself instead of serving God. Even scrolling through your phone nonstop or getting sucked into news cycles? That’s noise, not fruit. The enemy doesn’t always attack with big sins anymore. He’ll slip in things that feel harmless but drain your energy, making you too tired to chase what matters. So ask yourself: is this helping me run toward Jesus, or just making me sweat? If it’s not fueling your mission, it’s probably a weight. And here’s the hard part—sometimes these weights are things you *like*. Cutting them out feels like a loss. But if you want to stay free, you’ve got to prune the clutter. Turn off notifications that aren’t from real people. Mute the stuff that agitates you. Learn to say no to the good so you can say yes to the best. Because the race God set before you? It’s not about doing more. It’s about running with purpose, unburdened, so you can finish strong. Digital clutter is the biggest weight slowing you down right now. We’re all stuck in this loop of endless scrolling, notifications buzzing in our pockets, and headlines pulling our attention every few seconds. Your brain isn’t built for this constant stimulation—it’s like trying to have a deep conversation while a jackhammer’s going off nearby. The enemy knows this. He doesn’t need to trap you with sin anymore; he just needs to keep you distracted, mentally cluttered, and too tired to focus on your Kingdom mission. The fix? Start pruning your digital life like it’s a garden. Turn off every notification that doesn’t come from a real person—no more pings from apps, social media, or news alerts. Ask yourself: does this thing add value to my mission, or is it just noise? If it’s noise, kill it. Uninstall the apps that turn your free time into mindless scrolling. Set limits on how much data you let in each day. And here’s the hard part: refuse to be a passive consumer of every outrage, meme, or trend that pops up. You’re not here to collect digital stuff—you’re here to run a race. When you clear the static, you create space to actually hear the still, small voice of the Spirit. Your brain needs quiet to function at its best, and your prefrontal cortex—the part that makes decisions—can’t do its job if it’s buried under a mountain of distractions. So take control. Audit your phone. Silence the chaos. Reclaim your mind for the mission God gave you. It’s not about being perfect; it’s about creating bandwidth to run with purpose. Here’s the deal: distraction isn’t just about sin. It’s about weights—good things that slow you down. Think of Mary and Martha in Luke 10. Martha was busy serving, doing ‘good’ stuff, but Jesus called her out for being distracted. Mary chose the best thing: sitting at His feet. That’s the heart of the Sacred No. Every time you say ‘yes’ to something that’s just ‘good,’ you’re saying ‘no’ to the ‘best’ thing God has for you. You know how you scroll through your phone at night instead of sleeping? That’s a Sacred No issue. You’re trading rest for noise. Or maybe you agree to a shallow social event just to avoid being ‘rude,’ but you’re missing a deep conversation with someone who really needs you. The enemy loves this because he knows a scattered heart can’t run the race. You’ve got to ask: what’s the opportunity cost? Is this hobby worth missing your morning watch? Does this news feed calm you or just fuel your anxiety? The Sacred No isn’t selfish—it’s strategic. It’s about giving 100% to your Kingdom assignment, not 10% to a hundred things. And here’s the kicker: comparison is a distraction too. Why does his marriage look better? Why is she further along? Stop looking sideways. Your race is unique. Your healing takes God’s perfect time. So today, pick one weight to drop. Turn off a notification. Cancel a ‘good’ meeting. Say ‘no’ to the noise so you can say ‘yes’ to the mission. Remember, you’re not here to be busy. You’re here to be directed. One thing is needed, and it’s yours to choose. Here’s the thing: sticking with it is how you turn your reclaimed hours into real Kingdom impact. You’ve already cleaned up your time, focused on your purpose, and cut out the distractions slowing you down. Now you’ve got to keep going, even when it’s hard. Persistence isn’t about being perfect—it’s about showing up, day after day, and trusting that God’s got a plan for the time you’re investing. Think of it like running a race. You don’t win by sprinting the first mile and then collapsing. You win by keeping your eyes on the finish line, even when your legs burn and the crowd starts to fade. The enemy? He’s out there trying to steal your momentum. He’ll throw comparison at you—‘Why isn’t my progress as fast as his?’—or fill your head with tiny distractions that don’t matter. But here’s the truth: your race isn’t about keeping up with someone else. It’s about finishing what God started in you. You measure your life by the significance of your mission, not by how many checkmarks you get on a to-do list. When you’re tired, when you’re tempted to scroll or binge or people-please your way into burnout, remember this—your ‘yes’ to the best thing is a ‘no’ to everything else. Keep saying that Sacred No. Keep lifting those weights. And every morning, read that mission statement you wrote. Let it fuel you. Let it remind you why you’re here. Because when you run this race with endurance, you’re not just winning for yourself. You’re making space for God to do something eternal through you. And that? That’s what it’s all about.
