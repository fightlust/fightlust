The Three Rules of Honor in Sharing Your Testimony

In this episode, the host examines the three rules of honor for sharing a testimony in a way that glorifies God and uplifts others. Drawing from the principles outlined in the book, the discussion focuses on how to honor the King by avoiding sin-porn, honor the listener by avoiding trauma-dumping, and honor the absent by avoiding blame-shifting. The episode emphasizes the importance of a testimony that highlights redemption over misery and equips believers to share their stories with grace and authority.

================================================================

When it comes to sharing your story, there's a way to do it that honors God and lifts others up. The three rules of honor act like a compass, keeping your testimony focused on the Light instead of the darkness. First, honor the King by avoiding making sin seem interesting or powerful. Second, honor the listener by being mindful of their readiness and not using your story as an emotional vent. Third, honor the absent by speaking about past hurts without bitterness or blame. These rules help you share with purpose, ensuring your words bring restoration, not trauma. Your testimony becomes a tool for the Kingdom when you follow these guidelines.

The first rule of honor is to focus on the King, not the sin. Sin is not compelling — it's destructive and boring. When sharing your story, spend just 10% of your time on the mess and 90% on the miracle of redemption. Let the glory of Christ drown out the noise of the darkness. Your testimony should be a spotlight on the Savior, not a stage for your failures. By keeping the focus on Jesus, you honor Him and give others a clear view of the healing power He brings. It's not about your trauma — it's about His triumph.

The second rule is to honor the listener by being protective and intentional. Your testimony shouldn't be a platform for your pain — it's a tool to uplift. With younger men, be tactical; with veterans, be compassionate. Don't force your trauma on someone unprepared. Ask: is this for their healing or my ego? Your words should build them up, not vent your wounds. You're not there to dump your mess — you're there to pass the baton of freedom. Let your story be a bridge to hope, not a burden to carry.

The third rule of honor is to speak about those who've hurt you with grace and forgiveness. Whether it's parents, exes, or abusers, never slander the absent. Acknowledge the wound without tearing down the person. You're a restorer, not a prosecutor. If you can't talk about them without bitterness, you're not ready to share that part of your story. Your testimony should reflect the authority of one who's already forgiven. This doesn't mean minimizing the pain—it means letting grace shape your voice. By honoring them in this way, you honor the King and open the door for true healing in your life and theirs.
