From Crisis to Covenant: Mastering Healing Through Counseling and Deeper Restoration

This episode explores the critical role of professional counseling in healing from trauma and sexual integrity struggles, and guides listeners through the deeper, ongoing journey of restoration. You'll learn four signs it's time to seek a specialist, how to vet the right counselor, and how to integrate clinical care with spiritual growth. Then, we'll dive into the transformative power of lament, the rhythm of God's unconditional love, and the layered process of wholeness. Discover how to move from survival to thriving by embracing God's thorough work in every corner of your heart.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 3, Shoes of the Gospel of Peace, we turn to Chapter 18—When Professional Help Is Needed. Now, I know what some of you might be thinking: ‘Isn’t faith supposed to be enough?’ Let me be clear—this isn’t about replacing God’s power. It’s about accessing His provision. Think of it like this: if you’re building a house, you don’t skip the foundation just because you believe in the walls. Healing your heart sometimes requires specialists who have the tools, training, and objectivity to stitch deep wounds safely. In this chapter, I walk through four red flags that signal you need a professional ally. Are you dealing with trauma stored in your nervous system? Complex issues like depression or addiction? Progress that’s flatlining? Or relationships teetering on the edge? If any of these hit close to home, this is your invitation to recruit reinforcements. But here’s the beauty—this isn’t a surrender. It’s a strategy. A counselor who follows Christ, is licensed, and specializes in trauma or sexual integrity becomes part of your spiritual team. They don’t replace your Legion or your prayer life; they sharpen both. And if you’re wondering why I’m emphasizing this? Because healing isn’t a solo mission. It’s a coordinated attack on the enemy’s strongholds. So whether you’re at the beginning of your journey or stuck in the middle, know this: God designed His body to function together. Today, we’re talking about when to call in the experts, how to vet them, and why this step isn’t a sign of weakness—it’s a declaration of wisdom. Let’s dive in. So, how do you find the right counselor? First, check their credentials—look for someone licensed as an lpc, lmft, or lcsw. They need to specialize in trauma, addiction, or sexual integrity, not just general counseling. Their faith should matter too; someone who actually follows Christ and integrates that into their work, not just tossing Bible verses into therapy. You’re not looking for a stranger—you want someone who gets both the head and the heart. Now, let’s talk about the elephant in the room: shame. Many of us think asking for help is weak, or that we’re somehow failing if we need a professional. But here’s the truth—this is strength, not weakness. God gives specialists as tools, just like a carpenter needs a hammer. If your healing hits a wall, it’s not a sign you’re broken; it’s a sign you need backup. Fear of being judged? That’s the enemy’s lie. A good counselor isn’t here to fix you—they’re here to walk alongside you, ask hard questions, and help you see patterns you might miss. Start by asking your church, accountability group, or pastor for recommendations. Do a quick search for specialists in sexual addiction or trauma recovery. Once you find someone, be ready to be honest. The first session isn’t a job interview—it’s about trust. Share your story, your struggles, and yes, even the messy parts. A good counselor won’t shock you with clinical jargon; they’ll meet you where you are. And remember, this isn’t replacing your spiritual team—it’s adding a new layer. Your counselor, your Legion, and your faith walk together, not in competition. So take a deep breath, do the research, and give yourself permission to ask for help. You’re not alone in this fight, and God uses people—real, trained people—to bring healing to the deepest wounds. Here’s how to make therapy and prayer work together like a well-oiled team: Treat your counseling sessions like spiritual training. Do the homework your therapist gives you—reflections, exercises, journaling—as seriously as you’d treat your prayer time or Bible reading. If they ask you to track patterns or write out your thoughts, do it. That’s your tactical training for healing. Then, share what you’re learning with your accountability group, but keep it discreet. Maybe say, “My counselor helped me see a pattern I didn’t notice before; can you help me stay alert to it?” It’s like having backup on your team. Also, invite the Holy Spirit into every session. Ask Him to guide the conversation, give your counselor words of wisdom, and help you hear truth even when it’s uncomfortable. Prayer isn’t just something you do before therapy—it’s the foundation. When you pray for clarity, healing, and strength, you’re aligning your heart with God’s purpose for this process. And don’t forget Proverbs 11:14: “Where there is no counsel, the people fall; but in the multitude of counselors there is safety.” That’s your reminder that seeking help isn’t weakness—it’s wisdom. Declare truths like, “Counseling is a clinical shield that protects my future!” or “I reject the shame of needing help; I use every weapon God gives me.” The goal isn’t just to survive your struggles but to thrive, and combining prayer with professional care gives you both the sword and the shield. It’s not about picking one or the other; it’s about building a team that covers all your blind spots. So show up to therapy ready to grow, pray like it matters (because it does), and trust that God uses specialists to bring His healing. You’re not just fixing problems—you’re building a life that lasts. Let me break this down in a way that makes sense. For way too long, we’ve been told that having ‘faith’ means always staying positive, never complaining, and pretending everything’s fine—even when our hearts are breaking. But the Bible is full of raw, honest prayers where people pour out their pain to God. Think of the Psalms, where David screams about his fears and doubts, or Lamentations, where I grieves Jerusalem’s destruction. Even Jesus cried out on the cross, ‘Why have you forsaken me?’ That’s not weakness—it’s faith in action. Lament is giving God your real emotions, your anger, your grief, your ‘I’m still hurting here’ moments. It’s not about being negative; it’s about being honest. When you stop numbing your pain with distractions and start speaking it to God, you’re freeing yourself. You’re saying, ‘I don’t need to fake happiness to be close to You.’ And guess what? God doesn’t want your mask. He wants your heart. Now, here’s the next part: your recovery can’t be about earning His love. The enemy will try to convince you that your purity streak or your ‘good days’ determine how much God accepts you. But that’s a lie. God’s love is a covenant—it’s not based on your performance. You’re loved because of what Jesus did, not because you’re perfect. When you mess up, He’s still for you. When you’re struggling, He’s still for you. Imagine that: walking through life knowing the Father’s love is unconditional. That’s the rhythm you need to rest in. It’s the difference between fighting for His approval and living in His peace. So start crying out. Say, ‘Lord, I’m angry about what happened to me. I’m sad I broke my marriage’s trust. I’m tired of the shame.’ Let it all out. Then, let His love fill the gaps. This isn’t about fixing yourself—it’s about letting God fix everything, one layer at a time. Here’s the thing about healing—it doesn’t usually happen all at once. Think of your life like an old, run-down mansion that’s been neglected for years. First, God shows up and stops the immediate damage, like putting a bandage on a bleeding wound. That’s Him freeing you from the actual harmful behavior. But that’s just the first floor. Next, He starts fixing the hidden stuff, like the broken pipes or faulty wiring—those are the lies you’ve believed, the twisted thoughts that keep pulling you back. Then He moves to the deeper parts, the foundation, where old wounds and trauma are stored. This is the heart surgery stuff, the messy, painful work of digging out roots so they don’t grow back. And finally, once everything’s stable, He opens the grand doors of your life so you can invite others in and share what He’s done. The kicker is, you might think you’re done, only to find a new ‘room’ of struggles you didn’t even know existed. But that’s not a setback—it’s proof God is going deeper, trusting you with more of His restoration. He’s not rushing to finish; He’s a perfectionist who won’t leave a single corner of your heart unhealed. So when you hit a new layer of pain or old habits resurface, don’t panic. That’s the Renovator moving to the next floor, the next wall, the next hidden box. Celebrate it. It means He’s not giving up on you. He’s committed to making your whole house—every room, every wall, every shadow—into a place where His light lives. And the result? A heart that doesn’t just survive but stands strong, confident in His work, ready to say to the enemy, ‘You’ve got nothing left to take here.’ That’s the power of layered liberation—God’s way of making sure no part of you stays broken.
