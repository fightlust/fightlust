From Breaking to Occupying: Mastering the Governor’s Authority for Lasting Freedom

This episode explores the critical shift from breaking free from bondage to occupying your liberated territory with the authority of the Kingdom. You’ll learn how residual guilt operates, how to reclaim your Kingdom rank, and why freedom exists to produce fruit for God’s mission. Practical tools like the R.A.N.K. Protocol will help you daily enforce your identity as a commissioned commander of light.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 2, The Breastplate of Righteousness, we turn to Chapter 14 — Going Deeper — where we’re not just tearing down old chains but learning to live in the freedom they unlock. Think about it: you’ve already smashed the locks, canceled the contracts, and kicked out the squatter. But here’s the truth — winning a battle doesn’t mean you’ve claimed the land. This chapter is about moving from the chaos of breaking free to the calm of truly occupying your soul. It’s about trading the panic of ‘I must stop sinning’ for the confidence of ‘I am a commander of light.’ You see, freedom isn’t just about getting rid of bad stuff; it’s about filling that empty space with the King’s presence. We’ll tackle the sticky guilt that lingers after a stronghold falls, the way the enemy whispers, ‘You’re still dirty,’ even when you’re clean. And we’ll reclaim the rank you lost — the authority to lead, to love, to build — because God didn’t just rescue you to survive; He wants you to thrive. This is where freedom becomes fruitfulness. No more hiding in the bushes — it’s time to step into your seat at the palace table and start making decisions that matter. Let’s get real about what it means to live like a restored commander, not a recovering addict, and how your choices today plant seeds for the legacy you’ll leave tomorrow. Here’s the deal: breaking chains is like being a demolition crew with a sledgehammer—you crash through the locks, clear the rubble, and make space for something new. But occupying? That’s the slow, steady work of building a palace on the cleared ground. It’s not about constantly fighting to keep the hill you took; it’s about ruling the territory you’ve won. When you break, you’re in crisis mode, focused on survival. When you occupy, you’re in peace mode, focused on purpose. You stop seeing your mind as a battlefield to defend and start seeing it as a Kingdom province to develop. Truth isn’t something you grab when the enemy attacks—it’s the air you breathe, the rhythm of your daily life. You’re not just ‘not a slave anymore’; you’re a governor, stepping into authority, building systems, and leading with confidence. It’s like moving from a temporary tent to a permanent home. The chains are gone, but now you’re reclaiming every corner of your heart, your time, your imagination, and filling them with the King’s presence. You’re not just avoiding the old habits; you’re creating new ones that honor your new identity. This shift changes everything. You stop running from guilt and start walking in freedom. You stop reacting to the enemy and start proactively building his kingdom. It’s not about white-knuckling your way through life—it’s about living as the commander you are, day after day, until freedom feels natural, not forced. Even after you’ve broken those deep-rooted chains, your heart might still feel like there’s leftover junk from the past—like a weird ache or a sudden hit of shame that makes you wonder if you’re really free. That’s the enemy’s ghost-pressure. He’s not trying to scare you; he’s trying to trick you into thinking your renouncing didn’t work. Maybe you feel ‘dirty’ in your head even though you know you’re forgiven, or old labels pop up like ‘failure’ or ‘addict’ to make you doubt your new identity. Here’s the thing: those feelings aren’t proof you’re still bound. They’re just echoes from a dead contract. Think of them like static from a broken radio—annoying, but not real. When that ghost-pressure hits, don’t let it win. Speak to it like you’re a boss: ‘I renounce this debris! I am clean because the King says I am clean!’ Ephesians 2:6 says you’re already seated with Christ in the heavenly realms—so your legal status is spotless. Refusing to agree with the ghost isn’t just about feeling better; it’s about proving you’re the owner of your soul now, not a tenant. Every time you say, ‘That’s just static,’ you’re clearing the debris faster and claiming your freedom. It’s like washing old paint off a wall—sometimes the color bleeds under the surface, but you keep scrubbing until it’s gone. Your job isn’t to fix your feelings; it’s to declare the truth so loud the lies can’t hang around. You’re not stuck in the past. You’re a restored commander, and your voice has authority. So next time the ghost-pressure shows up, hit it with a ‘Renounce!’ and remind yourself: the King already signed your clean bill of health. You’re not just surviving; you’re thriving in your new rank. Sexual addiction doesn’t just mess with your brain chemistry—it messes with your whole sense of who you are. It strips you of power, makes you feel like a lowly soldier hiding from your commander, always waiting for the shame to hit. But here’s the truth: Ephesians 2:6 says you’re not stuck in that pit. You’ve been promoted to the heavenly realms, seated with Christ like a general in the King’s palace. That’s not some future hope—it’s your legal reality right now. So you gotta stop acting like a fugitive and start walking like the commander you are. Say it out loud: ‘I am a restored commander of light.’ That’s not just words; it’s a declaration that you’re done hiding. Your freedom isn’t just about avoiding bad stuff anymore. It’s about protecting your family, leading your team, and advancing God’s mission. Every choice you make now isn’t just for you—it’s for the people you’re responsible for. Your kids, your spouse, your Legion—they’re watching. And when you live like the King’s officer you are, you stop letting the past define you. You start using your freedom to build something bigger, to love harder, to fight smarter. So take that rank seriously. Your purity isn’t just about staying clean—it’s about holding the line for others. You’ve got a mission, a legacy, and a command to protect. And the best part? The King’s already won. You just get to live in the victory. The R.A.N.K. Protocol is your daily playbook for staying rooted in your new identity as a commander of light. It’s simple: start your day by Re-Affirming who you are—say out loud that you’re a restored leader, not a recovering addict. Then, Announce your mission for the day, like ‘I will love my family well and honor God at work.’ Next, Name any residual guilt or old lies that pop up, like ‘That shame is a ghost, not the truth,’ and kick it to the curb. Finally, at the end of the day, Know and celebrate one good thing your freedom made possible, like a kind word to your spouse or a step toward your goals. This routine isn’t just a checklist—it’s how you turn freedom into a lifestyle. By morning, you’re owning your rank; by noon, you’re acting on your purpose; by night, you’re counting blessings instead of regrets. It’s like training wheels for your new identity, helping you move from ‘I’m trying not to fail’ to ‘I’m built to lead.’ And guess what? The more you practice this, the more your brain wires itself for confidence, not panic. You’ll start seeing your choices as a general sees a battlefield—strategic, purposeful, and full of Kingdom impact. So grab that mirror this weekend, shout your commission for 20 minutes, and let the world hear you declare: I am a commander, not a casualty. And every time that old guilt tries to sneak in, just say, ‘I renounce you!’—then go build something beautiful with your freedom.
