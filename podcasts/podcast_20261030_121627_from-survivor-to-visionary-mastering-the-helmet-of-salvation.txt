From Survivor to Visionary: Mastering the Helmet of Salvation for Kingdom Impact

Discover how to transition from a 'survivor' mindset to a 'visionary' identity by embracing the 'Mount Already-Done' truth of Christ's finished work. Learn to set strategic Kingdom goals with the smarter framework, align your spirit, soul, and body, and overcome the battle between immediate pleasure and long-term purpose. This episode equips you to lead with restored imagination and design a future rooted in God's victory.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we turn to Chapter 6 — The Power of Vision — where I talk about how to stop staring at your struggles and start seeing your future through the lens of Jesus’ finished work. You know how when you’re climbing a mountain, it’s easy to fixate on the rocks in your path or the distant peak that feels impossible? Well, I explain that the key to walking in vision is realizing Christ already summited that mountain for you. He’s not just holding the flag at the top — He’s reaching down to pull you up. That’s the Mount Already-Done mindset. It’s about trading the survivor’s fear — the guy who’s just trying not to fall back into old traps — for the visionary’s purpose. You’re not just avoiding failure anymore; you’re stepping into the life God designed you to build. And here’s the thing: vision without action is just daydreaming. So in Chapter 7, we get practical. I break down how to turn your restored imagination into smarter Kingdom goals — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound, evaluated, and reviewed. Because if you want to rebuild your life, you can’t just hope for it. You’ve got to slice that mountain into pebbles and move them one at a time. Oh, and I’ll tell you straight — trying to do this alone is a setup for failure. That’s why your Legion squad isn’t just a support group; they’re your accountability partners, holding you to the goals you speak out loud. So whether you’re wrestling with delayed gratification or feeling stuck in the mud, this episode is your blueprint for walking from the summit of Christ’s victory into the future He’s already written for you. You know how some recovery programs leave you just barely holding on, always looking over your shoulder to make sure you don’t slip back into old habits? That’s the survivor mindset—clean but scared, like you’re still in the cage, just with a ‘not today’ sticker on it. You’re focused on not failing, not relapsing, not going back. But OnlyFaith isn’t about surviving; it’s about becoming a visionary. A visionary isn’t just someone who avoids the cage—they’re someone who leads others out of it. Think about it: most recovery leaves you stuck in survival mode, where your identity is tied to your past struggles. You’re always reacting, always afraid. But Kingdom freedom trains you to stop surviving and start designing. You learn the lessons, master the defenses, and then step into the front lines to lead the advance. You’re not just staying free—you’re building a future where you solve problems, heal families, and advance the Gospel in ways you’ve never imagined. The key? Your restored imagination. It’s not about pretending you’re perfect or pretending the past never happened. It’s about realizing that Christ already secured your future, and now you get to live from that truth. You stop looking at the cage and start looking at the summit. And here’s the thing: someone in your life needs your restored imagination to break free too. Maybe it’s a friend, a family member, or even a stranger. Your freedom isn’t just for you—it’s a tool to set others free. So ask yourself: who needs your vision, your courage, your example to step out of survival mode and into the bold, purpose-driven life Christ has already won for them? That’s the real power of OnlyFaith—it doesn’t just free you; it equips you to be a leader in someone else’s breakthrough. So here’s how you actually turn your big dreams into real steps. First, do the Power of Vision Audit: write down where you’re strong, where you’re struggling, and the very next thing you can do. Maybe you’re great at staying focused in the morning but fall apart after work—so your next step could be setting a specific time to review your goals each evening. Then, grab Proverbs 29:18—‘Where there is no vision, the people perish’—and write it out by hand. Yeah, actually take a pen and paper and scribble it. Then say it out loud every morning for a week. It’s not just about knowing the verse; it’s about letting it sink into your brain like a GPS signal. And don’t keep your goals to yourself. Tell someone—your Legion group, a friend, a spouse—and ask them to check in on you. Why? Because shame thrives in secret, but freedom needs light. When you say, ‘Hey, I’m aiming to do X by Date,’ and you let others hold you to it, you’re not just making a plan—you’re building a wall against the ‘I’ll mess up anyway’ voice in your head. It’s like training wheels for your willpower. The audit keeps you honest, the scripture roots you in truth, and the accountability keeps you from drifting. And guess what? Every small step you take—like sharing your password with your wife or locking your phone during a meeting—adds up. You’re not trying to be perfect; you’re stacking wins so the mountain starts to look less like a wall and more like a staircase. The key? Keep it simple, keep it real, and never let your goals live in your head alone. Share them, speak them, and watch how the Holy Spirit turns your ‘what if’ into ‘what’s next.’ So here’s the deal with setting goals that actually work. You know how sometimes you tell yourself, “I need to get better,” but it’s so vague you don’t even know where to start? That’s where the smarter framework comes in. It’s not some fancy system—it’s just a way to make sure your goals are clear enough to actually hit. Let me break it down. First, be specific. Don’t say, “I want to be more disciplined.” Say, “I’ll spend thirty minutes in the Word every morning before checking my phone.” That’s a target your brain can lock onto. Next, make it measurable. How do you know if you’re winning? Track it—like, “I’ll attend every group meeting this month” or “I’ll avoid second looks this week.” That way, you’re not guessing; you’re checking boxes. Achievable means don’t set yourself up to fail. If you’ve struggled for years, don’t aim for perfection overnight. Start small—maybe use the rise protocol when you stumble. Relevant means your goal has to tie into your bigger purpose. Is it about building intimacy? Serving others? If it doesn’t connect to your why, it’s just busywork. Time-bound gives you a deadline. “By the end of the month, I’ll share my story with my pastor.” Deadlines keep you from dragging your feet. Evaluated means you stop weekly and ask, “What worked? What flopped?” and adjust. And reviewed means you tweak the plan if needed—no shame in scaling back if a goal’s too high. Oh, and don’t forget the whole person—spirit, soul, body. Set spiritual goals like a 24-hour Sabbath, soul goals like weekly heart talks with your spouse, and body goals like eight hours of sleep. Why? Because the enemy can’t attack just one part. Finally, break big goals into tiny steps. Rebuilding trust after a decade? Start with telling your wife about the three-second rule tonight. Then share your phone password tomorrow. Then go for a walk and ask how she’s feeling. Every little step is a victory. And share your goals with your accountability group—vocalize them, let them ask questions, and invite prayer. Because secret goals die in the dark. This isn’t about working harder; it’s about working smarter. Proverbs 21:5 says the plans of the diligent lead to profit. So stop winging it and start building. Your future’s waiting, and it’s already won in Christ—now go claim it. Here’s the thing about delayed gratification—it’s not about being a good boy and waiting for a treat. It’s about rewiring your brain to see what’s real. Your flesh wants the Now: a quick fix, a distraction, something that gives a dopamine hit right this second. But the Kingdom is all about the Later—the joy that comes when you say no to the easy thing and yes to the hard, holy path. Let’s be honest: your body is wired to crave instant pleasure. That’s why junk food tastes good, why scrolling through your phone feels urgent, why you might want to avoid that tough conversation with your wife. But here’s the twist—when you keep choosing the Later, you’re not just resisting temptation. You’re training your brain to trust that the long-term joy of a restored marriage, a clear conscience, or a legacy of faith is way better than the temporary high of whatever you’re avoiding. How do you actually do that? Start by breaking the habit of reacting. When that ‘Now’ urge hits, pause. Ask yourself, ‘Is this thing I want going to matter in a week? A month? A year?’ Then lean on your Legion. Tell your accountability partner exactly what you’re fighting. Let them call you out when you’re about to make a choice that looks good in the moment but will haunt you tomorrow. And don’t forget the Holy Spirit—He’s not just your therapist; He’s your reprogramming tool. Every time you pick the hard path, you’re not just winning a battle. You’re building a future where the ‘Later’ isn’t a sacrifice. It’s a celebration of the life Christ already won for you. So next time you’re tempted to grab the low-hanging fruit, remember: the real feast is waiting.
