From Fences to Freedom: Mastering Boundaries for Spiritual Victory

In this episode, we explore how healthy relationships and godly boundaries are essential for spiritual freedom. Learn to diagnose toxic dynamics with the 'Fruit Audit,' shift from consumer mindsets to covenant-based connections, and build protective hedges in your inner circle, grey-area relationships, and opposite-gender interactions. Discover why boundaries aren’t walls but fences with gates—and how to enforce them with biblical wisdom.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 3, Shoes of the Gospel of Peace, we turn to Chapter 22 — Healthy vs. Unhealthy Relationships — and I want to cut straight to the heart of what this chapter is really about. You see, relationships aren’t just about who you’re with or how often you see them. They’re about whether those connections are fueling your freedom in Christ or quietly pulling you back into the shadows. I’ve spent years watching how these dynamics play out, and I’ll tell you this: a healthy relationship leaves you feeling *full* of Kingdom vision, not drained by emotional weight. It’s like two warriors sharpening each other with truth and grace, not tearing each other down. But the tricky part? Spotting the unhealthy ones. That’s why I created the Fruit Audit — four simple questions to ask about your top three relationships. Does this person love you enough to tell you the ugly truth, even when it stings? Can you be honest about your struggles without fear of judgment? If your answer is no, that’s a red flag. And here’s the thing: boundaries aren’t about building walls to isolate yourself. They’re about building fences with gates — protecting your heart while still inviting the right people in. I’m not talking about being cold or distant. I’m talking about being wise. When you start seeing relationships through this lens — not as a consumer looking for what others can give you, but as a covenant-keeper focused on how you can help others flourish — everything changes. That’s the shift we’re diving into today. So if you’ve ever felt stuck in a dynamic that left you empty instead of empowered, this is for you. Let’s walk through how to identify those draining patterns, how to set boundaries that actually work, and how to build relationships that push you deeper into God’s freedom. And I promise, it’s not about being harsh or cutting people off — it’s about creating space where your new identity in Christ can thrive without compromise. Let’s get started. Here's the thing: a lot of us approach relationships like we're shopping for something. We're always asking, 'What can this person give me? How can they make me feel better? How can they fill this void in me?' But here's the problem—when we do that, we're setting ourselves up for disappointment. Because when the person we're relying on fails to meet our expectations—which they always will—we get mad, we manipulate, or we shut down completely. It's like trying to hook our self-worth to someone else's performance, and it never works. The shift we need to make is flipping that script. Instead of being a 'consumer' who's always taking, we become a 'covenant-keeper' who's focused on giving. So we start asking, 'How can I help this person grow? How can I protect their values? How can I support their mission?' When you do that, it changes everything. Suddenly, you're not desperate for validation from them—you're getting your identity and peace from Christ. And when you're rooted in Him, you can actually love others freely, without needing anything in return. It's like a waterfall effect: the more you pour into others, the more you overflow with grace and truth. This isn't about being a martyr or pretending to be perfect. It's about creating real, healthy connections where people aren't codependent or power-struggling. You're not 'leeching' off their energy; you're 'abiding' in Christ's strength and then 'spilling' that love onto them. And that's what builds trust, breaks cycles of unhealthy patterns, and creates the kind of deep, lasting relationships that actually help everyone involved grow. It's not easy, but it's so much more freeing than trying to use people to fix yourself. When you stop chasing validation and start walking in covenant, you start seeing relationships for what they're meant to be—partnerships where everyone can flourish, not just survive. Let me tell you about walls versus boundaries using a gardener’s story. Imagine a guy who plants a beautiful garden, spends weeks tending the soil, and finally gets it just right. Now, if he doesn’t build a fence, what happens? Rabbits and raccoons trample it. Even the healthiest garden can’t survive without protection. That’s where boundaries come in—they’re like fences with gates. They let the right people in, the ones who’ll help you grow, while keeping out the things that’ll destroy you. Walls, on the other hand, are different. A wall is built when you’re hiding from shame or fear. It’s thick, impenetrable, and it cuts you off from healing. You might feel safe behind it, but you’re also stuck. No light gets in, no growth happens. Boundaries are smarter. They say, ‘I’m not hiding, but I’m protecting my heart.’ Think of God—He set boundaries in Eden, told the ocean where to stop, gave us the Ten Commandments. He’s not a cosmic killjoy; He’s a gardener who knows His design needs protection to thrive. When you build a boundary, you’re not being selfish. You’re being wise. You’re creating a safe space where people can love you truthfully, where you can be vulnerable without fear. It’s like an operating room for your heart—controlled, clean, and healing-focused. Walls trap you in isolation; boundaries connect you to freedom. So if you’re struggling with purity or relationships, ask yourself: Are you building fences with gates, or hiding behind walls? Because the right boundaries let the Kingdom in and keep the mess out. So, where do you start building these boundaries? Usually, they fall into three main areas. First, your inner circle—like your spouse or accountability partners. These are the people closest to you, and the boundaries here create a safe space for trust. For example, maybe you decide no phones in the bedroom at night or give your accountability partner access to your browser history. These aren’t punishments; they’re about protecting your progress and showing your spouse you’re serious about change. Second, the grey area relationships—coworkers, old friends, or people who drain your energy. If a coworker always brings up crude topics or an old friend pushes you into risky situations, you might set a boundary like limiting time with them or avoiding certain places. It’s about distancing yourself from enablers who don’t respect your new path. Third, opposite-gender interactions—this one’s non-negotiable. Protect your marriage by setting clear limits: no meeting alone, no secret texting, no emotional dumping outside your marriage. Keep communication with others professional and open. These boundaries aren’t about being cold or legalistic; they’re about being wise. If you don’t build these hedges, the enemy will find a way in. Your recovery depends on it. Here’s the thing: if you set a boundary but don’t follow through, it’s just a polite request. And in the middle of a war for your heart, polite won’t cut it. You’ve got to back your fences with action. If someone keeps walking into your garden and trampling your plants, you can’t just sigh and say, ‘Hey, maybe don’t do that.’ You’ve got to block their number, end the friendship, or avoid that triggering place. Your purity is worth more than trying to be everyone’s favorite. Now, let’s talk about the questions you’re probably asking. Should you tell your date about your struggle? Yeah, eventually. You don’t need to dump your whole history on the first date, but before things get serious, they deserve to know. If they care about you, they’ll respect your honesty. If not, better to find out early. What if someone just won’t respect your limits? That’s on them, not you. A boundary is something *you* control. If they won’t stop crossing the line, build a wall for a while—even if it feels awkward. You’re protecting your heart, not hurting theirs. And no, it’s not wrong to avoid ‘spiritual’ people who still trip you up. Even good folks can be triggers if their energy strains your recovery. Step back until you’re stronger. This week, take action: First, find your ‘low spot’—that one spot where your fence is broken. Is it a person? A place? A habit? Name it. Second, write your protocol. Be specific. Instead of ‘I’ll try to be careful,’ say, ‘I won’t use my phone after 10 pm.’ Third, pick one person and set a boundary. Just say it plainly, like, ‘I’ve decided to stop to protect my health. Thanks for your support.’ Then, memorize Proverbs 4:23—‘Keep your heart with all diligence…’—and let that truth anchor you. Speak it out loud: ‘I guard my heart. My boundaries are fences of freedom. I choose the uncomfortable no over the sinful yes.’ You’re not building walls of shame. You’re building hedges of protection so your garden can grow. The enemy loses ground when you act. Your recovery becomes a lifestyle, not just a wish. You’re worth the fight. Now go fix that fence.
