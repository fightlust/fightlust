From Vision to Victory: Building Kingdom Goals with Prophetic Imagination

This episode explores how to transform your spiritual vision into a tactical plan for lifelong freedom. We'll break down the smarter framework for setting Kingdom-ready goals, discuss the holistic restoration of your spirit, soul, and body, and learn how to rewire your brain for delayed gratification. Through the example of Moses and practical steps for neural retraining, you'll discover how to find joy in the 'slow build' of your journey and align your daily choices with your eternal purpose.

================================================================

In the book fightlust, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we turn to Chapter 7 — Setting Kingdom Goals — where I walk you through the difference between hoping for freedom and actually building a path to it. You see, freedom isn’t just about avoiding mistakes; it’s about designing a future with the precision of an architect. We’re diving into why planning isn’t a lack of faith but a act of obedience — like how God gave Moses blueprints for the Tabernacle or Nehemiah a schedule to rebuild Jerusalem. You’ll learn the smarter framework for goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound, Evaluated, and Reviewed. This isn’t vague wishful thinking; it’s about carving out daily steps that turn your vision into reality. And we’re not just talking about spiritual goals — no, we’re tackling spirit, soul, and body together. Because true victory means restoring every part of you. I’ll also break down how to slice huge mountains of change into pebbles you can move today, and why hiding your goals in the dark is a surefire way to fail. This is about building a future where freedom isn’t a fleeting hope but a structured, sustainable reality — and I’ll show you exactly how to start. Let me break down the smarter framework for you—it’s like a roadmap to turn fuzzy wishes into real wins. First, be specific. Don’t just say, 'I want to be more pure.' That’s too vague. Instead, pick a clear action: 'I’ll read Scripture for thirty minutes every morning before checking my phone.' That gives your brain a target it can actually hit. Next, make it measurable. How do you know you’re winning? Track it—like committing to attend every group meeting this month or avoiding second glances all week. Numbers and checkmarks beat guesswork. Now, keep it achievable. If you’ve struggled for years, don’t aim to never feel tempted again. That’s a setup for shame. Focus on what you *can* do, like using the rise protocol within ten minutes of a stumble. Stretch, but don’t snap. Then, ask if it’s relevant. Does this goal tie into your bigger 'why'—like deeper connection, purpose, or legacy? If not, drop it. Time-bound means setting a deadline: 'I’ll share my full story with my pastor by the end of this month.' Deadlines push you forward. Evaluate weekly. Ask: What worked? What flopped? A goal without review is just a dream you forgot. Finally, review and adjust. If a boundary is too weak, beef it up. If a goal’s too tough, scale back with humility. Be stubborn about your vision but flexible in your tactics. Think of it like building a house—you check the blueprints, tweak the plans, and keep laying bricks. This turns wishful thinking into a battle you can actually win. To build a strong foundation for freedom, you’ve got to tackle life as a whole—spirit, soul, and body. Think of it like building a house: if you skip the foundation, the walls, or the roof, the whole thing falls apart. Spiritual goals are your anchor. Maybe that means carving out time for Sabbath rest, diving into Scripture, or deepening your prayer life. These habits keep you connected to God, the source of your strength. Then there’s your soul—your emotions and mind. That could look like working through past wounds in counseling, reading books that refuel your purpose, or having real talks with your spouse where you both open up, not just talk about the kids or the grocery list. Neglect your soul, and you’ll feel empty, even if your spiritual life is on track. And let’s not forget your body. It’s not just about looking good—it’s about fueling your willpower. Get enough sleep, move your body regularly, and fast occasionally to prove you’re in charge of your habits, not the other way around. If you ignore one of these areas, the enemy finds a gap to slip in. A strong spirit can’t fix a broken body, and no amount of prayer can heal a neglected heart. But when you work on all three, you create a fortress. Freedom stops being a fleeting moment and becomes the default setting. You’re not just fighting for survival anymore—you’re building a life where victory is the norm. It’s like training for a marathon: you can’t just work out your legs and ignore your lungs. You’ve got to prep your whole self. That’s how you make sure the enemy can’t sneak in through any back door. When spirit, soul, and body are all on the same page, you’re not just surviving; you’re thriving, and the path to freedom is clear. Here's the thing—when you're staring at a huge goal, like rebuilding a marriage or fixing a broken habit, it's easy to feel like you're looking up at a mountain and thinking, 'I'll never make it to the top.' But here's the secret: you don't climb the whole mountain in one go. You slice it into pebbles. Break that big goal into daily steps so small they feel almost silly. Like, tonight you might just tell your wife about the 'three-second window' rule you're trying to practice. Tomorrow, you could hand her your phone password so she can check in on you. A few days later, maybe you go for a walk and actually ask how she's feeling about your progress—no small talk, just real talk. Each of those steps is a pebble. You don't need to carry the whole mountain; you just need to move one pebble at a time. The key is focusing on the 'next right step' instead of the whole journey. It's not about finishing the race today—it's about running the mile you're on. When you do this, panic turns into a plan. Every tiny victory adds up, and before you know it, you're halfway up the mountain without even realizing how far you've come. The enemy loves to make you feel paralyzed by the size of the goal, but when you break it down, you take away his power to overwhelm you. It’s like building a house: you don’t try to pour the foundation, roof, and walls all at once. You start with one brick, then the next, and soon you’ve got a solid structure. That’s how freedom works—pebble by pebble, step by step, until the mountain becomes a memory. Here’s the thing: addiction trains your brain to crave quick fixes, like a fast dopamine hit when stress hits. Your brain starts seeing long-term rewards—like a healthy marriage or a legacy of integrity—as boring compared to the instant buzz. But Moses gives us a blueprint for flipping that script. He didn’t deny the pleasure of staying in Pharaoh’s palace—it had luxury, comfort, no risks. But he saw the bigger picture. He chose the hard road, the ‘affliction’ of walking with God’s people, because he fixed his eyes on a reward that mattered more than gold. That’s delayed gratification: saying no to a small, temporary win so you can say yes to something way bigger. And here’s the kicker—your brain can relearn this. Every time you resist a trigger, every time you pick a ‘stone’ in your restoration journey—like memorizing a verse, having a raw conversation, or bouncing your eyes away from a distraction—you’re rewiring your neural pathways. You’re teaching your brain that real joy comes from waiting, not rushing. It’s not about gritting your teeth through the struggle; it’s about finding satisfaction in the process, like a craftsman who loves laying each brick because he knows the cathedral will outlast him. Celebrate the small wins. They’re proof you’re building something that lasts. Over time, your brain starts valuing the slow, steady work of freedom over the empty flash of instant fixes. You’re not just surviving anymore—you’re investing in a future that stretches into eternity. And that? That’s a reward worth waiting for.
