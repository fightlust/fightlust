From Blueprint to Eternity: Mastering Planning, Delayed Gratification, and the Mind of the Victor

This episode unpacks the sacred art of planning as a holy act of faith, the science of delayed gratification, and how to rewire your brain for long-term joy. We’ll explore the smarter framework for Kingdom goals, the three pillars of holistic restoration (Spirit, Soul, Body), and the radical example of Moses who chose eternal reward over temporary pleasure. You’ll learn to break down overwhelming visions into daily steps, leverage accountability for success, and find joy in the 'slow build' of your spiritual restoration.

================================================================

In my book fightlust, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we’re diving into Chapter 7 — Setting Kingdom Goals — where I tackle a question a lot of us wrestle with: Is planning a sign of faith or a lack of it? Let me be clear — the Bible doesn’t just nudge us to trust God; it commands us to *plan* with purpose. Proverbs 21:5 contrasts the ‘diligent’ man who builds walls with the ‘hasty’ man who hopes for miracles. God gave Moses blueprints for the Tabernacle, Nehemiah a construction schedule for Jerusalem’s walls — and He wants us to do the same for our lives. Goals aren’t about relying on our strength; they’re about giving the Holy Spirit a roadmap to work through. Think of them as sutures holding your vision in place while it becomes reality. Now, how do we build goals that actually stick? I introduce the smarter framework — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound, Evaluated, Reviewed. No more vague desires like ‘be more pure.’ That’s a recipe for confusion. Instead, say, ‘I’ll spend thirty minutes in the Word every morning before touching my phone.’ That’s a target your brain can lock onto. But here’s the kicker: you can’t just plan in your head. Your goals need to cover every part of you — spirit, soul, and body. Spiritual goals might be a 24-hour Sabbath rhythm. Soul goals could mean weekly heart-to-hearts with your spouse. Body goals? Eight hours of sleep, three times a week in the gym. Why? Because the enemy thrives in broken systems. When you neglect one area, he finds a loophole. And let’s talk about scale — rebuilding a marriage after a decade? That’s a mountain. But you don’t climb it all at once. Slice it into pebbles: one honest conversation, one boundary, one step at a time. Oh, and never keep your goals secret. Share them with your Legion. Shame loves darkness, but freedom needs light. Let your brothers hold you accountable. This isn’t about perfection; it’s about progress. By the way, we’re also diving into delayed gratification next — because the real battle isn’t just about setting goals, but choosing the hard ‘later’ over the easy ‘now.’ Spoiler: Moses had this figured out. More on that soon. Here’s the deal with smarter goals—they’re like a GPS for your brain when you’re trying to build something real. Let’s say you’re sitting there thinking, ‘I want to be pure,’ but that’s way too fuzzy. Your brain can’t grab onto that like a target. So instead, you turn it into something specific, like ‘I’m going to spend thirty minutes in the Word every morning before I even touch my phone.’ That’s a clear step, right? Measurable means you track it, like counting how many times you actually do that or making sure you have zero second looks this week. It’s not about being perfect; it’s about having proof you’re moving forward. Achievable means don’t set yourself up to fail by picking something impossible, like never having a bad thought. Start small—maybe use the rise protocol within ten minutes of a stumble. Relevant means asking, ‘Does this goal actually matter to my bigger purpose? Is it helping me build a better marriage, a stronger relationship with God, or a legacy I’m proud of?’ If it doesn’t tie into your ‘why,’ drop it. Time-bound gives you a deadline, like sharing your story with your pastor by the end of the month. Deadlines keep you from dragging your feet. Then you evaluate weekly: What worked? What flopped? That’s where your accountability partner comes in—they’ll ask hard questions and keep you honest. And reviewed means you adjust the plan if needed. Maybe your goal was too easy, so you raise the bar. Or it was too hard, so you scale back with humility. The point is, you’re not just winging it. You’re building a system that turns vague dreams into daily actions. And you don’t do it alone—your Legion squad is there to keep your goals out of the shadows, where they die. When you vocalize your goals, invite questions, and ask for prayers, you’re creating a team around you. That’s how you turn a secret wish into a living plan. It’s not about being rigid; it’s about being smart. You’re giving your brain a roadmap, and the Holy Spirit a way to move through you. So start small, stay specific, and let your team hold you accountable. That’s how you build something that lasts. Here's the deal: you can't build a fortress of freedom in just one area of your life and expect to stay strong. You've got to tackle all three pillars—Spirit, Soul, and Body—because leaving any of them open is like leaving a door unlocked for the enemy. Let's break it down. For your Spirit, think about goals that deepen your relationship with God. Maybe that means committing to a 24-hour Sabbath rest, where you actually unplug and recharge, or memorizing a few key Bible verses to carry with you daily. These aren't just checklists; they're the foundation that keeps everything else stable. Now, your Soul is about healing and connection. If you've got deep emotional wounds or patterns that keep dragging you down, counseling could be a game-changer. It’s also about investing in your mind and heart—like reading books that help you understand God’s design for relationships or setting up regular heart-to-heart talks with your spouse where you actually listen to each other, not just talk about the kids or the laundry. And then there’s your Body, which is the physical engine that powers your willpower. Getting eight hours of sleep consistently for a month? That’s not just about feeling rested—it’s about giving your brain the fuel it needs to say no to temptation. Exercising three times a week to burn off stress chemicals? Fasting one meal a week to remind yourself that you’re in control, not your cravings. When you set goals in all three areas, you’re not just checking boxes; you’re building walls around your life. The enemy can’t sneak in through a spiritual gap, a soul wound, or a weak body. You’re creating a setup where freedom feels natural, like breathing. It’s not about perfection—it’s about covering every angle so the path to victory is the easiest choice you can make. Think of it like building a house: you don’t just reinforce the front door and leave the windows open. You secure every entry point so the whole structure stands strong. That’s how you close the door on old patterns and open the door to a life where freedom isn’t a struggle—it’s the default. You ever look at a huge project and feel like you can’t even take the first step? Like trying to climb a mountain when you’re still sitting at the base? Here’s the thing: that mountain isn’t as big as it seems when you break it into pebbles. Let’s say you’re trying to rebuild a marriage after years of betrayal. You don’t have to fix everything tonight. Instead, focus on the next right step. Tonight, maybe you have a honest conversation with your spouse about one specific thing you’re working on. Tomorrow, you might take a practical step, like sharing your phone password or letting them track your location. Then, a few days later, you go for a walk and ask them how they’re really feeling about the progress. Each of those tiny steps is a victory. You’re not trying to rebuild the whole relationship in one day—you’re creating a rhythm of progress. Every pebble you move adds up. The key is to stop staring at the whole mountain and start moving one pebble at a time. You don’t have to see the entire path ahead. Just take the next step, then the next, and trust that the process will carry you forward. That’s how legacies get built—one small win after another. Here's the deal: delayed gratification isn't about being a robot or pretending sin doesn't feel good for a second. It's about choosing what lasts. Think of Moses in the Bible—he didn’t ignore the comfort of Pharaoh’s palace, but he saw through the temporary pleasure. The palace had gold bars, sure, but it was a cage. Moses picked the hard road of walking with God’s people because he was fixed on a bigger reward. That’s how you train your brain. Every time you say no to a quick hit—whether it’s a distraction, a bad habit, or anything that gives you a fast dopamine fix—you’re building new neural highways in your prefrontal cortex. It’s like teaching your brain to see colors it forgot existed. The more you wait, the better you get at waiting. And here’s the kicker: you start to actually enjoy the process. Imagine a stone mason building a cathedral. He doesn’t get frustrated if the roof isn’t done in a week. He takes pride in each block, each chisel mark. That’s your life now. Every small win—like memorizing a Bible verse, having a real talk with your spouse, or just sitting with discomfort instead of escaping it—is a stone in your legacy. And when you zoom out, you realize this life is just the first chapter. The real story is forever. So when you’re tired of waiting, remember: the pain you’re enduring now is building something that’ll outlast your 70 years. You’re not just surviving; you’re constructing a future that matters. That’s the Mind of the Victor—choosing eternal joy over instant fixes, one patient step at a time.
