From Defense to Declaration: Mastering the Shield of Faith

This episode breaks down the tactical art of raising the shield of faith in three steps, contrasts faith with willpower, and reveals how spoken truth becomes a weapon in spiritual warfare. You'll learn to turn daily triggers into practice drills, lock shields with your community, and declare life-giving 'I am' statements that rewire your brain and defeat the enemy's lies.

================================================================

In the book fightlust, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to Chapter 7—How Faith Extinguishes. This is where the rubber meets the road when it comes to fighting the fiery darts that try to hijack your mind. You’ve got three seconds—yes, just three seconds—before a tempting thought hooks into your identity and starts to feel like the truth. So what do you do? I break it down into three moves: first, you Interject—stop that lie dead in its tracks. Don’t argue with it, just say, 'Stop! That’s a lie!' Then you Interpose—grab that 3x5x7 memory verse you’ve been drilling into your brain and mentally throw it up like a shield between you and the trigger. Finally, you Invest—put your full trust into that verse and declare, 'I choose to believe this Word more than I believe my feelings.' This isn’t about white-knuckling your way through life; it’s about swapping willpower for faith. Willpower burns out. Faith? That’s your connection to God’s endless strength. And here’s the thing—your shield isn’t some static thing you only use in a crisis. It’s a muscle. You’ve got to practice raising it during the small stuff—when you’re stuck in traffic, or anxiety flickers about money, or you catch yourself lingering on a screen. Every time you choose God’s truth over your feelings, you’re building that muscle. But don’t go it alone. Lock shields with your accountability squad. When you’re triggered, text them: 'I’m under fire. Raise your shield with me.' That’s how you turn from a lone soldier into an impenetrable fortress. And we’ll dig deeper into how your voice becomes part of that shield—because faith isn’t just something you think, it’s something you speak out loud. Let’s get into it. Here’s the deal: willpower is like a gas tank that runs out. You try to hold on tight, but stress, bad sleep, and constant decisions drain it fast. It’s not sustainable. Faith is different. It’s not about gritting your teeth and pushing through—it’s about trusting that God’s Word is stronger than your feelings. When you raise that shield, you’re not saying, ‘I’m tough enough to resist,’ you’re declaring, ‘God’s truth is powerful enough to stop this.’ Think of it like this: willpower is you carrying the weight of the battle, but faith hands the burden to God. You stop wrestling with your emotions and start relying on His promises. It’s not about feeling strong—it’s about choosing to believe what He says, even when your brain screams otherwise. Faith is a muscle you build by practice, not a feeling you wait for. Every time you swap your doubts for His Word, you grow stronger. The key? Don’t wait until you’re overwhelmed to use it. Start small—like when traffic frustrates you or anxiety nags at you. Say, ‘I choose God’s truth over this.’ Over time, your shield gets bigger, and the enemy’s attacks lose power. It’s not about trying harder; it’s about trusting deeper. That’s how you turn exhaustion into victory. Faith isn’t just a feeling—it’s a choice you make every day, and like any muscle, it gets stronger the more you use it. That’s why I want you to start small. When you’re stuck in traffic or feeling a wave of stress, don’t just let those thoughts roll through your mind. Instead, practice what I call the 10-Time Drill: ten times a day, whenever you notice a negative thought or feeling, stop it dead in its tracks and interpose a Scripture verse. It could be something simple like ‘This too shall pass’ or a promise from God that speaks directly to your struggle. The key is to build muscle memory so that when the big attacks come—when you’re alone, tired, or facing a powerful temptation—you’re already strong enough to raise your shield without flinching. Pick one Bible verse that specifically addresses your biggest challenge—your ‘shield-verse’—and make it your go-to weapon. Maybe it’s Philippians 4:13 for strength, or Psalm 91 for protection. Repeat it out loud, write it down, and let it sink into your heart. Every time you choose God’s truth over your feelings, you’re training your brain to default to faith instead of fear. Over time, you’ll notice a shift. You won’t be at the mercy of your emotions anymore; you’ll be the one steering the ship. And when the real battles come, you’ll be ready. You’ll stand firm, shield raised, and watch the enemy’s fiery darts fall to the ground like wet matches. This daily practice isn’t just about surviving the tough moments—it’s about building a habit of trust that becomes second nature. The more you practice, the easier it gets. What starts as a conscious effort becomes automatic, so even when you’re under pressure, your faith doesn’t waver. That’s how you turn your shield from a fragile defense into an unbreakable wall of protection. Here’s the thing about the shield of faith—it’s not just something you hold up to block attacks. It’s a weapon you use to declare victory before the battle even starts. You see, your words shape your reality. Proverbs 18:21 says, 'Death and life are in the power of the tongue,' which means what you speak creates what you experience. If you let lies about yourself—like 'I’m stuck' or 'I’ll always struggle'—become your default language, you’re feeding those lies into your brain and your soul. But when you flip the script and start shouting truths like 'I am a new creation' or 'I am more than a conqueror,' you’re rewriting your mind and interrupting the enemy’s plans. This isn’t just about thinking positive; it’s about speaking scripture so loud and so often that it becomes the soundtrack of your life. Your voice actually rewires your brain—it breaks the cycle of negative thoughts and creates a new atmosphere where victory thrives. And here’s the kicker: the enemy can’t read your mind, so he’s always guessing if his lies landed. But when you yell the Word of God, you’re giving him a direct answer: 'Nope, not buying it!' You’re also locking in with your community, because when you and your brothers declare truth together, you become a fortress the enemy can’t penetrate. So start small. Practice saying 'I am a temple of the Holy Spirit' every morning. When temptation hits, don’t just think about resisting—scream 'Jesus!' until the fire dies out. Your words are your power, and the more you use them, the stronger your shield gets. It’s not about feeling strong; it’s about speaking strong, loud, and unashamedly for who God says you are. You ever notice how Roman soldiers fought in a tight formation called the testudo, where they locked their shields together to block arrows? That’s the kind of protection we need in spiritual battles. You can’t rely on your own shield forever—eventually, the enemy will find a weak spot. But when you team up with others, you create an unbreakable wall. Here’s how it works: share your shield-verse—the Bible verse you use to fight temptation—with your accountability partner. When you’re triggered, send them a text like, ‘I’m under fire, raise your shield with me.’ Suddenly, their faith becomes your backup. You’re not just fighting alone anymore. And when you both declare, ‘I am a temple of the Holy Spirit,’ the enemy hears that unity and runs. It’s not about waiting until you fail to ask for help. It’s about real-time, side-by-side warfare. Your partner prays your verse over you, and you do the same for them. That’s locking shields. The enemy can’t flank a group. He can’t drain your energy when you’re refilling each other’s tanks. You become a fortress of light they can’t penetrate. So don’t bear it alone. Find your team, practice those texts, and let your ‘I am’ declarations echo together. That’s how you turn weakness into strength and watch the enemy scatter.
