From Armor to Authority: Embracing Your New Identity in Christ

This episode explores the transformative power of the 'Daily Armor Routine' and the five pillars of your new identity in Christ. Learn how to shield your heart with righteousness, defeat shame, and walk in the freedom of your new nature through practical declarations, environment audits, and identity-based decision-making.

================================================================

In the book *fightlust*, Book 2, *The Breastplate of Righteousness*, we turn to Chapter 16—*Freedom Is a Daily Walk*—where the author challenges us to build a life of liberty by starting with a simple but powerful habit: the *Daily Armor Routine*. This isn’t just about checking off a to-do list; it’s about showing up every morning with your spiritual gear on, ready to walk in the freedom Christ has already given you. Think of it like a warrior suiting up before stepping into battle—your belt of truth, breastplate of righteousness, and every piece of armor are tools to protect your heart and mind from the enemy’s attacks. But freedom isn’t just a one-time win; it’s a daily choice. The chapter pushes you to audit your environment, remove triggers, and share your plan with someone who’ll hold you accountable. Then, in Chapter 17, the focus shifts to your *New Identity* in Christ. The author breaks down five pillars that redefine who you are: you’re not stuck in your past, you’re not fighting for righteousness—you already have it, and you’re not just a survivor; you’re a conqueror with a destiny. These truths aren’t just abstract ideas; they’re meant to become your daily declarations, like saying, *‘I am a new creation!’* or *‘I am more than a conqueror!’* to rewire your mind and reject lies that try to drag you back. And it doesn’t stop there. Chapter 18 dives into the *Daily Armor Routine* in action, showing how to ‘put on’ each piece of spiritual armor every morning through prayer and spoken truth. It’s about grounding yourself in who God says you are, not how you feel in the moment. So if you’ve ever struggled to stay free, or wondered why old patterns keep creeping back, this episode is your roadmap. We’re talking about practical steps, powerful declarations, and the mindset shift that turns freedom from a fleeting hope into a lived reality. Let’s get into it. Let me break down these five pillars that redefine who you are in Christ. First, you’re a *New Creation*—2 Corinthians 5:17 says the old you is gone, like a canceled debt or a legal contract burned up. Imagine your past as a broken vase; God doesn’t just patch it, He replaces it with something whole. Your identity isn’t tied to mistakes but to the fresh start He gave you. Then there’s *God’s Righteousness*—2 Corinthians 5:21 swaps your shame for Jesus’ perfection. Think of it like trading your dirty laundry for a clean, brand-new outfit. You don’t earn this righteousness; it’s a free gift. Next, you’re *More Than a Conqueror* from Romans 8:37. Picture a war where the enemy’s already surrendered. You’re not fighting to win; you’re stepping into a victory that’s already yours. The fourth pillar? You’re God’s *Workmanship*—Ephesians 2:10 calls you His ‘masterpiece.’ Imagine a painter restoring a damaged painting; the cracks tell the story of their skill, not your brokenness. Your struggles don’t define you; His redemptive artistry does. Lastly, you’re *Chosen and Holy* from Colossians 3:12. You’re like a sacred temple vessel—set apart, not because you’re perfect, but because you belong to Him. These pillars don’t just change your mindset; they rewrite your story. When shame whispers, ‘You’re damaged,’ you counter with, ‘I’m a new creation.’ When temptation tugs, you say, ‘I’m already righteous.’ This isn’t just belief—it’s living from a truth deeper than your feelings or past. Your identity isn’t a label you wear; it’s the reality you walk in. Declarations are like spiritual reset buttons that flip the script on your mind. When you say out loud, 'I am a new creation' or 'I am more than a conqueror,' you’re not just reciting words—you’re hijacking the enemy’s lies and replacing them with the truth of who God says you are. It’s like putting a stop sign on the highway of negative thoughts. For example, if shame creeps in and whispers, 'You’ll never change,' you can fire back with, 'My victory is already decided!' That simple act interrupts the cycle of doubt and reboots your brain into freedom. Here’s the kicker: you don’t need to feel ready for this. You can create an 'Identity Declaration' card right now—write down the five pillars from your new identity, like 'I am God’s masterpiece' or 'I am chosen and holy.' Carry it with you and read it three times a day, maybe once with coffee, once during lunch, and once before bed. The more you speak these truths, the more they sink into your soul like roots in soil. Over time, your mind starts to believe what you declare, and the old patterns lose their grip. It’s not about faking it till you make it—it’s about waking up to the reality that you’re already made new. So grab a pen, write those declarations, and let your voice become a weapon of liberation. Your words matter—they’re the daily proof that you’re not just surviving, you’re thriving in your new identity. Let’s talk about your environment—because the places and things around you can either protect your recovery or trip you up. Think about it: if you’re trying to stay focused on freedom, but your phone is full of apps or social media accounts that pull you back into old habits, those are like hidden landmines. Same with physical spaces—maybe there’s a corner of your room, a certain website, or even a playlist that whispers, ‘Just one look, just one scroll.’ Today’s challenge is simple: do an environment audit. Walk through your home, your workspace, your digital world, and ask, ‘What here is a trigger I can remove?’ Maybe it’s deleting an app, unfollowing someone on Instagram, or clearing out a messy area that feels like a trap. The goal isn’t perfection—it’s taking one step to guard your mind. Why does this matter? Because the Belt of Truth, part of your spiritual armor, starts with protecting your thoughts. If you let harmful stuff linger in your space, it clouds your thinking and weakens your resolve. But when you clean up your environment, you’re like a soldier tightening their belt before battle—you’re readying yourself to stand firm. So pick one thing to remove today. It could be as small as muting a group chat or as big as resetting your browser homepage. Then declare, ‘I’m not letting this control me anymore—I choose a space that honors my freedom.’ Your environment isn’t just background noise; it’s a frontline in your fight for victory. Here's the deal: your feelings might swing like the weather, but your identity in Christ is solid as a rock. So when the old lies pop up—'You're a failure,' 'You'll always struggle'—don't buy into them. Use the Three-Second Rule: the second you hear that voice, say, 'No! That person died with Christ. I'm a new creation!' It's like hitting pause on the old labels. Now, trust the Holy Spirit as your Internal Manufacturer. He's not just helping you try harder; He's the one growing faith, joy, and self-control in you. That's your new nature at work. Here's a practical step: pick a New Name for yourself, like 'Victor' or 'Freedom,' and tell your partner. Let them remind you of who you are when you're tempted to slip. Every time you face a choice—like resisting a screen or a risky situation—ask, 'Does this fit my new identity?' You're not fighting for freedom; you're living from it. Your identity is already secure, so let that truth guide your actions. The more you do this, the more your daily life will match the new you in Christ. And remember, the Daily Armor Routine isn't just a checklist; it's about starting each day with your spiritual gear on, so you're ready to walk in victory.
