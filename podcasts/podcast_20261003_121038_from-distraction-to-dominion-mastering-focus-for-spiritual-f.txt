From Distraction to Dominion: Mastering Focus for Spiritual Freedom

This episode explores how to identify and remove spiritual 'weights' that hinder your focus, tackle digital clutter as the defining distraction of our age, master the 'Sacred No' to protect your time and mission, and shift from avoidance to adoration to sustain long-term victory. Learn practical strategies to reclaim your mind, stay anchored in God’s purpose, and run the race set before you with unbroken momentum.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to a chapter that’s urgent for anyone chasing freedom—it’s called Avoiding Distraction. Let me ask you: What’s slowing you down right now? Not sin, necessarily, but the ‘good’ or ‘neutral’ things that sneak in and weigh you down? Think of a marathon runner lugging a winter coat and a backpack of rocks. He’s not breaking any rules, but he’s guaranteed to lose. That’s the trap. In this chapter, we dig into the weights clogging your life—hobbies turned obsessive, toxic social circles, the endless scroll of news that keeps you agitated. The enemy hides there, because a heavy heart can’t run. We’ll talk about digital clutter, this constant ping of notifications that trains your brain to flinch every fifteen seconds. Your phone isn’t just a tool; it’s a thief of stillness. You can’t hear the Spirit’s whisper when your mind’s a construction site. Then there’s the ‘sacred no’—the courage to say no to 90% of life’s demands so you can give 100% to your mission. Martha’s story in Luke 10 is our roadmap. She was busy, but Jesus called her out for letting good things crowd out the best. We’ll confront the compare-trap too—the lie that your journey matters less than someone else’s highlight reel. And finally, we pivot from avoidance to adoration. Freedom isn’t just about cutting stuff out; it’s about filling your heart with Jesus so deeply that worldly distractions look… boring. This isn’t about perfection. It’s about momentum. Let’s get clear on what’s holding you back—and how to drop it. Here's the thing about digital clutter—it’s the biggest weight most of us are carrying these days. Think about how your phone buzzes, dings, and flashes every few minutes. You’re in line at the store, waiting for two minutes, and already you’re checking headlines or scrolling through social media. Your brain’s getting trained to jump from one thing to another every fifteen seconds, like a kid bouncing between toys. This constant noise creates this mental static that drowns out the quiet voice of the Spirit. It’s like trying to hear a friend talk in a room full of jackhammers—you know the noise is there, but you can’t focus on what really matters. The fix? Start decluttering. Turn off every notification that’s not from a real person. Unsubscribe from those news feeds that make you anxious or bitter. Limit how much information you let into your brain each day. Instead of mindlessly scrolling through outrage or memes, ask yourself: is this fueling my mission, or is it just making me distracted? When you clear out the digital junk, you’re not just deleting apps—you’re giving your brain space to breathe. Your prefrontal cortex, the part that makes decisions and stays focused, needs that quiet to work properly. Suddenly, you’re not just fighting distraction—you’re taking control of your time, your energy, and your thoughts. What was once a foggy, scattered mind becomes a focused weapon for the Kingdom. You stop being a passive consumer of chaos and start being an active commander of your own clarity. It’s not about perfection—it’s about creating space to hear God, to rest, and to move toward the life He’s calling you to. And honestly? That feels way better than doomscrolling. Here's the thing: most of us say 'yes' to way too much because we're scared of letting people down or missing out. We end up with calendars so full we can't breathe, wondering why we don't have time for prayer, real rest, or deep relationships. But here's the truth—every 'yes' you give to something good is actually a 'no' you're giving to the best thing God has for you. Think of it like this: if you say 'yes' to an extra hour of scrolling through your phone at night, you're saying 'no' to the restorative sleep your body needs. If you agree to a shallow coffee date just to avoid being 'rude,' you might be missing a chance to talk honestly with someone in your Legion who’s really struggling. The problem isn’t the things themselves—it’s what they’re costing you. You have to start evaluating the 'opportunity cost' of your time. What’s the real price you’re paying when you say 'yes' to something that doesn’t line up with God’s unique mission for your life? This is where the 'Sacred No' comes in. It’s not about being rude or selfish—it’s about being a good steward of your focus and energy. You have to have the courage to say 'no' to 90% of the world’s demands so you can give 100% of your heart to the King. It’s radical, but it’s necessary. When you protect your time for what truly matters, you’re not just avoiding distraction—you’re creating space to run the race God has set before you. And that’s where freedom lives, not in trying to do it all, but in learning to say 'no' so you can say 'yes' to your destiny. You know how sometimes you’re trying to do everything—hosting friends, working on a project, scrolling through your phone—and suddenly you realize you haven’t even prayed that day? That’s Martha from the Bible story in Luke 10. She was busy serving Jesus, which sounds great, but Jesus gently called her out. He said Mary had chosen the ‘good part’—sitting at His feet, listening. Martha wasn’t ‘bad’ for being helpful; she just let good things distract her from the best thing. And here’s the truth: distraction isn’t just about sin. It’s about letting *anything*—even hobbies, social media, or busywork—steal your focus from the main mission of abiding in Jesus. Think about it: if your morning quiet time with God gets pushed aside because you’re ‘too busy’ with a hobby, that hobby becomes a weight. If you scroll through news feeds that leave you anxious or comparing yourself to others, that’s a weight too. The fix? Consolidate your life around one thing: Jesus. Prune the hobbies that make you miss your time with Him. Mute the news feeds that stir up agitation or entitlement. Delegate tasks that pull your eyes off the ‘Prize’—His presence. Mary’s choice wasn’t about being lazy; it was about prioritizing what matters most. When you keep your heart focused on abiding in Him, everything else—your work, relationships, goals—falls into place as a rhythm around that center. It’s like building a campfire: if you let too many sticks and logs pile up, the fire gets scattered. But if you clear the space and focus the fuel, the flame burns bright. The enemy loves fragmentation because it makes you ineffective. But when you choose the ‘one thing’—Jesus—you create a momentum the enemy can’t stop. You’re not ignoring life; you’re living it from the right foundation. And that’s where real freedom starts. Here’s the deal: lasting focus isn’t just about saying no to the wrong things—it’s about saying yes to Jesus so passionately that everything else fades into the background. For a long time, we’ve focused on avoidance—‘I won’t look at the screen,’ ‘I won’t check that app,’ ‘I won’t go there.’ But here’s the catch: if you just clear out the junk without filling the space with something better, the old stuff finds a way back in. It’s like cleaning your house but leaving the doors unlocked. The enemy doesn’t need to be loud to win; he just needs you to be distracted, bored, or complacent. The real game-changer is adoration. It’s about being so captivated by Jesus—his character, his victory, his beauty—that the world’s distractions feel… boring. You stop gritting your teeth to resist and start grinning because you’re hooked on something way better. How? You reoccupy your mind. Instead of letting your imagination run wild with escape fantasies, you picture the legacy you’ll leave—the men you’ll free. Instead of replaying toxic memories, you let Scripture verses flood your thoughts. Instead of chasing the next dopamine hit, you anticipate the peace of your morning time with God. And you keep this up even when life feels quiet, because the enemy loves when you lower your guard. The key? Holy curiosity. Don’t read the Bible like it’s a dry textbook—treat it like an adventure, a treasure hunt for truths that blow your mind. When you do this, your holiness stops being a list of rules and becomes a life defined by awe for the King who set you free. You’re not just avoiding the dark anymore; you’re mesmerized by the Light.
