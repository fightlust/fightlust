From Contracts to Covenants: Mastering the Belt of Truth for Lasting Freedom

In this episode, we explore how covenants—unlike temporary contracts—create unshakable safety and purpose in relationships, recovery, and spiritual growth. Drawing from the ancient practice of blood covenants and the sacrificial heart of God, we’ll uncover how to replace performance-based fear with covenantal freedom, heal relational wounds, and transform your sexuality into an act of worship. Whether you’re rebuilding marriage, battling addiction, or seeking deeper intimacy with God, this episode will equip you to live as a covenanter, not a consumer.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 1, The Belt of Truth, I wrote a chapter called ‘Covenant Vs. Contract’ that changed the way I see relationships, recovery, and even my own heart. Let me ask you: Do you treat your marriage, your faith, or your recovery like a contract? A deal that depends on performance, on conditions, on ‘if you do this, I’ll do that’? That’s not what God designed. In this episode, we’re diving into why covenants—those unbreakable, sacrificial promises—are the only foundation strong enough to heal your soul and fight addiction. I’ll take you into the ancient, visceral way covenants were made with blood and bones, like when God walked through the pieces of His promise with Abraham, saying, ‘Even if you fail, I’ll take the penalty.’ That’s the depth of covenant. It’s not about ‘I’ll stay faithful if things are easy’—it’s about ‘I will honor this vow even when it costs me.’ We’ll talk about how addiction is really a contract you make with yourself for a quick hit of relief, but it always leaves you emptier. And we’ll confront the lie that marriage is a contract too—when it’s actually a covenant meant to be a sanctuary, not a transaction. This isn’t just about rules; it’s about shifting your heart from a consumer mindset to a covenanter’s mindset. You’ll walk away understanding why covenants create safety to be honest, healed, and free from the lure of old habits. Let me be clear: This isn’t theoretical. It’s life-changing. If you’ve ever felt trapped by performance-based relationships or stuck in cycles of shame, this is for you. Let’s start breaking contracts and signing covenants—together. Let me paint a picture for you. Back in ancient times, when people made a covenant, it wasn’t just a handshake or a signed paper—it was wild, real, and heavy. They’d kill animals, split them in half, and walk side by side through the guts and blood. It was like saying, ‘If I break this promise, let what happened to these animals happen to me.’ Crazy, right? That’s how serious they took vows. Now imagine God doing something even wilder. In Genesis 15, He makes a covenant with Abraham, but instead of both of them walking through the pieces, God does it alone. Why? Because He’s saying, ‘If Abraham fails, I’ll take the hit.’ That’s the same as Jesus on the cross—He paid the price for our covenants before we even messed up. Here’s the kicker: your relationship with God and your spouse isn’t a contract that you keep or break based on your mood or performance. It’s a covenant. That means you don’t live in fear of ‘What if I mess up and get kicked out?’ You live in freedom because the debt was already paid. When you’re in a covenant, you can be honest about your struggles without hiding, you can heal slowly without rushing, and you stop chasing fake ‘deals’ from the enemy because you’re already in the best one. But here’s the question: Are your relationships built on ‘I’ll stay if you perform’ or ‘I’m in this no matter what’? Audit your heart. Are you trading your peace for a performance contract, or have you signed a blood-bought covenant where the promise is already yours? Here's the thing: sexual addiction is basically a deal you make with yourself. You trade a quick dopamine hit for future integrity. It’s like saying, 'I’ll take this temporary relief now, even if it costs me later.' But the hit fades fast, and the price? It’s way higher than you thought. You end up chasing that same rush, but it leaves you emptier each time. The hidden costs? Shame, isolation, and a fake sense of control that crumbles when the high fades. The good news? You can break that cycle by switching to a covenant mindset. A covenant isn’t about what you can get; it’s about what you can give. It’s a promise to honor your body as a temple, treating your sexuality as sacred, not a tool for quick fixes. This means swapping the old 'I’ll stay pure if...' mindset for a 'I choose to be faithful because...' mindset. It’s about stewardship—asking, 'How can I serve my King and my relationships today?' instead of 'How can I feel good now?' Practically, this looks like drafting a restoration covenant: a written promise to yourself and your Higher Power to walk away from the cycle. You purge habits like secret behaviors or using people for validation, and replace them with acts of service and honesty. You see your body not as something to exploit, but as a gift to steward. And you lean into the truth that your worth isn’t tied to past mistakes. The covenant becomes your daily weapon—not something you grit through, but something that sets you free. Because when you’re rooted in a promise, not performance, the old lures just don’t hold the same power anymore. Hey there, let’s talk about this ‘Consumer-Purge’ thing. So, here’s the deal: we’ve all been there, right? Maybe you’ve got a friend or family member you’ve been treating like a vending machine—using them for validation, attention, or just to feel good. Like, instead of really listening to them or serving their needs, you’re kind of like, ‘Hey, you here to make me feel okay today?’ That’s the consumer mindset. But now it’s time to flip the script. The ‘Consumer-Purge’ is about digging up one specific relationship where you’ve been doing that. Maybe it’s your sibling, your partner, a coworker—someone you’ve been leaning on for emotional gas but not really giving back. Once you name that person, the next step is to commit to serving them instead. Like, not just saying it, but doing it. Maybe it’s calling them first instead of waiting for them to text, or doing something small but thoughtful that shows you care about their needs as much as your own. This isn’t about being a martyr; it’s about building covenants—those deep, promise-based connections where you’re not just taking, you’re giving. And here’s the kicker: when you start serving instead of using, you’ll feel this weird freedom. It’s like, ‘Oh, I don’t need this person to fix me anymore. I get to be here for them, and that’s its own reward.’ It’s not easy, though. You might feel awkward at first, like you’re breaking a habit you didn’t even realize you had. But that’s the point. You’re training your heart to value people as people, not as tools. And when you do that, guess what? Relationships start to feel less transactional. You stop ‘shopping’ for the next emotional hit and actually start connecting. So, pick one person. Name the relationship. Write it down. Then, this week, do something for them that’s not about you. Serve. Watch how covenantal love starts to feel less like a theory and more like a lifestyle. You got this, friend—start small, stay consistent, and let the serving do the rest. Going deeper into freedom means shifting from just following rules to building a real relationship with God. It’s not about gritting your teeth and avoiding bad stuff anymore—it’s about learning to adore the One who made you. Think of your eyes, hands, and body as tools. Instead of using your eyes to stare at screens or objectify people, train them to see others as image-bearers of God. Instead of letting your hands scroll through isolation, use them to serve others and build something meaningful. Your body isn’t just a prison of shame; it’s a temple where God lives. Reclaim it by choosing to honor Him with every action, every thought, every moment. Even if you still feel the weight of past mistakes, remember: God’s forgiveness isn’t a Band-Aid—it’s a full cleanse. Your scars aren’t just reminders of failure; they’re proof of His healing work. When you start seeing your sexuality as part of a bigger story—a symphony where every part of your life plays in harmony with God’s plan—it stops being about self-control and starts being about worship. You’re not just avoiding sin; you’re actively making your life a praise song. The goal isn’t to survive; it’s to thrive, to let your body and heart sing His glory instead of echoing the noise of the world. This is where freedom becomes automatic, where purity isn’t a struggle but a joy. You’re not just fixing your habits—you’re falling in love with the Designer, and that changes everything.
