From Armor to Authority: The Daily Routine for Spiritual Warfare

This episode unpacks the practical and identity-driven tools for staying sober and vigilant in freedom. From midday check-ins to environment management, crisis protocols, and the daily armor routine, we explore how to weaponize your new identity in Christ to resist temptation and walk in lasting victory.

================================================================

In my book fightlust, Book 2, The Breastplate of Righteousness, we turn to Chapter 16 — *Freedom Is a Daily Walk* — where I lay out the gritty, practical work of staying free in a world that’s constantly trying to pull you back. You see, freedom isn’t a one-time victory; it’s a rhythm, a daily habit you have to *choose* again and again. Think about it: most stumbles don’t happen during prayer time or Bible study. They creep in during the quiet moments — the drive home, the mid-afternoon slump at your desk — when hunger, anger, loneliness, or tiredness starts to whisper lies. That’s why I call it the *Midday Check-In*. It’s a simple alarm that forces you to pause and ask: Am I in a halt state? Am I entertaining grey-area thoughts? And, *Lord, help me walk in Your light for the rest of this afternoon*. It’s like spiritual sentry duty, keeping you from drifting back to old habits. But it doesn’t stop there. Freedom also demands you take control of your environment. Are your phone apps triggering you? Are you binge-watching content that objectifies others? Are certain places still tied to your old patterns? You’ve got to redesign your world to *protect* your progress. And when the enemy launches a full-scale attack — that dopamine storm when your willpower is offline — you need a Crisis Protocol. The reject method is your emergency exit: Recognize the trigger, Escape the situation, Cry out to Jesus, Engage your accountability partner, Choose a replacement activity, and Thank God for the win. But here’s the deeper truth I unpack in Chapter 17: freedom isn’t just about doing the right things. It’s about becoming the right *person*. When you’re in Christ, you’re not an ‘addict trying to get better’ — you’re a *new creation*. Your identity isn’t tied to your past; it’s rooted in the victory Jesus already won. That’s the heart of this chapter — and why your daily walk isn’t just about survival, but about living like the masterpiece God says you are. Your environment plays a huge role in your journey toward freedom. Think about the spaces you occupy—digital, physical, and even the media you consume. If your phone is loaded with apps that trigger old habits, or your home has corners tied to past patterns, those things are like open doors for temptation. Start by auditing your digital life: delete apps that act as gateways to unhealthy behavior, update filters to block harmful content, and make your phone a tool for good, not a trap. What you watch, read, or scroll through matters too. If your media habits objectify people or feed destructive ideas, you’re poisoning your mind and making it harder to resist urges. Swap that out for content that honors others and lifts you up. Then look at physical spaces. If certain rooms or routines remind you of old struggles, reclaim them with new, positive habits or avoid them until your new choices feel natural. This isn’t about perfection—it’s about creating a setup where victory is easier. By designing your world to support your goals, you’re building the first wall of defense against the enemy’s attacks. It’s like cleaning out your house before a storm hits; you’re removing the things that could trip you up when the pressure comes. Your environment isn’t just background noise—it’s either your ally or your enemy. Choose wisely, and you’ll give yourself a fighting chance to stay strong every single day. Here’s how it works: When you feel the trigger, immediately name it—'This is an ambush!'—then physically remove yourself from the situation. Close apps, turn off screens, or step outside to break the signal. Call on Jesus right then, asking for strength and clarity. Reach out to your accountability partner with a quick message to stay grounded. Pick an activity that gives you positive dopamine, like listening to music or calling a friend, to replace the urge. Once you’ve weathered the storm, take a moment to thank God for the victory. This isn’t just a reaction; it’s a rehearsed routine that turns panic into power. By practicing reject daily, you build muscle memory so you can act quickly when the pressure hits. You’re not just surviving the temptation—you’re reclaiming control and building a new pattern of response. Over time, these steps become automatic, helping you walk in freedom even when the old trails try to pull you back. The key is to practice it until it’s second nature. When the enemy launches a surprise attack, you’re not scrambling—you’re stepping through each move with confidence, knowing the storm will pass and you’ll walk away stronger. This protocol isn’t about perfection; it’s about preparation. Every time you use it, you’re training your brain to respond from victory instead of fear, turning chaos into clarity and letting God’s power carry you through. Here's the truth you need to hear: you're not just an addict trying to be good—you're a brand-new creation in Christ. When you surrendered to Him, your old self—the one trapped in cycles of shame and failure—died with Christ, and a new you was born. This new identity isn't just a label; it's your reality. First, you're righteous. Not because you've earned it through perfect days, but because Jesus swapped your sin for His perfect righteousness. God sees you as holy and blameless, so the temptation to chase validation through screens or secrets loses its grip. Second, you're more than a conqueror. You're not fighting to survive battles; you're stepping into a war already won. The enemy’s darts fall short because your King has already crushed him. Third, you're God’s masterpiece—a work of art He’s restoring, not a broken tool. Your past struggles don’t define you; they’re proof of His restorer’s work. Fourth, you’re chosen and holy. You’re not common or available for the enemy’s schemes—you’re set apart for God’s sacred use, like a temple vessel reserved for the King. Finally, you’re complete in Christ. No longer scavenging for wholeness in the dark, you’re seated at His feast. These truths rewire your brain: shame loses power, sin feels cheap, and your choices align with who you already are. Stop fighting from a place of brokenness; fight from the unshakable truth of your new nature. When the enemy whispers, 'You’ll always be this way,' declare, 'No—I’m a new creation, righteous, victorious, and complete!' Hey there, friend. Let me tell you something important about the armor of God. You know how soldiers don’t just grab a sword and hope for the best? They put on their full gear before stepping into battle. Well, the same goes for us. The armor God gives in Ephesians 6 isn’t some symbolic metaphor—it’s real, practical protection for your daily walk. Every morning, you need to intentionally ‘put on’ truth, righteousness, the gospel, faith, salvation, and the word of God. Think of it like suiting up for a game. You’re not fighting for victory; you’re fighting from victory. Why? Because the enemy is still out there, aiming fiery darts at your heart. If you skip this routine, you’re basically walking around half-dressed in a war zone. The truth piece? That’s your reality check—declaring who you are in Christ. Righteousness? That’s the armor of God’s own character covering you. The gospel? It’s the reminder that Jesus already won. Faith is your shield, and salvation is your helmet protecting your mind. And don’t forget the sword of Scripture—your offensive weapon to cut through lies. This isn’t a one-time thing. You do this every morning, like brushing your teeth or tying your shoes. It stabilizes your new identity as a conqueror, not a prisoner. When you armor up, you’re not trying harder—you’re trusting deeper. You’re saying, ‘I’m ready to face whatever comes, because I’m covered in what matters most.’ So, take five minutes each day to mentally and spiritually gear up. It’s the difference between standing firm and getting swept away. The enemy doesn’t care how ‘good’ you feel—what matters is who you are in Christ. Walk in authority, not fear. That’s the point of the armor. Now go put it on, and let’s win this thing together.
