From Slides to Strength: Mastering the rise Protocol for Rapid Recovery

This episode dives into the critical difference between a stumble and a slide in recovery, explaining how prolonged surrender weakens your spiritual defenses. You’ll learn the rise Protocol—Repent, Investigate, Speak, and Engage—as a step-by-step tool to stop slides before they take root. Discover the power of speed in rising, the danger of negotiation after a fall, and how accountability partners can rescue you from isolation. Walk away with actionable strategies to turn setbacks into breakthroughs.

A stumble is a single misstep—a momentary lapse where you slip but immediately grab the rock, pull yourself back up, and keep moving. But a slide? That’s something else. It’s when you lean into the fall, let the momentum take over, and spend days, weeks, or even months repaving old neural highways of addiction. Think of it like a mountain ridge: stumble, and you stay on the path. Slide, and you’re tumbling down the cliff. The key? Speed. How fast you rise after a stumble determines everything. Here’s the deal: the difference between a stumble and a slide comes down to how fast you get back up. Maybe you’re bruised, but you’re still standing. But if you freeze, panic, and start making excuses like, 'Might as well fall all the way since I’m already slipping,' gravity takes over and you start sliding. In recovery, that 'gravity' is shame. The moment you mess up, the enemy piles on guilt to keep you stuck in the mud. The key is speed. If you get back into the light within minutes—repent, call your accountability partner, fix the problem—you stop the slide before it gains momentum. But if you wait hours or days, letting shame harden around your choices, you’ve basically handed the enemy a front-row ticket to your downfall. The first five minutes after a stumble? That’s your golden window. Don’t debate, don’t wait for a 'good time' to feel holy again. Act like a soldier: grab the rise Protocol and execute it fast. Because the longer you linger in the mess, the more you rebuild those old bad habits like highways in your brain. It’s not about being perfect—it’s about being *quick*. The faster you rise, the more you prove to yourself and the enemy that you’re not staying in the dark. So next time you slip, remember: your rescue mission starts the second you stop negotiating with the lie and start moving toward the light. Here's the thing about stumbles—they’re actually a gift, not a curse. When you slip up, it’s like getting a GPS signal pointing you straight to the hole in your wall where the enemy snuck in. Let me break it down: maybe you stumbled because you were scrolling on your phone at 11:30 pm in the kitchen (environmental failure)—that’s your brain’s way of shouting, ‘Stop letting screens rule your bedtime!’ Or maybe you gave in because you were holding onto some anger toward your boss and used the stumble to ‘get back’ at him (root cause failure)—your soul’s waving a flag saying, ‘Hey, that bitterness is the real weakness here.’ Or maybe you missed your 3x5x7 prayer drill for days and your spiritual armor was rusty (discipline failure)—your body’s dropping a note: ‘Your habits matter more than you think.’ The key is to treat every stumble like a detective case. Ask yourself, ‘What was the trigger? What was I feeling? What boundary did I cross?’ Once you find the answer, that’s where you throw your weight into fixing it. If you ignore it, that little crack turns into a landslide. But if you listen, you turn a mistake into a strategy session. Every time you get back up, you’re not just recovering—you’re building a stronger wall for next time. So don’t see stumbles as ‘I failed’—see them as ‘I learned.’ That’s how you stop slides before they even start. Here’s the deal with the rise Protocol—it’s your game plan to stop slides and turn stumbles into strength. First, when you mess up, you don’t wait. You hit the 'Repent' button right away. That means talking to God immediately, not tomorrow or when you feel like it. Just say, ‘I messed up, I’m sorry, and I’m turning back to You.’ It stops the shame spiral before it takes hold. Next, you ‘Investigate’ like a detective. Ask: What triggered this? Was I tired, stressed, or in the wrong place? What lie did I believe? This isn’t about blaming yourself—it’s about learning how the enemy got in so you can lock the door next time. Then, you ‘Speak’ to your accountability partner. Yep, even if it’s awkward. You’ve got to tell them the truth within 24 hours, ideally way sooner. Keeping it secret just fuels the problem, but sharing it kills the shame and gets help. Finally, you ‘Engage’ by fixing the gap. If your phone was in bed, move it. If a certain app is a problem, delete it. Don’t just ‘try harder’—build better habits. The key is speed. The faster you do this, the less damage the stumble does to your brain and your freedom. It’s not about being perfect—it’s about rising so fast the enemy can’t catch you. Think of it like a military response: you don’t wait to treat a wound, you act instantly. Rise isn’t just a plan; it’s your way to turn every failure into a step forward.
