From Lies to Light: Mastering Cognitive Reframing for Freedom

In this episode, we’ll uncover the seven enemy distortions that twist your thinking—like disqualifying good news, jumping to worst-case scenarios, and letting feelings control your reality. You’ll learn how to spot these mental traps, name them in Jesus’ authority, and replace lies with the unshakable truth of Scripture. Walk away with practical steps to reframe your mind, rebuild your identity, and declare victory over the distortions that steal your peace.

In my book Only Faith, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we turn to a battlefront most of us face daily: the war in our minds. Chapter 2 dives into cognitive distortions—those sneaky lies the enemy uses to twist our thinking until we mistake them for truth. Think of them as viruses infecting your inner world. They show up as ‘Yeah, but’ thinking that disqualifies your victories, fortune-telling that predicts disaster, or labeling yourself as a ‘failure’ instead of a ‘child of God.’ These distortions don’t just mess with your head—they attack your identity. When you let them in, you trade the prince’s robe for pauper’s rags. But here’s the good news: you don’t have to play along. In this episode, we’re uncovering how to spot these lies, name them for what they are, and replace them with the unshakable truth of Scripture. We’ll walk through real-life examples like turning ‘I’m a porn addict’ into ‘I am a saint who struggles,’ and swapping fatalistic thoughts like ‘I’ll relapse this weekend’ with the command to ‘occupy the I-am of today.’ This isn’t just mental gymnastics—it’s spiritual warfare. Let me break this down for you. The enemy uses these sneaky mental traps to twist how you see yourself and your progress. First, there’s disqualifying the positive—like when you hear someone say you’re doing great, but you instantly argue back, ‘Yeah, but I’m still a pervert inside’ or ‘This 90-day goal doesn’t count yet.’ You’re basically robbing yourself of joy and progress by acting like God’s grace isn’t enough for *your* story. Then there’s jumping to conclusions: you might assume your spouse is tired of your recovery journey or convince yourself you’ll definitely relapse this weekend. You’re making up a whole future that doesn’t exist, and it just loads you up with stress. Emotional reasoning is when you let your feelings call all the shots—like thinking, ‘I feel broken, so I *must* be broken,’ or ‘I feel distant from God, so He’s gone.’ But feelings are like weather—they change, and they’re often just a reaction to your hunger, sleep, or stress levels. Lastly, labeling: instead of saying, ‘I messed up today,’ you say, ‘I’m a porn addict’ or ‘I’m a lazy Christian.’ You’re turning a mistake into your entire identity, which locks you into a cage. These distortions aren’t just thoughts—they’re attacks on who God says you are. They’re addictive because they keep you stuck in shame and defeat, making you believe the lie that your struggles define you. But here’s the good news: you can spot these tricks and push back. When you catch yourself thinking, ‘I always fail,’ remind yourself, ‘No, I’ve won battles this month.’ When shame says, ‘You’re broken,’ say, ‘The blood of Jesus made me clean.’ It’s like training your brain to see reality through the lens of God’s truth instead of the enemy’s lies. The key? Name the distortion, declare the truth, and keep moving forward. You’re not your mistakes—you’re a child of God with a future full of hope. Here’s the deal: the enemy sends these sneaky ‘scouts’ into your mind—thoughts that feel like truth but are actually lies in disguise. When you catch yourself thinking something like, ‘I always fail,’ don’t just say, ‘I’m thinking this.’ Instead, name it for what it is: ‘The All-or-Nothing virus is attacking me!’ That’s how you strip its power. Think of it like calling out a spy in a soldier’s uniform—once you expose the disguise, the attack falls apart. The key is creating ‘clinical distance’ from the lie. Instead of letting the thought own you, you step back and say, ‘This isn’t my thought; it’s the enemy using the tactic.’ That’s not just mental gymnastics—it’s spiritual warfare. Paul says in 2 Corinthians 10:5 to ‘take every thought captive to obey Christ,’ which sounds intense, but it’s practical. You arrest the lie at the door before it gets a foothold. How? First, label the distortion—like ‘Disqualifying the Positive’ or ‘Emotional Reasoning.’ Then, interrogate it: Does this align with what God says about me? If not, toss it out. The more you practice this, the more your brain rewires to reject lies automatically. It’s like training your mind to say, ‘Nope, that’s not from the Commander,’ and replacing the lie with Scripture. For example, if the enemy whispers, ‘You’re still a porn addict,’ you fire back, ‘I’m a saint who sometimes stumbles, not a sinner trying to be holy.’ The goal isn’t to debate the lie—it’s to shut it down with the truth of who you are in Christ. And here’s the kicker: keep a record of your wins. Write down every time you stood firm, every victory, every moment you felt God’s joy. That’s your ‘evidence locker’ to pull from when the lies come. Naming the attack isn’t just clever—it’s survival. It’s how you turn the enemy’s scouts into your footnotes and walk forward in the freedom He already gave you.
