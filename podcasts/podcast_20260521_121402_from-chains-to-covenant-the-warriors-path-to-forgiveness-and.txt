From Chains to Covenant: The Warrior's Path to Forgiveness and Healing

This episode explores the transformative power of forgiveness as a legal act of freedom and the strategic importance of professional healing in the journey toward sexual wholeness. You'll learn how to release toxic debts, reframe bitterness, and recognize when to enlist a trained counselor as an instrument of divine restoration.

In the book fightlust, Book 3, Shoes of the Gospel of Peace, we turn to Chapter 16 — The Healing Journey. Today, we’re diving into how to actually walk through healing old wounds and letting go of the heavy baggage we carry. This chapter gives us practical steps like scheduling a 'Rest Session' where you meditate on Matthew 11:28 and visualize handing your past pain to God. You picture that weight lifting off your shoulders and trusting the Great Physician to bind up your broken heart. Memorizing Psalm 147:3—'He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds'—becomes your daily reminder that healing is happening right now. But here’s the twist—Chapter 17 gets real about forgiveness. It’s not just about feeling good; it’s a powerful decision to release the debts of the past. The Greek word aphiēmi means canceling a debt, sending it away. Holding onto bitterness is like drinking poison and expecting someone else to die. It fuels addiction and keeps you trapped. So today, we’re unpacking how forgiveness really works, why it’s so hard, and how it sets you free. We’ll walk through the Debt-Release Protocol—listing those debts, tearing up the ledger, and letting go. And we’ll tackle the tough one: forgiving yourself. Because refusing to forgive yourself is a form of spiritual arrogance. Plus, we’ll talk about when it’s time to bring in a professional counselor—not because you’re failing, but because sometimes you need expert help to navigate deep wounds. Four signs might tell you it’s time: when you’re stuck in cycles, need specialized tools, or need objective guidance. This is your guide to healing, freedom, and walking in the fullness of what God has for you. Here’s the thing about forgiveness—most people get confused about what it actually means, and the enemy loves to exploit that confusion. Let me break it down. First, forgiveness isn’t excusing the hurt. Just because you forgive someone doesn’t mean you’re saying, ‘What they did was okay.’ No way. What they did was wrong, plain and simple. Forgiveness isn’t about pretending the pain didn’t happen or letting them off the hook. It’s about you choosing to let go of the anger and the need for revenge. Think of it like this: you’re not erasing the bad thing they did, but you’re refusing to let it control your heart anymore. Second, forgiveness isn’t forgetting. You don’t have to wipe the memory of the hurt from your mind. The wound might still be there, but the sting? That goes away. The memory becomes a scar, not an open sore. You can remember what happened without feeling that same fire of rage or the urge to lash out. Lastly, forgiveness doesn’t always mean reconciliation. Just because you release someone’s debt doesn’t mean you have to let them back into your life. Maybe they’re dangerous, or they don’t even know they hurt you. Forgiveness is your choice to cut the cord so you’re not trapped in bitterness anymore. It’s like closing a chapter in your story so you can keep writing the next page. The key is understanding that forgiveness is a legal move—you’re handing the case over to God and walking free, not letting the past hold you hostage. When you get clear on what forgiveness isn’t, it makes it way easier to actually do it and stop letting anger fuel your struggles. Here’s the thing about bitterness—it’s like a pressure cooker for your soul. When you hold onto unforgiveness, it builds up this internal heat, this constant ache that makes you crave quick fixes. You might not even realize it, but that anger is masquerading as lust or distraction. Your brain’s screaming, ‘I need something to numb this!’ and that’s when the addictive patterns kick in. The key is to dig deeper: ask yourself, ‘Am I really mad, or do I just think I’m tired?’ Because that anger? It’s fueling the cycle. The Debt-Release Protocol isn’t just about forgiving others—it’s about cutting the cord to that toxic energy. When you tear up that mental ledger, you’re not letting someone off the hook; you’re freeing yourself from the weight. It’s like opening a valve to let the steam out. You stop chasing false fixes and start healing the real wound. And here’s the kicker: if you’re still punishing yourself for past mistakes, that’s just another kind of bitterness. You’re saying your standards are higher than God’s. But Christ already paid the price. Let that truth sink in. You don’t have to carry that weight alone—swap the burden for peace, and watch how the pressure lifts. Here’s the thing about self-forgiveness—it’s the hardest debt to let go of, but it’s also the most important. You might tell yourself, ‘I can’t forgive myself for what I’ve done,’ but deep down, you’re actually saying something way bigger than that. You’re saying, ‘My standards are higher than God’s. My shame is more powerful than His grace.’ That’s not humility; that’s spiritual arrogance. You’re acting like Christ’s sacrifice on the cross wasn’t enough for you personally, like your pain and mistakes somehow matter more to the Creator than they do to you. But here’s the truth: when God says you’re clean, you don’t get to argue with Him. You don’t get to keep punishing yourself with shame and guilt like that’s somehow making up for your past. That’s not repentance—it’s self-prosecution. Real forgiveness means accepting what Christ already did for you. It means walking away from the courtroom of your own mind and stepping into the freedom of His Sonship. You’re not letting yourself off easy; you’re choosing to trust the Great Physician’s diagnosis over your own. Your identity isn’t based on what others did to you or what you did to others—it’s based on what He did for you. So when you say, ‘I’m dirty,’ you’re rejecting the blood of Jesus. When you say, ‘I’m whole,’ you’re aligning with His truth. The weight you carry isn’t yours to keep. You can hand it over to the Commander and let Him finish the healing work He started. That’s how you stop bleeding out and start walking in freedom. Hey there, if you're wondering whether you need a professional counselor, let me tell you—this isn’t about lacking faith or being weak. Think of it like needing a specialist for a tricky medical issue. Just as you’d trust a doctor for a deep infection, a trained Christian counselor can help you dig into the messy, hidden parts of your heart where shame, trauma, or addiction have taken root. Here’s the thing: these pros offer four key things you might not get elsewhere. First, they give you a safe, judgment-free space to be totally honest without fear of gossip or shame. Second, they’ve got the training to spot patterns you might miss—like how past wounds keep triggering you or why certain thoughts loop endlessly. Third, they use tools like emdr or cbt to actually rewire your brain, not just give spiritual advice. And fourth, they’re not caught up in your daily drama, so they can see the bigger picture and call out lies you’re buying into. Now, when should you grab a counselor? If you’re stuck in cycles of shame or guilt that won’t let go, if you keep reliving the same trauma over and over, if your accountability group isn’t helping you move forward, or if your struggles feel way too heavy for what you’ve tried so far—those are signs it’s time to bring in backup. Think of it as God equipping you with a shield for the battle, not as a failure. You’re not avoiding the fight; you’re arming yourself smarter. So don’t brush off the idea of professional help. It’s not a last resort—it’s a strategic move to let God work through the right tools for your healing.
