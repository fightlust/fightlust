From Defense to Dominion: Mastering the Breastplate of Righteousness for Lasting Freedom

In this episode, we explore the dynamic journey of recovery through three critical seasons—Honeymoon, Grind, and Deep Restoration—and how to navigate each with Spirit-led vigilance. We’ll uncover the hidden dangers of pride, complacency, and discouragement, and shift from a defensive mindset to an offensive one by embracing the 'Quick Rise' principle. You’ll learn how to distinguish between 'having a victory' and 'being a victor,' and walk away with actionable steps to maintain your armor, renew your mind, and build a legacy of freedom. This is your field manual for transitioning from survival to spiritual dominion.

In my book Only Faith, Book 2, The Breastplate of Righteousness, we turn to Chapter 19 — Going Deeper — where I talk about something I’ve seen time and time again in my own journey and in the lives of others: recovery isn’t a straight line. It’s not just about ‘getting better’ and then coasting. No, the battle changes as you grow. Think about it — when you’re first starting out, everything feels fresh and exciting, right? You’re motivated, you’re making progress, and that’s awesome… but if you’re not careful, pride can creep in and trip you up. Then, a few months in, the excitement fades, and the daily grind sets in. That’s when complacency becomes the real danger. You start skipping your routines, letting your guard down, and suddenly you’re in a tough spot. And by the six-month mark, God starts digging deeper — not to punish you, but to heal you. Old wounds, identity issues, unmet needs — they come up, and you might feel like you’re backtracking. But here’s the truth: you’re not going backward. You’re going deeper. In this episode, we’re breaking down those three seasons of recovery — the Honeymoon, the Grind, and Deep Restoration — and how to navigate them with wisdom. We’ll talk about Proverbs 24:16 and what it means to measure your progress not by perfection, but by how fast you rise when you stumble. Because freedom isn’t just about never falling — it’s about being a victor who keeps getting back up, stronger every time. So whether you’re in the early days or the long haul, this is for you. Let’s dig in. Here’s the truth: your freedom isn’t about never stumbling. It’s about how fast you get back up. Proverbs 24:16 says the righteous fall seven times but rise again. A beginner might spend days—or even weeks—hiding in shame after a misstep, letting guilt paralyze them. But a warrior? They hit the ground, repent in seconds, call their accountability partner within ten minutes, and are back on the front lines before the enemy can cheer. The key is the Quick Rise Drill. First, you lock in who you’ll call immediately—your Legion partner. No overthinking, just a text or a call. Second, you speak truth to God out loud: ‘I’m sorry, Lord. I choose to align with Your truth. Help me fix this.’ You don’t let the enemy linger in your mind; you cut the conversation short. Third, you make this reflex automatic. Practice it like a fire drill. What if you stumbled five minutes from now? Who’s your first call? What words will you say? Rehearse it until it’s second nature. The enemy stops targeting someone who won’t stay down. When you rise fast, you disrupt their rhythm. They waste no more bullets on a moving target. This isn’t about perfection—it’s about momentum. Every time you rise, you prove your armor is functional, your sword is sharp, and your Commander is trustworthy. You’re not just avoiding failure; you’re building a habit of victory. The faster you rise, the more the enemy sees you as a threat they can’t afford to fight. So train your reflexes. Own the rise. Your freedom depends on it. Here’s a big shift that changes everything: if you’re just holding on to a victory, you’re always looking over your shoulder, wondering when you’ll slip up. You might call yourself an ‘addict’ or a ‘work in progress’ because you’re still tied to the old identity. But when you truly become a victor, it’s like your whole life flips. You stop trying to ‘stay clean’ or ‘be good’ and start living from the truth that you’re already free. It’s not about perfection anymore—it’s about who you are in Christ. Think of it like armor: when you first put it on, you have to remember to fasten every strap, but after a while, it sticks to your skin like a second layer. You don’t think about it; you just move in it. That’s what freedom looks like. A victor doesn’t panic when they stumble because they know the battle’s already been won. They don’t ‘try’ to be pure—they act like the new creation they are. The old habits still show up, but they’re just ghosts now. You don’t take them seriously. You might still face challenges, but you’re not ruled by fear. You speak truth over your life like, ‘I am a new creation. My old self is gone. My liberty is permanent.’ That’s not just words; it’s a declaration that rewires your brain and your heart. You start seeing every day as a chance to live out who you are, not as a test to prove you’re ‘good enough.’ The armor’s not a chore—it’s your power suit. And the best part? You’re not just holding on for yourself. You become a walking reminder that freedom is possible for everyone. So if you’re still chasing victory, ask yourself: am I holding on, or have I let go and stepped into who I already am? Freedom isn’t a one-time event—it’s a daily discipline you have to keep up. Think of it like wearing armor every morning: you don’t just assume it’s on; you intentionally strap it on piece by piece, praying through Ephesians 6 and making sure your spiritual gear is tight. Then there’s the 3x5x7 System, which is all about rewiring your brain. You pick a scripture verse, repeat it, visualize it, and let it sink into your biology until it becomes automatic. It’s like building highways of holiness in your mind. And don’t forget Radical Transparency—being totally honest with your accountability partner about the small stumbles, the near misses, the stuff you’d rather hide. That openness isn’t weakness; it’s fuel for your freedom. Every week, you do an armor audit: check which piece feels dull or rusty—maybe your shield of faith needs recharging or your sword of truth needs sharpening. Then you tweak your plan, swap out old habits for new dopamine sources that line up with your values. Long-term, you’re not just surviving; you’re building a lifestyle where victory isn’t a fluke but a rhythm. It’s about turning temporary wins into permanent change by showing up every day, piece by piece, step by step. You’re not fighting to stay free; you’re fighting from the freedom you already have. And that? That’s how you walk into the next season of your life, unshaken and unbroken. Hey there, friend—let’s wrap this up by talking about the four big ideas that’ve shaped your journey: first, renewing your mind. You’ve learned to catch those sneaky thoughts before they take root, right? Like using that three-second window to swap lies for truth, or filtering everything through Philippians 4:8. By repeating godly habits—like the 3x5x7 system or meditating on Scripture—you’re literally paving new highways in your brain. It’s like training your body but for your mind! Third, breaking chains. You’ve faced those deep-rooted issues—maybe pride, shame, or old habits—and used the power of Jesus’ victory to say, ‘No more!’ Renouncing those lies isn’t just a one-time thing; it’s a daily stand. And finally, walking in freedom. You’re not just avoiding the old stuff anymore; you’re living out who God says you are. Your daily armor routine, your honest talks with your accountability partner, and choosing joy over guilt—that’s freedom in action. And where do you still feel ‘stuck’? That’s your next target. Finish strong by declaring out loud, ‘I don’t fight for victory; I fight from victory!’ or ‘My armor’s on, my sword’s sharp, and I’m ready for whatever comes next!’ This isn’t just a plan—it’s your new way of life.
