Breaking the Chains: Mastering the Four Species of Bondage for Spiritual Freedom

In this episode, we uncover the four deadly chains—Entitlement, Escape, Exclusion, and Envy—that keep us trapped in cycles of sin. Through real-life case studies and actionable protocols, we’ll learn how to identify these chains, declare the truth that dismantles them, and enforce our freedom in Jesus’ name. You’ll walk away with a step-by-step Renouncing Protocol to legally evict the enemy from your life.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 2, The Breastplate of Righteousness, we turn to Chapter 12 — a chapter that digs into the hidden traps that keep us stuck in cycles of struggle. Today, we’re talking about four specific ‘species’ of chains that bind us: Escape, Entitlement, Exclusion, and Envy. These aren’t just abstract ideas — they’re real, sneaky forces that twist our thinking and keep us from God’s freedom. Take John, for example. He’s drowning in financial stress and marital conflict. When the pressure gets too much, he grabs his phone not for a sexual high, but to numb the pain of his life. That’s the chain of Escape — using sin as an anesthetic. Then there’s David, who hides a secret fetish, convinced no one could ever understand him. That’s Exclusion — the lie that you’re uniquely broken and must stay in the shadows. And Tom, scrolling through social media, comparing his messy life to someone else’s highlight reel, then turning to porn to ‘even the score’ — that’s Envy, the poison of thinking you’re owed something better. But here’s the good news: God has already defeated these chains at the Cross. The key is learning to name them, renounce them out loud, and replace them with His truth. In this chapter, we walk through a step-by-step protocol to legally evict these lies from your life. No more hiding. No more numbness. Just freedom — real, lasting, and bought with a price. Let’s break these chains together. Hey, let me tell you about John. He’s been stuck in this cycle where life feels like a never-ending storm—money worries, fights with his wife, and this gnawing sense that no one gets him. When the pressure hits, he grabs his phone not just for, you know, the usual stuff, but to drown out the noise of his problems. It’s not really about the content at first; it’s about the mental ‘white noise’ that numbs the pain. But here’s the catch: that escape? It’s a trapdoor. It doesn’t fix anything—it just leaves him more drained, like he’s running on a treadmill backward. The truth is, God isn’t some distant helper. Psalm 46:1 says He’s our *refuge*—a safe place, right here, right now. John’s chasing a fake peace, but God offers real rest. When you run from your reality, you’re just avoiding the fight. But when you run into God’s presence, you’re choosing a peace that actually *restores* you. It’s like swapping a temporary bandage for a permanent cure. The enemy wants you to believe you need escape, but the truth is, you need Presence. So next time the pressure builds, ask yourself: am I reaching for a screen, or am I stepping into the arms of the One who already holds the storm? Hey, I want to talk about David for a second. He’s got this thing where he’s convinced his specific struggle is so weird, so dark, that no one else could possibly get it. He’s lived in this cage of secrecy for years, thinking if anyone found out about his specific thoughts, they’d run away in disgust. You know how that feels, right? Like you’re the only one with those ‘twisted’ thoughts, and if you share them, you’ll lose everything. But here’s the thing: David’s not a freak. He’s a soldier with a wound that needs a bandage, not a label. The enemy uses that ‘terminal uniqueness’ lie to keep him isolated, because darkness can’t survive when you step into the light. 1 Corinthians 10:13 says God won’t let you be tempted beyond what you can handle, and He always gives a way out. That ‘way out’ isn’t just a generic escape hatch—it’s the truth that your struggle isn’t unique. Every brother in Christ has a ‘secret’ they’ve never shared. The problem isn’t the struggle itself; it’s the silence. When David finally talks to someone he trusts, the shame starts to crumble. Vulnerability isn’t weakness—it’s the disinfectant that kills the infection of isolation. The longer he hides, the more power the enemy has over him. But when he says, ‘Here I am, broken, but I’m still your brother,’ that’s when the chains start to break. You don’t have to carry that weight alone. Your struggle is common to mankind, and the Cross covers it. So stop acting like you’re ‘damaged goods’—you’re a masterpiece in progress, and the Legion’s got your back. So here’s how you actually break free from these chains. First, you repent by taking real responsibility for the lies you’ve believed. Like, you don’t just feel bad—you say out loud, ‘I messed up by thinking I needed this sin or that I deserved it.’ That’s not about shame; it’s about clearing the way for God’s power to move in. Then comes the big one: renounce. You stand up, speak with authority, and say, ‘I cancel the deal I made with ! You’re out of here!’ Why? Because the enemy only has power when you let him. And finally, receive—fill that empty space with God’s truth. You invite the Holy Spirit in, declare you’re whole in Christ, and make sure darkness doesn’t move back in. And here’s the thing: you gotta say all this out loud. Your voice isn’t just a sword—it’s how your brain rewires, how the spiritual realm knows you’re serious, and how you stop second-guessing yourself. Think of it like a court case: you repent to admit fault, renounce to serve the eviction notice, and receive to claim your new identity. No shortcuts. No whispering. Just bold, loud, clear declarations that line up with the truth that Jesus already won. It’s not about trying harder; it’s about enforcing the victory He already gave you. Here's how you can tackle these chains in your life. First, map out your triggers: Entitlement (I deserve this), Escape (I need to check out), Exclusion (I’m uniquely broken), or Envy (Why them?). When a pull hits, name the species out loud—like, ‘I see you, Escape!’—then declare war on the lie. For example, if you’re fleeing stress into your phone, renounce the agreement out loud: ‘I reject this! My refuge is the living God.’ But don’t stop there. Fill the gap with truth—invite the Holy Spirit to flood that space with peace, joy, and God’s presence. This isn’t just about feeling better; it’s a legal eviction. Christ already nailed your debt to the cross, disarmed the enemy, and claimed victory. You’re just serving the final notice. Practice this daily: write your Chain-Map, label triggers under each species, and drill the vocal steps until they’re automatic. When you stumble, don’t spiral into shame—use the ‘10-minute rise’ to repent, renounce, and reoccupy. Remember, freedom isn’t about being perfect; it’s about anchoring your identity in the King who’s already won. You’re not fighting alone. The Legion’s got your back, and the Light is waiting to dissolve every chain.
