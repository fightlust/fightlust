From Contracts to Covenants: Building Sacred Foundations of Freedom

This episode explores the transformative power of covenant relationships in spiritual restoration, contrasting the anxiety of 'contracts' with the safety of divine promises. We’ll unpack how God’s covenant with Abraham models His unwavering commitment to you, expose the hidden costs of self-dealing 'contracts' with sin, and reveal how covenantal thinking redefines marriage, recovery, and sexuality as acts of worship. Practical steps include auditing your relationships, drafting a personal restoration covenant, and reclaiming your body as a temple. Discover how shifting from performance-based 'consumers' to promise-keeping 'covenanters' creates unshakeable freedom.

================================================================

In my book fightlust, Book 1, The Belt of Truth, we turn to Chapter 13 — *Covenant Vs. Contract* — where I unpack a truth that could change how you see your relationships, your recovery, and even your walk with God. Picture this: In Genesis 15, God made a covenant with Abraham, but here’s the kicker — He walked through the sacrificed animals alone. That meant He was taking the penalty if the covenant failed. That’s not just a cool story; it’s a blueprint for how God sees your restoration. He’s already paid the price. Your freedom isn’t a contract hinging on your performance; it’s a covenant rooted in His sacrifice. Now, let’s get real. If you’ve ever battled addiction, you’ve probably made a ‘contract with yourself’ — like, *‘Just one look, and I’ll feel better.’* But here’s the trap: That hit never lasts, and the shame it leaves behind always costs more than you bargained for. This chapter challenges you to swap that cycle for something radical — a covenant mindset. It’s about trading the anxiety of ‘if I mess up, I’m doomed’ with the peace of ‘no matter what, the King has my back.’ We’ll dig into how marriage, friendships, and even your recovery should be covenants — not transactions. You’ll learn why trying to ‘earn’ love or purity keeps you stuck in a loop of stress and shame, and how covenant creates a safe space to heal, be honest, and finally break free from the enemy’s lies. And if you’ve ever felt like your past disqualifies you, I’ll show you how God’s not just forgiving your mistakes — He’s rewriting your story. This isn’t just theology; it’s a call to action. We’re talking about drafting your own *Restoration Covenant*, tearing up contracts with sin, and choosing to live as someone who’s not just ‘not broken’ but *renewed*. Stick around — this is where freedom starts. Here's the deal: when you're stuck in sexual addiction, you're really just signing a lousy contract with yourself. You tell your old nature, 'Hit me with a quick dopamine fix, and I'll ignore the mess it leaves behind.' But here's the catch—sin always lies. That ten-second high? It fades fast, and the cost? It's way higher than you bargained for. Every time you give in, you're trading a piece of your future integrity for a temporary escape. It's like borrowing against your soul with no plan to pay it back. The shame that follows isn't just a side effect; it's the fine print you didn’t read. The problem is, this cycle convinces you that your worth is tied to your performance, and that’s a recipe for constant anxiety. But here’s the good news: God offers something better—a covenant. It’s not based on your behavior today but on His promise yesterday. When you walk through life under a covenant, you’re not trying to earn safety; you’re living in it. You can be honest about your struggles without fear because the deal isn’t about keeping up your end—it’s about trusting His. So how do you break the contract? Start by auditing the hidden agreements you’ve made with your sin. Ask yourself: What false promises am I believing? What am I trading my peace for? Then, tear up that contract and declare freedom. Replace the lie with truth—like Malachi 2:14, which reminds us that marriage is a sacred covenant, not a transaction. Your body, your choices, your future? They’re not bargaining chips. They’re part of a bigger story where you’re already loved, already free, and already enough. The cost of staying in that self-dealing contract? It’s a life of shadows. The cost of choosing covenant? It’s the eternal weight of glory. And honestly? That’s a trade I’ll take every time. Marriage isn’t a contract where you trade loyalty for validation. The world sells it as a transaction—‘if you don’t meet my needs, I’ll find someone who does’—but Scripture calls it a covenant. A covenant isn’t based on what your spouse does today; it’s rooted in the promise you made before God. It’s saying, ‘I choose to honor and stay faithful even when it’s hard.’ That’s what Malachi 2:14 talks about—God as the witness to your vows. When you live in covenant, you stop treating your marriage like a store where you’re always shopping for a better deal. You become a steward, not a customer. This creates safety for healing. If you’re always waiting for your spouse to ‘perform,’ you’ll live in anxiety, like you’re one mistake away from abandonment. But a covenant says, ‘My loyalty isn’t based on feelings or performance—it’s based on a promise.’ This applies to every relationship, even your bond with God. If your recovery feels like a contract—‘I’ll stay pure if You fix my life’—you’ll always be spiritually exhausted. But a covenant mindset shifts you from ‘What do I get?’ to ‘What do I keep?’ You’re not using people to fill your needs; you’re serving them because you’re loyal to something bigger. Covenant doesn’t mean ignoring problems—it means facing them together, knowing your commitment isn’t conditional. It’s the difference between a fragile agreement and a fortress of trust. When you live in covenant, you stop chasing validation and start building a legacy of faithfulness. That’s where real intimacy grows, not from using bodies, but from honoring souls. And that’s how you break the cycle of performance-based anxiety and step into the freedom of a promise-keeping life. Changing how you relate to others—and to God—starts with daily choices that swap the contract mindset for covenant living. It’s about asking, ‘How can I serve my King and my people today?’ instead of ‘How can I feel good right now?’ That shift isn’t just a mindset; it’s a practice. For example, if you’ve been using someone for validation—like a friend who always cheers you up—start serving them instead. Maybe listen to their struggles without trying to fix them, or do something kind just because. It’s awkward at first, but it trains your heart to value relationships over quick fixes. Then there’s honesty. If you’re in a recovery group, share something raw you’re afraid to say—like a relapse or a hidden struggle. Trust that your covenant partners won’t abandon you; they’ll walk through the mess with you. And don’t skip the scriptures. Memorizing verses like Malachi 2:14—‘The Lord has been witness between you and the wife of your youth…’—reminds you that God honors promises, even when you mess up. This isn’t about being perfect; it’s about building a life where your worth isn’t tied to your performance. Every day, you’re signing a new ‘restoration covenant’ with yourself and your community, tearing up old deals with sin, and choosing freedom that outlasts every failure. It’s slow, messy work, but over time, you’ll stop chasing temporary highs and start living in the steady, unshakable peace of a promise. That’s how freedom becomes a lifestyle—not by white-knuckling through rules, but by falling in love with the One who already wrote your story. Here's the thing: going deeper into freedom isn't just about following rules—it's about falling in love with the One who made you. Think of it like this: when you're in a relationship with someone you truly admire, you don't need a list of dos and don'ts to stay loyal. You just want to honor them because they mean that much to you. That's what God wants with you. For years, maybe you've let your eyes and hands serve lies, but it's time to reclaim them. Use your eyes to see people as God does—full of worth, not just objects. Use your hands to build, to serve, to bless. And when shame from the past tries to creep in, remember this: the blood of Jesus didn't just cover your mistakes—it healed them. You're not damaged goods; you're a work in progress, and the Holy Spirit is turning your scars into stories of redemption. The final step? Let your whole life become worship. Imagine your sexuality as an instrument in a symphony. For too long, it might have played off-key, but now it's time to tune it to God's design. When you align your desires with His heart, purity stops being a chore and becomes the air you breathe. You're not just avoiding sin—you're radiating freedom. You're not managing a problem—you're living a miracle. And the best part? This isn't about being perfect; it's about being real, right here, right now, with a God who’s already written your story of victory.
