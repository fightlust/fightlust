Let’s talk about redesigning your space to protect your heart. The first line of defense is managing your physical environment because most temptation happens when you’re alone and tired. Start by keeping all screens in public areas—laptops and tablets stay in the living room, kitchen, or dining room, never behind closed doors. Why? Visibility is the enemy of addiction. When you know someone might walk in at any second, those three-second windows of weakness become harder to act on. If you need privacy for work, do it in a library or a common space where others can see. Next, make your bedroom a sanctuary for rest and marriage, not a trap. Phones belong in the kitchen at night, not in the bed. Most relapses happen when you’re tired and alone, so a physical alarm clock (which costs ten bucks) is way better than using your phone for sleep tracking. Finally, charge everything in a central spot overnight—like the kitchen counter. This sends a message: I’m a man of integrity with nothing to hide. It also stops the midnight scroll that turns into a two-hour binge. If your phone isn’t in reach, your brain gets a fighting chance to say no. These changes aren’t about being extreme; they’re about creating holy spaces where temptation can’t hide. Your willpower isn’t the solution—it’s the design of your world. Build a life where the path of least resistance leads toward light, not darkness. Let’s talk about building real protection for your digital life. Level 2 is all about using software to lock down your habits. Get accountability software like Covenant Eyes—it sends a report of your internet activity to your partner or a trusted friend. Knowing someone will see where you’ve been keeps you honest. Then set up DNS filtering on your router, like CleanBrowsing or OpenDNS. That blocks bad stuff at the source, so every device in your house—your phone, your kid’s tablet, even the smart tv—is automatically guarded. Don’t trust the default settings on YouTube or Netflix? Turn on SafeSearch and Restricted Mode everywhere. They act like a filter, catching the grossest stuff before it hits your screen. For your phone, use Screen Time features to block high-risk apps during your weakest hours. Let your accountability partner set the passcode, not you—if you’re the one who can unlock it, you’ll find an excuse to cheat. Now for Level 3: your social media. Be brutal about unfollowing anyone who even flickers with temptation. A fitness influencer, an old friend, a news account—if it makes you pause or scroll longer, cut it loose. Your purity is worth more than likes or staying “in the loop.” And if someone asks why you’re gone, just say you’re simplifying your feed. Finally, practice the ‘pluck out the eye’ strategy. If a platform or account is a trap for you, delete it. Don’t argue with yourself—get rid of it. You’re not fighting this with willpower alone. You’re designing a world where the enemy can’t reach you. That’s how you win.
