From Testimony to Leadership: The Warrior's Path to Freedom and Multiplication

This episode explores the 3-3-3 Testimony framework for sharing your story of freedom and transitions into the call to lead others out of spiritual bondage. Learn how to craft a focused, Kingdom-impact testimony, avoid common pitfalls, and step into leadership by building a team and guarding against the 'hero-trap.' Discover the paradox of keeping freedom by giving it away and the power of intercession in spiritual leadership.

================================================================

In the book fightlust, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we turn to Chapter 12 — The 3-3-3 Testimony — where we learn how to turn your story into a high-performance tool for freedom. Here’s the deal: sharing your journey isn’t about making yourself the hero or diving into gritty details. It’s about pointing others to Jesus, the real Savior. The 3-3-3 framework gives you a clear structure — three minutes total — to share your past struggles, God’s rescue, and your present freedom. Think of it like this: first, the ‘mess’ — a 60-second snapshot of the cage you were in, focusing on the cost of sin, not the sin itself. Then, the ‘miracle’ — how God stepped in, maybe through community or Scripture, to rewrite your story. Finally, the ‘message’ — your life now, walking in light with hope for others. But it doesn’t stop there. You also pass on three key lessons: fighting from victory, leaning on community, and healing root wounds. And here’s the kicker — you set boundaries. No sin-porn, no trauma-dumping, no blaming others. Why? Because your story isn’t about you — it’s about the King. Oh, and what if you only have 60 seconds? The ‘blitz’ version packs the whole thing into a hallway conversation: trapped, rescued, free. But here’s the bigger picture: leading others out of darkness isn’t just noble — it’s how you keep your own freedom fresh. Think of it like the Sea of Galileo, flowing with life, versus the Dead Sea, stagnant and lifeless. When you pour into others, you stay alive in the Spirit. Today, we’re unpacking how to craft your testimony, avoid the hero trap, and become a leader who multiplies freedom — not just for yourself, but for the whole squad. Let’s get into it. Here’s the thing about those three lessons I learned—they’re the real game-changers. First, identity. I used to think I had to earn my freedom every day, but now I know better. It’s not about trying harder or feeling stronger. It’s about remembering who you already are in Christ. Even when your emotions say otherwise, you walk like a winner because God says you’re a new creation. That shifts everything. You stop fighting from a place of ‘I hope I’m okay’ and start fighting from ‘I already am.’ It’s like having a built-in cheat code for victory. Second, community. Trying to do this alone? That’s a trap. The enemy thrives in isolation. But when you’re part of a group—like a Legion squad—you’re not just sharing your mess; you’re holding each other accountable. It’s not about being perfect. It’s about being real. When you open up, others do too, and that’s how you all stay clean. You become a team that’s got each other’s backs. And third, roots. My struggles weren’t just about bad habits—they were symptoms of deeper wounds. Loneliness, fear, brokenness. The only real fix is letting God heal those parts. It’s not just about stopping the bad stuff; it’s about filling the emptiness with His love. The enemy tried to use my past against me, but now I see it as part of my story that God is rewriting. These lessons don’t just help me—they’re tools I can pass on. They turn my story into a roadmap for others. Instead of just telling what happened, I’m showing them how to win. That’s the real deal. Here's a radical truth in the Kingdom: the more you give your freedom away, the more you keep it. It sounds backward, but think about the Sea of Galilee versus the Dead Sea. The Galilee has water flowing in and out, so it’s full of life. The Dead Sea only takes in water, no outflow, and it’s just... dead. Same with our spiritual lives. If you only receive the Word, rest, and support but never pour them out to others, your heart becomes a swamp—stagnant and self-absorbed. That’s the 'hoarder of grace' trap. You think keeping it all for yourself is safe, but it’s actually dangerous. When you teach someone the reject method, you’re not just helping them; you’re reinforcing that tool in your own mind. When you encourage a friend to 'bounce' their eyes, you’re sharpening your own reflexes. It’s like learning by teaching. Leadership isn’t about ego; it’s about stewardship. You stop trying to stay clean for yourself and start staying clean because you won’t let your team down. That’s a stronger motivation. But watch out—if you start believing you’re the hero saving people, you’re in the 'Messiah Complex.' That’s a trap. Your job is to point others to Jesus, not make them follow you. Keep the focus on Him, or you’ll end up leading them into a new cage. So, give it away. Lead others, and you’ll find your own freedom deepening, not shrinking. It’s the paradox that works. So here's how you start building your team. First, look around at the people you already know—your coworkers, friends, maybe even family. Find someone who’s stuck in their struggles, feeling alone, and ready for change. They might not know how to get out of the mess they’re in, but they’re tired of staying there. Once you spot that person, use your 60-second story to connect. Keep it simple: tell them you’ve been where they are, the King set you free, and you want to walk with them. Don’t overcomplicate it. Then, invite them into a group—maybe a small team or a weekly meeting. Share the tools you’ve learned, like setting boundaries online or using the reject method. Walk them through the basics, step by step. But here’s the key: don’t let them think you’re the one saving them. Your job is to point them to Jesus, not to yourself. If you start acting like the hero, you’ll burn out fast and lead them into a new trap. Stay humble. Also, set strong boundaries as a leader. Never mentor someone alone—have your own team watching your back. And if you’re mentoring someone of the opposite sex, that’s a big no. Protect your relationships and theirs. Finally, pray for them before you even talk to them. Ask God to heal their heart, strengthen their resolve, and protect their family. Leadership isn’t about being the center of attention—it’s about being a guide who points others to the Commander. Keep that focus, and you’ll build a team that’s not just surviving but thriving in freedom. Here's the thing about leading others: you've got to guard your leadership like it's the last safe spot in a war zone. First rule—never walk into someone's mess alone. If you're mentoring without your own team watching your back, you're not a leader, you're a target. Imagine trying to guide someone without your own squad backing you up—it’s like holding a flashlight in a storm without a raincoat. Second, keep your gender boundaries tight. When you’re helping someone heal, especially someone of the opposite sex, emotions run deep. One wrong step and you’re not just risking your marriage, you’re crashing theirs too. It’s not about distrust—it’s about refusing to let the enemy plant seeds where he doesn’t belong. Third, don’t let people lean on you like you’re their crutch. Your job isn’t to carry them; it’s to point them to the Commander. If they can’t make a move without your okay, you’ve accidentally built a new cage around them. Now, here’s the secret sauce no one mentions: before you ever talk to your team, you pray for them. Hard. Pray for their hearts to heal, for their weak spots to get strong, for their families to stay protected. This isn’t just nice—it’s the real work. You’re not just leading with words; you’re fighting for them in the invisible war while they sleep. That’s where the power is. That’s how you keep the focus on Jesus, not on you. Because if you don’t guard your own ground first, you’re just setting yourself—and your squad—up to fail. And no one needs another broken leader adding to the mess.
