From Isolation to Abiding: Mastering the Shield of Faith for Lasting Freedom

Discover how to transform your spiritual warfare strategy by leveraging community accountability, aligning your obedience with faith, and cultivating a deep awe of God. This episode breaks down the legal foundations of protection, the Holy Spirit's role in strengthening your shield, and the radical rest that comes from dwelling in God's victory. Learn practical steps to move from reactive defense to abiding in the shelter of the Most High.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to Chapter 9—Going Deeper—where we unpack what it really means to live under the protection of God’s shield. You see, the Shield of Faith isn’t just a tool we hold; it’s a way of life that requires humility, community, and a daily choice to trust God even when it’s hard. I talk about how accountability partners aren’t just optional—they’re essential. When your faith is shaky, they’re the ones who cover you with their prayers and remind you of truth when you’re too tired to shout it yourself. But here’s the hard part: your shield only works if you’re living in obedience. If you’re hiding secrets, avoiding forgiveness, or flirting with gray areas, you’re creating holes in your armor. The enemy doesn’t need to attack from the outside; he can walk right through the breaches you’ve made. That’s why faith and obedience are two sides of the same coin. I also break down how the Holy Spirit isn’t just a helper—he’s the power behind your shield. He’s the one who reminds you of Scripture in the heat of battle and gives you strength when your arms get weary. And the biggest shift I talk about? Moving your focus from fearing the enemy to being in awe of God’s power. When you start seeing Jesus as the unstoppable King and the enemy as a defeated dog on a short leash, those fiery darts suddenly look like spit wads. This chapter isn’t just about survival—it’s about moving from a life of constant reaction to a place of deep, unshakable rest. Let me take you there. Here’s the deal: your spiritual shield doesn’t work if you’re living in rebellion. Think of it like this—if you’re intentionally flirting with gray areas, holding onto secret sins, or refusing to forgive someone who hurt you, you’re basically cutting a hole in your own armor. The enemy doesn’t need to attack from the outside; he can just walk right through the hole you made. That’s legal ground, like signing a contract that gives him permission to strike. Faith and obedience go hand in hand. You can’t claim to trust God if you’re ignoring what He says in specific areas of your life. Obedience isn’t about being perfect or trying harder; it’s about aligning your choices with God’s truth, even when it’s uncomfortable. For example, if you’re stuck holding a grudge, that’s not just a ‘personal issue’—it’s a loophole. The shield weakens because you’re tangled up with the enemy’s kingdom. But when you choose to walk in integrity—forgiving that person, stepping away from temptation, or repenting of hidden stuff—you remove those entanglements. Suddenly, your shield isn’t just yours anymore; it’s backed by the King Himself. The enemy sees you as a representative of Jesus, not a target. That’s why obedience hardens your defense. It’s not about earning points; it’s about clearing the table so there’s no legal reason for the enemy to attack. Your shield becomes unbreakable not because you’re strong, but because you’re no longer mixed up with his kingdom. You’re on His side, and that changes everything. You know how Roman soldiers kept their shields moist to make them stronger? The Holy Spirit is like that moisture for your Shield of Faith. He’s the one who keeps your faith alive and strong, especially when you’re in the middle of a battle. When a fiery dart hits, it’s the Spirit who brings the right Scripture to your mind—like a reminder of God’s promises at exactly the moment you need it. And when your arms are getting tired, holding that shield up feels impossible, the Spirit gives you the strength to keep going. He’s not just some background helper; He’s actively quenching those attacks with His power. The key here is to stop relying on your own willpower and start leaning into Him. When your shield feels heavy, pause and listen for His whisper. He’ll guide you to the exact verse that dismantles the enemy’s attack, like a sniper shot to the heart of the problem. It’s not about gritting your teeth and fighting harder—it’s about partnering with the Spirit, letting Him do the work while you trust Him. Think of it like driving a car: you steer, but the engine does the heavy lifting. If you try to power the shield on your own, you’ll burn out fast. But when you’re connected to the Spirit, you’re tapping into an endless source of energy. He reminds you that this isn’t a solo mission. You’re not just holding up a piece of equipment; you’re carrying the presence of God Himself. So when the darts come flying, you’re not asking, ‘Can I handle this?’ You’re saying, ‘I’ve got the Spirit with me—let’s see what He does.’ That’s how you go deeper: not by forcing yourself to be stronger, but by surrendering to the One who already is. It changes everything. Here's the thing about addiction—it tricks you into thinking the sin is the big bad wolf. You start to fear the high, the lure, the momentary power of the dopamine hit more than you fear God. But freedom? It starts when you flip that script. You stop being in awe of the dart and start being in awe of the Commander who holds the shield. How do you do that? You spend time with Jesus. Not just reading about Him, but meditating on His power. You look at creation and realize the same God who made the stars is the one who died on the Cross for you. You remember that the enemy? He’s a defeated foe. His arrows are spit-wads compared to the Sun of Righteousness who walks in your soul. When you start seeing God as this massive, unstoppable force—this King who already won the war—the enemy’s attacks lose their sting. It’s not just a mental trick. It’s a total shift. Your shield stops being about trying to block arrows and becomes about worshiping the One who already protected you. You’re not just protecting your mind anymore; you’re living in the awe of the God who made you. That’s what turns a shield into a shield of faith. And that’s what makes the fiery darts feel like nothing more than raindrops in a storm you’re already safe from. True freedom isn’t about being on edge all the time—it’s about resting in the victory Jesus already won. Reactive believers are always stressed, always looking over their shoulder, always waiting for the next problem to hit. But deep warriors? They’re at peace. Why? Because they know their Commander already took care of the battle. They’re not trying to ‘win’ every fight; they’re living in the win that’s already theirs. This rest isn’t about sitting back and doing nothing—it’s about standing strong on the wall while your heart stays calm. Your shield feels lighter because you’re not fighting for a victory anymore; you’re living in the one Jesus bought with His blood. It’s like moving from a survival mindset to a ‘I’m safe because my King already won’ mindset. This kind of rest doesn’t just save your energy—it multiplies your impact. When you’re peaceful inside, you can face attacks with a smile, knowing no weapon against you will work. You’re not just hiding in a secret place; you’re living there, day in and day out. That’s where spiritual stamina comes from—not from white-knuckling through every day, but from trusting the One who holds your shield when your arm gets tired. The more you rest in His victory, the more you’re ready to fight, because you’re not running on your strength anymore. You’re powered by the One who already won.
