From Stumble to Rise: Mastering the rise Protocol for Spiritual Resilience

This episode unpacks the critical difference between a 'stumble' and a 'slide' in spiritual warfare, showing how immediate action after a setback determines long-term victory. You'll learn the rise Protocol—Repent, Investigate, Speak, Engage—to transform moments of weakness into strategic growth. Discover why speed of recovery matters more than perfection, how to audit your patterns, and why your Legion (accountability partners) is essential for breaking cycles of shame. Walk away with practical tools to turn failures into fuel for resilience.

================================================================

In my book fightlust, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to Chapter 21 — The Reality of the Skirmish — where I explain a truth that could change everything for you: when you trip, it’s not the end of the battle, it’s a signal. You see, if you see setbacks as failures, you’ll quit. But if you see them as clues, you’ll win. Every stumble is a diagnostic event — a moment where your defenses dipped, your wall got low, or your sword went dull. The question isn’t whether you’ll stumble; it’s what you do *next*. And here’s the hard part: the real danger isn’t the stumble itself, it’s the slide that follows. A stumble is a five-minute choice. A slide is a season of surrender. It’s when you tell yourself, ‘I’ve already blown it, why not enjoy the sin for a few days?’ That’s the enemy’s trap — the moment you agree with his lies, the old highways of addiction start repaving. But here’s the good news: you can stop it. You can rise faster than the slide can take hold. In this chapter, I break down the difference between a stumble and a slide, why the speed of your recovery matters more than the size of the fall, and how to use every setback as a chance to patch your walls and sharpen your sword. We’re talking about the rise protocol — Repent, Investigate, Speak, Engage — your first-aid kit for the soul. And I’m not just telling you to ‘get back up’ — I’m showing you how to rise like a warrior, not a victim. Because when you stop fearing the stumble and start treating it as intelligence data, you’ll never be held hostage by a slide again. Here’s the thing about repentance—it’s not about beating yourself up until you feel ‘bad enough’ to get forgiven. It’s a spiritual stop-loss, plain and simple. When you mess up, the enemy’s voice immediately starts whispering, ‘You’re already ruined. Might as well stay in the mud.’ But that’s the exact moment you need to run back to God, not hide. You don’t wait until you ‘feel spiritual’ again or until your shame wears off. You don’t wait for the right mood or the perfect time. You drop to your knees right there, in the mess, and say, ‘I messed up. I’m sorry. I need You.’ The goal is to kill the shame-spiral before it takes root. If you delay, the guilt hardens like concrete around your feet, and suddenly you’re not just dealing with one stumble—you’re in a full-blown slide. But if you hit the ‘repent’ button within minutes, you short-circuit the enemy’s plan. You’re not pretending you’re perfect; you’re declaring, ‘I’m not staying here.’ It’s like putting a bandage on a cut before it bleeds out. The devil wants you to wait, to ruminate, to let the sin fester. But the Commander’s hand is already out, ready to pull you up. So you grab it fast. You don’t let the ‘Square One’ lie win. You don’t let the old patterns rebuild. You just confess, move, and get back on the wall. That’s how you turn a stumble into a setup for victory—not by pretending you’re flawless, but by refusing to let the failure define you. The first step isn’t about earning grace—it’s about activating it. And grace is always faster than shame. So after you repent, the next step is to dig into why the stumble happened. This is the 'Investigate' part of rise, and it’s like being your own detective. You look back and ask, 'What exactly broke down here?' Was it an environmental failure? Like, were you in a situation that just screamed for temptation—maybe scrolling on your phone late at night or hanging out in a place that’s a red flag? Or was it a discipline issue? Did you skip your daily prayer or scripture routine, letting your spiritual armor get rusty? Or maybe it’s a deeper root problem—like holding onto some unprocessed anger, guilt, or hurt that you haven’t dealt with? For example, if you stumbled because you were bitter about a work conflict, that’s not just a 'mistake'—it’s a signal that your heart needs healing. The point here isn’t to beat yourself up but to treat the stumble like a GPS alert. It’s telling you, 'Hey, this part of your life is weak—go fix it!' If you ignore it, the same spot will crumble again. But if you patch it up—like adding a new boundary, rebuilding your morning routine, or finally talking through that old wound—you turn a failure into a setup for future wins. Think of it this way: every stumble is a free diagnostic test from your soul. The question is, are you going to read the report and take action, or just shrug and hope it doesn’t happen again? The goal here is to get specific. Name the cause, own the pattern, and then build a plan to fix the leak before it floods the whole house. That’s how you turn a 'setback' into a 'setup for comebacks.' Here’s the thing about the third step in rise—Speaking Truth—it’s your brain’s reset button. After you repent and figure out what went wrong, you gotta hit pause on the shame spiral. That’s where declarations come in. You don’t let the Accuser define you as a ‘failure’ or a ‘loser’; you flip the script by saying, ‘I am a son, a warrior, a child of God.’ Out loud. Right then. It’s not about pretending you didn’t mess up—it’s about anchoring yourself in the truth that *you’re still His*. The enemy thrives when you let guilt rewrite your identity, but when you declare who you are in Christ, you short-circuit his lies. Think of it like rewiring a broken circuit: every time you say, ‘I’m not stuck in this,’ or ‘My victory is already mine,’ you’re training your brain to default to hope instead of despair. This isn’t just positive thinking—it’s spiritual combat. You’re taking the weapon the enemy tried to use against you (your shame) and turning it into a shield. The faster you speak truth over the mess, the harder it is for the slide to gain momentum. It’s like putting a firewall up before the virus spreads. You’re not just surviving the stumble—you’re using it to level up. The more you practice this, the more your brain learns to trust God’s truth over the Accuser’s static. And that’s how you turn a ‘moment of weakness’ into a ‘moment of power.’ Here’s the deal: when you’re stuck in a slide, you can’t just tough it out on your own. The shame and guilt are like quicksand—the harder you try to climb out alone, the deeper you sink. That’s why step four of the rise protocol is about calling in your team. Your Legion, your accountability partners, they’re not just people you check in with once a week. They’re your rescue squad. The second you realize you’re in a slide, you send out an S.O.S. You text your partner, say, ‘I’m stuck. I need you.’ And you don’t wait until you ‘feel ready’ or ‘clean enough’ to ask for help. That’s the enemy’s trick to make you think you’re too broken to bother anyone. But here’s the truth: your Legion exists to pull you out when you’re deep in the mud. They’ll show up with prayer, with truth, with practical steps to get you back on your feet. You might be tempted to hide, to say, ‘I’ll handle this myself,’ but that’s exactly what the enemy wants. He knows you can’t beat a slide alone. It’s like trying to put out a house fire with a water gun. Your team brings the firehose. They’ll help you confess the lies you’re believing, reignite your morning routine, and maybe even sit with you until you’re steady again. And this isn’t about shame—it’s about life. When you call them, you’re not saying, ‘I failed,’ you’re saying, ‘I need my brothers.’ The longer you wait, the harder it gets. But when you engage your Legion fast, you’re giving them a chance to stop the slide before it turns into a full-blown disaster. That’s how you win. Not by pretending you’re stronger than you are, but by leaning into the power of the body of Christ. You’re not a lone soldier in this fight—you’re part of an army. And armies don’t leave their wounded behind.
