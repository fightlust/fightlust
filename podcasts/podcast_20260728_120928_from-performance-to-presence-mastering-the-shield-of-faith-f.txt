From Performance to Presence: Mastering the Shield of Faith for Spiritual Rest

This episode explores how to shift from anxiety-driven striving to resting in God’s presence through spiritual disciplines. You’ll learn how Spirit-led awareness differs from modern mindfulness, how to repent of the 'rush' mindset, reclaim silence as a weapon, and use rest as a platform for intimacy with God and spiritual warfare. Practical steps include creating a 'Rest Protocol' to anchor your life in the finished work of Christ.

================================================================

In my book fightlust, Book 4, The Shield of Faith, we turn to Chapter 19 — *Going Deeper* — where we confront a question that’s quietly sabotaging our spiritual lives: What happens when ‘rest’ becomes just another checklist item? You’ve probably heard the buzz around mindfulness — apps, breathing exercises, the whole ‘be present’ thing. But here’s the truth: Those practices miss the point. They’re like trying to fix a leaky boat by patching the hull but ignoring the storm. In this chapter, I’m taking you beyond surface-level calm to a radical, Jesus-centered way of living. It’s not about detaching from your thoughts; it’s about attaching to Him. Imagine pausing in the middle of a chaotic day and whispering, ‘Lord, You’re here.’ That’s not mindfulness — that’s Spirit-led awareness. We’ll dig into why your to-do list is often a mask for fear, how silence isn’t just ‘quiet time’ but a weapon against the noise that fuels addiction, and why true rest isn’t a reward for being ‘good’ but a daily declaration that your King already won. This isn’t about perfection. It’s about learning to rest so deeply in His presence that even when the storms come, your soul says, ‘No thanks — I’m resting now.’ Let’s go deeper together. Here's the truth we all need to face: a lot of us are running on fear. We're scared of failing, of being forgotten, of our past finally catching up to us. So we keep moving, thinking that if we just work hard enough, God will finally be happy with us. But that's a trap. It's this lie that says our value comes from our productivity, that our freedom is a prize we have to earn every single day. The problem is, when you tie your identity to your to-do list, every mistake feels like a disaster. You start believing that your recovery stats or your 'perfect streak' define you. But here's what I want you to hear: your standing with God was never about your effort. It's a gift. Jesus already did the hard work. You don't have to prove yourself to Him. When you try to 'buy' His favor with your performance, you're not just draining yourself—you're betraying the truth of the Gospel. So how do we fix this? By repenting of the rush. That means looking at your schedule, your goals, your endless tasks, and saying, 'Lord, I surrender this. I'm not going to let fear drive me anymore.' It means trusting that His grace is enough, that your freedom is already secured through His finished work. You don't have to be a prisoner fighting to escape; you're a child of the King learning to live in His palace. When you stop trying to earn approval and start resting in His love, the pressure disappears. You're not chasing results anymore—you're abiding in a Reality that can't be shaken. That's where true rest begins. Here's the thing: Jesus didn't wait for a quiet life to pray. Even when He was busy—like in Mark 1:35, where He got up before dawn to pray in a solitary place—He made stillness a priority. That’s the model we need. Sexual addiction feeds on noise, right? It thrives in the constant buzz of screens, podcasts, and endless distractions. We’re wired to fear silence because in that quiet, we might hear our own thoughts—or worse, feel the weight of our past. But here’s the truth: silence isn’t something to run from. It’s where we meet God. So reclaiming it means carving out 30 minutes a day—no phones, no music, no background noise. Just you, maybe staring at a tree, the sky, or sitting in a room where your heart can finally slow down. That’s where the magic happens. Your brain gets a break from the digital static, and your soul starts to breathe again. You realize you don’t need the next dopamine hit because you’re already saturated in His presence. It’s like hitting refresh on your whole being. You don’t have to fill the silence with noise to feel safe. In fact, the quieter you get, the louder God becomes. That’s the point. So start small: turn off your phone during your commute, take a walk without headphones, or sit in a chair and look at nothing for five minutes. Let the stillness wash over you. It’s not about being perfect—it’s about learning to rest in the One who already holds everything together. When you do this, you’re not just unplugging… you’re plugging into the Source. Think about it—when you're perpetually rushed, exhausted, or chasing deadlines, how can you even begin to share your heart with someone? You can't experience the quiet, deep kind of love if you're always sprinting. But when you take that long walk and finally slow down, that's when you start opening up to a friend about the stuff that really matters. When you're in stillness during worship, that's when you feel the Father's hand on your shoulder like He's saying, 'I'm right here.' And when you sit down to a Sabbath meal with your spouse, that's when the relationship starts feeling like a sacred promise instead of just a daily grind. Rest is prioritizing the Person—not your to-do list. It's saying, 'This relationship is more important than my productivity.' The more you ground yourself in the Father's love, the more the chaos of life becomes background noise. You're not just fighting battles anymore; you're walking through them with the King at your side, finding joy in the journey instead of just surviving it. What happens is your soul starts to thrive because you're no longer trying to earn worth through hustle—you're resting in the One who already loves you completely. Every nap you take, every walk in silence, every moment you turn off the noise and just be still—it's like throwing a punch at the enemy's plan to burn you out. Instead of scrambling to fight every attack, you're learning to live in His presence so consistently that the war becomes a quiet backdrop to your joy. It's like trading a sprint for a steady, unshakable stance. The enemy? He doesn't stand a chance when you're rooted in rest.
