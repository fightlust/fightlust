From Channels to Catalysts: The Warrior's Journey in Leadership and Restoration

This episode explores the transformative power of leadership in spiritual growth, emphasizing how giving to others sustains our own walk with God. We'll discuss the dangers of self-absorption, the joy of restoration, and practical steps to build teams while avoiding the 'Hero-Trap.' Learn how to lead with humility, steward Kingdom assets, and find lasting joy in setting others free.

================================================================

In the book fightlust, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we turn to Chapter 13 — Leading Others. This chapter drops a truth bomb about why leadership isn’t just about titles or programs but about survival. Picture this: the Sea of Galilee thrives because water flows in and out, but the Dead Sea dies because it only takes and never gives. That’s the warning here — if you soak up the Word, rest, and support but never pass it on, your spiritual life turns to a swamp. Leadership is the ultimate teacher. When you teach someone the reject method, you’re rewiring your own brain. When you help a brother ‘bounce’ his eyes away from temptation, you’re sharpening your own discipline. This isn’t just about helping others — it’s about locking truth into your soul. The chapter calls leadership the ‘final layer of heart surgery,’ flipping your motivation from ‘I need to stay clean’ to ‘I won’t let my squad down.’ But here’s the catch: you don’t wait for a title or a stage. You start now, where you are. Look around — is there someone in your middle circle, stuck in excuses, longing for a way out? That’s your first recruit. But beware the Hero-Trap — thinking you’re the savior. Your job isn’t to give advice or play strongman. It’s to point them to Jesus. And if you’re leading, your boundaries get stricter. No lone-wolf mentoring. No isolation with someone of the opposite sex. You’re a guide, not a crutch. And before you ever speak to your squad, you pray for them. That’s where real power hides — in the quiet, unseen intercession. Let’s unpack how to lead without burning out, how to guard your heart, and why the joy of restoring others beats every fake high this world sells. This is about becoming a channel, not a dam — and letting the overflow of your own healing flood into someone else’s life. Here's a truth I've learned the hard way: teaching others doesn't just help them—it sharpens you. When you walk a brother through the reject method, you're not just coaching him—you're drilling that process into your own brain. When you teach a sister to 'bounce' her eyes away from temptation, you're actually training your own reflexes to stay sharp. And when you help someone see their new identity in Christ, you're not just speaking truth into their life—you're stitching that same truth deeper into your own soul. Leadership isn't about being a hero; it's about becoming a conduit. The more you pour into others, the more you realize you're not just surviving for yourself anymore. You stop trying to 'stay clean' out of fear and start staying strong because you refuse to let your team down. It's like upgrading from self-management to Kingdom stewardship—your motivation shifts from 'I don't want to fail' to 'I won't let them fail.' The old you was all about avoiding the cage; the new you is about building a ladder out for others. And the best part? When you lead like this, you're not just reinforcing good habits—you're creating a ripple effect. Every time you help someone break free, you're proving to yourself that freedom is real. It's like training wheels for your faith: the more you guide others, the more you trust the system yourself. You become a living example of what you're teaching, and that's when the real transformation happens—not just for them, but for you. So here's the thing about building your squad—leadership doesn't start with a title or a fancy plan. It starts by looking around the people you already know, like the ones in your 'middle circle' who are stuck in struggle, feeling isolated, or just tired of fighting alone. You don't wait for permission or a perfect moment. You spot someone who's sick of their excuses, maybe someone who's carrying shame or hiding in the shadows, and you step in. The first move is a simple 60-second blitz: 'I’ve been where you are. I know that darkness. But the King set me free, and I want to stand in your foxhole with you.' That’s not a sales pitch—it’s an invitation to walk through the fire together. Then you get practical. Share the tools you’ve learned, like setting digital boundaries or walking through the basics of their morning routine. But here’s the catch: leadership isn’t about being a hero. If you start thinking you’re the one saving them, you’re falling into the 'Hero-Trap.' Your job isn’t to fix their problems or play the strong, wise mentor. Your job is to point them to the Commander who already did the real work. You don’t give them your advice—you give them God’s Word. You don’t offer your strength—you point them to Christ’s victory. If you try to be their savior, you’ll burn out fast and lead them into a new cage—one where they’re following you instead of following Jesus. So stay small. Keep the focus big. You’re a guide, not a destination. And the moment you remember that, you stop carrying the weight of their failures and start walking in the freedom of being a brother who’s just pointing the way home. So here's the thing about leadership—when you start helping others, you've got to watch out for this ego trap. It's easy to think you're the hero, the one saving people, right? But that's not how it works. You're not the destination; you're just a signpost pointing people to Jesus. If you start believing you're the fixer, the fixer will burn out. You don't give people your advice; you give them God's Word. You don't offer your strength; you point them to Christ's victory. You don't solve their problems—you help them find the Great Physician. The danger is when you let the praise or gratitude from others start to feed your ego. That’s identity vandalism. You were never the savior. Now, here’s another key part: don’t let someone become codependent on you. If they can’t make a move without your permission, you’ve accidentally become their crutch. Your job is to build believers, not dependents. And here’s a hard but important rule—never mentor someone of the opposite sex. The emotional intensity of healing can create unhealthy attachments. Protect your marriage and theirs. Also, never lead alone. You need your own Legion squad watching your back. If you don’t have accountability, you’re not a leader; you’re a liability. The goal is to lead people to the King, not to make them follow you. If you keep the focus on Jesus, you’ll avoid leading them into a new cage—one where they’re just following a man instead of the Commander. Stay small so the King stays big. That’s how you build a team of free people, not a group of followers who need you to survive. It’s all about stewarding the Kingdom’s assets, not your own ego. So, stay humble, stay accountable, and remember—you’re just the channel. The real power is in the One you point them to. Here’s the deal: leading others is a gift, but it’s not sustainable if you’re running on empty. Every day, you’ve got to hit the reset button by handing the weight of everyone’s struggles back to God. You can’t carry their sins, their failures, or their pain like it’s your own—because it’s not. At the end of the day, you stand at the altar and say, ‘Lord, these are Your kids. I’m just their brother.’ That’s the daily burden-exchange. It’s how you keep from burning out. If you try to be their hero, you’ll crumble under the pressure. But when you remember you’re not the savior, just a guide pointing them to Jesus, you find freedom. Now, here’s the kicker: your identity doesn’t hinge on how many people you ‘fix’ or how clean your squad stays. You’re a child of God first, a leader second. If your whole team relapses tomorrow, you’re still loved, still whole. That’s sonship—unshakable, no strings attached. The real fuel that keeps you going? Watching someone break free. It’s the moment shame cracks and light floods in, or a marriage starts healing after years of hurt. That joy? It’s addictive in the best way. It beats any fake high because it’s real, it’s Kingdom work. And before you even meet with them, you’re in prayer, locking spiritual shields with the people you lead. You’re not just giving advice—you’re shifting their atmosphere. That’s how you sustain through the long haul: overflow from your own fullness, not burnout from your own emptiness.
