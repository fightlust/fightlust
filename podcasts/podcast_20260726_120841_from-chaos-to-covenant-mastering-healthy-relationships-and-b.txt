From Chaos to Covenant: Mastering Healthy Relationships and Boundaries

In this episode, we tackle the urgent work of sorting your relational ecosystem. We’ll break down the Bullseye Protocol to identify who belongs in your inner circle, expose the hidden ways addiction rewires your intimacy patterns, and teach you how to recognize toxic dynamics before they derail your recovery. You’ll learn practical tools like the Fruit Audit to evaluate your relationships, discover the difference between codependency and covenant, and understand how to set godly boundaries that protect your progress without cutting off connection. This is the real work of freedom—building relationships that fuel your spiritual growth, not sabotage it.

================================================================

In the book fightlust, Book 3, *Shoes of the Gospel of Peace*, we turn to Chapter 21 — *Relational Health Check*. This chapter isn’t just about who’s in your life, but *why* they’re there and what they’re doing to your soul. I’ve talked before about how addiction doesn’t live in a vacuum — it’s fed by the people around you, the patterns you let slide, and the soil where your heart takes root. So here’s the hard truth: if you want to walk in freedom, you’ve got to audit your relationships. Imagine your life as three circles — the bullseye, the middle ring, and the outer edge. The bullseye? That’s your inner circle — three to five people who have total access to your soul. No gray-area friends. No enablers. Just those who’ll hold you in grace, speak truth, and let the Holy Spirit do His work. The middle circle? Your friends, family, coworkers — people you love and serve, but who don’t get to control your mind. And the outer circle? Acquaintances, the world at large — you’re a light to them, but you keep your distance. Most of us make the mistake of letting long-time ‘nice’ people into our bullseye just because. That’s a security breach. Cleaning up your soil is messy work. It might mean cutting ties with old crews, having hard talks with family, or feeling lonely for a season. But here’s the deal: you can’t keep fighting addiction while letting toxic relationships poison your recovery. The alternative — staying stuck in the mud — is worse. When you plant your new nature in Kingdom soil, purity becomes inevitable. The environment stops fighting you, and you start fighting for it. That’s the goal. Let’s dig in. Here's the thing about sexual sin—it doesn’t just mess up your purity. It rewires how you see people, period. Think about it: when you’re trapped in that cycle, you start seeing people as tools to hit your dopamine button. You stop seeing a person as someone made by God and start seeing them as a quick fix for your hunger. That’s objectification. Then there’s the lying—like, you get really good at hiding your mess, spinning stories to keep your secret life safe. You become a fake version of yourself, always playing a role. And isolation? That’s the kicker. You withdraw because real connection feels too risky. Why put in the work to be vulnerable when you can just scroll and get a hit in seconds? These habits don’t stay in your secret life. They leak into everything—your marriage, your friendships, even how you parent. You start tolerating people instead of loving them, performing for their approval instead of resting in God’s. The problem is, this creates what I call a ‘contractual’ mindset. You’re just trying to keep your head above water, scrambling to avoid disaster instead of thriving in the freedom Christ offers. You’re stuck in survival mode, not covenant mode. But here’s the good news: you can retrain your brain. It starts by admitting what’s been stolen—your ability to see people as sacred, to be honest, and to lean into real connection. Then you rebuild, step by step, replacing ‘What can I get?’ with ‘How can I serve?’ That’s the shift from consumption to covenant. It’s messy, it’s hard, but it’s possible. Your relationships don’t have to be a battlefield anymore. They can become a place where you grow, where you’re known, and where you finally stop hiding. Here’s the thing: even if you’ve got great people in your life, you can still be stuck in toxic patterns. Relationships that look okay on the surface can secretly be traps that hold you back. The first red flag is deception—when you or the other person can’t be totally honest. You start hiding parts of your life, spinning stories to protect your image, and that creates a cage instead of a safe space. Then there’s codependency, where your happiness or identity gets tied up in someone else’s moods or choices. You end up losing yourself, trying to fix their problems instead of being a true partner. Power imbalances are next—when one person’s emotions or needs control the whole relationship. You’re walking on eggshells, avoiding blowups, and bending over backward to keep the peace, but it’s all based on fear, not love. Lastly, stagnation: relationships that feel comfortable but never push you to grow. You’re just sitting in the same old patterns, making excuses for each other’s compromise, and telling yourselves it’s ‘not that bad.’ These dynamics don’t just hurt your freedom—they kill it. The key is learning to spot these traps so you can swap fake safety for real connection, where honesty, balance, and growth actually happen. It’s not about perfection; it’s about building relationships that lift you up instead of holding you down. Here’s how you know if a relationship is truly healthy—it doesn’t just feel good; it changes you. Look for four things: first, mutual honesty, where you can show up as your real self without masks or hiding. If you’re always spinning stories or keeping parts of your life locked away, it’s not a safe space. Second, authentic interdependence—this isn’t about clinging to each other or letting someone’s mess drain you. It’s about being allies, cheering each other on without losing your own peace. Third, growth through truth and grace. A healthy connection pushes you toward better habits, not just comfort. You should leave feeling inspired, not wiped out. And finally, emotional safety—you can admit struggles, say, ‘I messed up,’ and not fear judgment. If someone loves you, they’ll gently correct you when needed, not enable bad patterns. To check your top relationships, do a quick ‘Fruit Audit’: ask if this person makes you hide parts of yourself, if you feel energized or drained after talking, if you can be honest about slip-ups, and if they’ll tell you hard truths when you need them. If you find even one ‘no,’ it’s a red flag. Healthy relationships fuel your freedom; unhealthy ones feed your fear. It’s not about perfection—it’s about patterns. When you prioritize these markers, you’re not just keeping people in your life; you’re building alliances that actually help you grow. And that’s the kind of connection worth fighting for. Here’s the thing about boundaries: they’re not about shutting people out. They’re about protecting what matters most. Think of them like fences around your garden—gates that let the good stuff in and keep the weeds out. I know it’s easy to feel guilty saying no, especially when we’ve been taught that being a ‘yes man’ is somehow spiritual. But that’s a lie. Boundaries are how you guard your new life in Christ. God Himself set boundaries in Eden, gave the Ten Commandments, and even told the ocean where to stop. He’s the original boundary-setter, and He does it out of love, not control. Your recovery is like that garden I mentioned—beautiful, fragile, and under attack. If you leave the gate open for old triggers, toxic people, or unhealthy patterns, you’re asking for trouble. Boundaries aren’t mean; they’re honest. They say, ‘I’m not a cosmic doormat. I’m a child of the King with a mission.’ You’re not building walls to keep people out; you’re building fences to keep your freedom safe. This isn’t about being selfish—it’s about stewarding the gift of purity you’ve claimed. When you set a boundary, you’re not rejecting someone; you’re protecting your soul. And guess what? The more you practice this, the more you’ll see God honor your yeses with strength and your noes with peace. It’s the only way to keep walking in the light, unshaken and unashamed.
