From Leadership to Legacy: Mastering Humble Discipleship and Spiritual Mentorship

This episode unpacks the radical shift from personal freedom to Kingdom leadership, exploring how to build a team of believers, avoid the hero-trap, and lead with radical boundaries while staying connected to Jesus. Learn how intercessory prayer and the Holy Spirit’s guidance empower you to multiply freedom and create a legacy of restoration.

================================================================

In my book Only Faith, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we turn to Chapter 12 — The 3-3-3 Testimony — where I explain how your story becomes a weapon for the Kingdom. Imagine your life as a flare shot into the night, signaling to someone trapped in darkness that help is coming. That’s the power of a concise testimony: it takes your mess, the miracle God did, and the message He wants to share, and turns it into a lifeline for others. But here’s the thing — if you’re still tangled up in your own drama, if you’re letting shame or pride twist your story into something that glorifies you instead of Jesus, you’ll miss the point entirely. This chapter pushes you to dig into your own story with honesty. Where’s the part of your past that distracts from Christ? What’s holding you back from sharing a testimony that makes Him the hero? And who can keep you accountable as you practice this? Once you’ve sharpened your own message, the next step isn’t just talking — it’s leading. Because freedom isn’t meant to stay in your pocket; it’s meant to be handed out. And that’s where the real work begins: building a squad, recruiting from the shadows, and learning how to lead without becoming a hero. We’ll get there, but first, let’s start with your story — and why it matters more than you think. Leadership starts by looking in your middle circle—friends, coworkers, family—for someone who’s stuck in the same old struggles. Maybe they’re tired of the cycle but don’t know how to break free. That’s your first recruit. Don’t wait for a perfect moment or a title; just reach out. Use a quick, honest 60-second story to share how Jesus set you free. Say something like, ‘I was in your shoes, but the King showed me a way out, and I want to walk through this with you.’ Then, invite them into a small group, like a fire-team, where you can walk them through the basics—tools like setting digital boundaries to protect their focus and creating their own 3-3-3 Testimony to share their journey. The 3-3-3 Testimony helps them name their mess, their miracle, and their message, making Jesus the hero of their story. But here’s the key: your job isn’t to fix them. You’re not their savior. You’re a guide, pointing them to Jesus. Help them find their own strength in Him, not in you. Avoid codependency by setting clear boundaries—be there to support, not to carry them. If they start relying too much on you, gently push them back to the King. Stay accountable by having your own Legion squad watching your back, and make sure you’re not getting too close in a way that could lead to unhealthy attachments. Leadership is about lighting the way, not being the destination. Let Jesus do the real work, and keep your focus on Him. Remember, if you try to be the hero, you’ll fall into the hero-trap, thinking you’re the one saving them. That’s not your role. Your role is to be a signpost, pointing them to the Commander who already did the hard work. That’s how you build a squad that’s strong, free, and ready to lead others too. Here’s the thing about leading others—your biggest danger isn’t failing, it’s thinking you’re the hero. I’ve seen guys fall into this trap where they start believing their own hype, like they’re the one saving people. They crave the gratitude, the respect, the sense of being indispensable. But here’s the truth: when you let that pride take root, you’re not pointing people to Jesus—you’re making them dependent on you. It’s a spiritual dead end. The enemy loves it because he knows if you become the ‘fix’ in someone’s life, you’ll burn out fast, and worse, you’ll lead them into a new cage where they’re just following you instead of following Christ. A real leader doesn’t take credit for someone’s breakthroughs. They don’t offer their own strength or advice. They stay small, stay humble, and keep Jesus front and center. Your job is to be a signpost, not a destination. If you try to be the savior, you’ll crash hard. But when you partner with the Holy Spirit and let Him do the work, you become a channel for God’s grace. That’s how you lead without burning out—by staying rooted in obedience, not ego. Your authority doesn’t come from how many people look up to you; it comes from how faithfully you follow the King. So guard your heart. Don’t let the rush of being needed turn into a habit of being worshipped. Keep your eyes on Jesus, and let Him be the hero of everyone’s story—including yours. Here’s the deal, if you want to lead others without burning out or getting derailed, you’ve got to build unbreakable boundaries. First, never mentor someone in isolation—always have your Legion squad watching your back. You’re not a lone wolf; you need accountability. If you try to lead from a dark room, the enemy will find the cracks in your armor and exploit them. That’s how he takes down leaders. Second, stick to strict gender lines. Never mentor someone of the opposite sex. The emotional intensity of healing can create dangerous connections, and you’re not there to be a hero or a crutch. Protect your marriage and theirs—it’s not worth the risk. And if someone starts leaning on you like they can’t breathe without your permission, you’ve got to push them toward Jesus. Your job isn’t to fix them; it’s to point them to the One who already did. If your spiritual armor isn’t stronger than the people you lead, the enemy will come at you harder, and your whole team will fall apart. You can’t pour from an empty cup, so keep your own life clean and your connection to God fresh. Boundaries aren’t about being cold—they’re about protecting your mission and theirs. Without them, you’ll end up dragging others down with you. So build your hedges, stay sharp, and let Jesus be the center of everything. That’s how you lead with integrity and keep the mission moving forward. Here’s the thing—your most powerful move as a leader happens when no one’s watching. I’ve learned that before you ever open your mouth to a brother you’re trying to help, you’ve got to get on your knees. Prayer isn’t just a checklist; it’s the secret weapon. You’re not just asking God to fix their problems—you’re asking Him to heal the cracks in their heart they don’t even know about. Maybe their three-second window is weak, or their family’s under pressure they’re hiding. You pray for their deep roots, for their soil to be clean, for their wife and kids to be shielded from whatever fallout might come. The Holy Spirit is your partner here. He’ll show you what’s really going on under the surface of their ‘I’m fine’ mask. You ask Him for wisdom to see the real issue, for discernment to know what they’re carrying that they won’t say out loud. When you do this, you’re not just a mentor—you’re a spiritual dad. Your prayers lock shields with them before the enemy even shows up. You’re building unity in the spirit, so when they face their battles, they feel your coverage even when you’re not in the room. It’s not about you being the hero; it’s about making sure Jesus is front and center in their story. And the truth is, the more you pray first, the more you’ll realize you’re not leading alone. The Spirit’s the one doing the heavy lifting. Your job is to stay in sync with Him and let Him use your mouth to speak truth into the chaos. That’s how you become a channel for real change—without burning out, without becoming their savior, just pointing them to the Commander who already set you both free.
