From Reframing to Renewing: Mastering the Mind of Christ

This episode explores the journey from cognitive reframing to total mental renewal in Christ, emphasizing how believers can reclaim their imagination, cultivate holy curiosity, and transform their thinking into an act of worship. Through group accountability, the Holy Spirit's guidance, and intentional focus on God's beauty, we learn to move beyond managing lies to occupying the freedom already won in Jesus.

================================================================

In my book fightlust, Book 5, The Helmet of Salvation, we dive into a game-changer for your mind — Cognitive Reframing. Let me paint a picture: imagine your thoughts are a war zone, and the enemy’s lies are so loud they drown out truth. That’s when you need your Legion squad — your spiritual brothers — to help you rewire your thinking. You say, “Hey, I’m buying this lie that I’m a burden,” and they hit reset on your brain with the logic of a child of God. This isn’t just about fixing a broken thought; it’s about shifting from a mindset of slavery to one of sonship. But here’s the next level: reframing is the first aid, and renewing your mind is the full-scale renovation. I talk about tearing down the graffiti of old lies and rebuilding your mind on the blueprint of Jesus’ beauty. You’re not just blocking bad thoughts anymore; you’re flooding your soul with the awe of who God is. The enemy stole chunks of your imagination — your escape plans, your reward system, your memories — and now you’re evicting him, room by room. And this isn’t a solo mission; the Holy Spirit is your co-builder, turning your focus from ‘don’t sin’ to ‘delight in the King.’ By the end, your thinking becomes worship, your mind a weapon for God’s mission. Sound too good? Stick around — we’re about to unlock the freedom you’ve been fighting for. Reframing is like patching a hole in your coat, but renewing your mind is like tearing down a run-down house and rebuilding it from the ground up with the right blueprint. That’s what Romans 12:2 means when it says to be transformed by the renewing of your mind. It’s not just about fixing a few bad habits or tweaking your thinking—it’s about completely redoing the way you see the world. When you focus on short-term fixes, you’re just holding things together with duct tape. But when you dive deeper into the beauty of Jesus and let His victory saturate your mind, those old temptations start to look boring. It’s not about gritting your teeth and forcing yourself to be good; it’s about being so captivated by who Christ is that you naturally avoid the wrong stuff. Your imagination, memories, and even how you look forward to the future all start to line up with His truth. The ‘grey areas’ that used to pull you in lose their power because you’re no longer just trying to avoid sin—you’re living in the freedom of His finished work. This isn’t about forcing your way through life; it’s about surrendering to a story that’s way better than anything the enemy could offer. Imagine your mind is a house that’s been taken over by a squatter. The drafting room where you’re supposed to plan your life is now a junkyard of shame, lust, and fear. The guy who’s been living there? He’s been storing up toxic memories, replaying old failures, and wiring your brain to chase the next dopamine hit. But here’s the deal—you’re the owner. It’s time to kick him out. Start by visualizing what your future could look like. Not the escape rituals or the shame cycles, but the real legacy you’re building. Maybe it’s the men you’ll lead to freedom, or the family you’ll protect, or the ministry you’ll plant. Let that future become your new blueprint. Next, go through the ‘archive’ of your mind—the memories that keep haunting you. Swap the toxic stories with Scripture that wins. If a memory of failure pops up, replace it with a verse about victory. If a lustful image tries to sneak in, let a truth about your worth in Christ drown it out. The same goes for your ‘reward system.’ You’ve been training your brain to crave screens, chemicals, or quick fixes. Now, train it to crave something better: the peace of morning worship, the joy of serving others, or the thrill of your next step in God’s plan. This isn’t about white-knuckling your thoughts or forcing yourself to think ‘positive.’ It’s about taking ownership. Audit every room of your mind—your imagination, your memories, your anticipation—and declare that Jesus is the only landlord now. You’re not just blocking bad ideas anymore; you’re redecorating the whole palace. When you walk into that drafting room, you’re not a prisoner—you’re the designer. And the more you practice this, the more your mind becomes a sanctuary for the King instead of a storage unit for the enemy. It’s not a quick fix. It’s a daily choice to occupy the space you’ve already won. Here's the thing about the quiet times in recovery—they’re the enemy’s favorite moment to strike. When the pressure eases and everything feels calm, that’s when your guard naturally drops. That’s when small lies start to creep in, gaining ground until they become full-blown strongholds. Conditioned vigilance means keeping your helmet on even when the sky looks clear. It means doing your morning proclamations even when you ‘feel’ fine, staying transparent with your Legion squad even when everything seems under control, and refusing to let ‘grey areas’ erode your foundation. The long middle of recovery—the part that’s not a crisis but also not yet full victory—is where your training becomes your nature. This is where you practice drills to keep your focus sharp, like reading Scripture aloud, checking in with your accountability partner, or pausing to ask, ‘Is this thought from the Spirit or the enemy?’ Vigilance isn’t about being paranoid; it’s about building habits that stick. It’s the bridge between the tactics you’ve learned—like reframing lies—and the deeper transformation of your heart. When you stay sharp in the quiet seasons, you’re not just surviving; you’re training your soul to live in the reality of your freedom. That’s how you move from ‘not sinning’ to ‘radiating victory.’ Hey there, let me break this down for you. The bottom line is, trying to fix your mind by yourself is like trying to build a house with just a hammer—you need the Master Builder, the Holy Spirit. He’s the one who takes God’s Word and makes it alive in your soul. So stop thinking of faith as a bunch of strict rules to follow. Instead, ask Him questions like, “Lord, show me how You think about this problem” or “What’s the hidden manna in this truth?” This isn’t about checking boxes—it’s about adventure. When you start exploring God’s wisdom like it’s an endless ocean, you’ll never get bored. The enemy loves stagnation, but holy curiosity keeps you moving. Your mind becomes a place where wonder battles old habits. And here’s the kicker: when you let worship be your default mode, your thoughts start changing the atmosphere everywhere you go. It’s not about gritting your teeth and forcing yourself to think positive. It’s about letting the Holy Spirit renew your mind so naturally that thinking God’s truth feels as easy as breathing. You’re not just managing your thoughts anymore—you’re living in the freedom of the King, and that freedom radiates out. So today, ask Him a question. Let your curiosity lead you deeper, and watch how His perspective turns your mind into a temple of awe and peace.
