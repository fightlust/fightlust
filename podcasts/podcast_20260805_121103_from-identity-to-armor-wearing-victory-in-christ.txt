From Identity to Armor: Wearing Victory in Christ

This episode explores how embracing your new identity in Christ fuels daily spiritual warfare. You'll learn the five pillars of your transformed self—New Creation, Righteousness, Conquering Victory, God's Masterpiece, and Chosen Holiness—and how to operationalize these truths through Ephesians 6's 'Daily Armor Routine.' Discover practical steps to guard your heart, anchor your reality in truth, and march forward in peace, turning your identity into actionable freedom.

================================================================

In the book *fightlust*, Book 2, *The Breastplate of Righteousness*, we turn to Chapter 16 — *Freedom Is a Daily Walk* — where I unpack a truth that changed everything for me: freedom in Christ isn’t a one-time event; it’s a rhythm, a daily choice to live from who you *are* in Him, not just what you *do*. Many of us get stuck thinking, ‘I’m an addict trying to get better’ — but that’s a setup for burnout. The Gospel says something bolder: when you surrendered to Christ, you didn’t just get a fresh start; you got a *new nature*. You’re not ‘wired for sin’ — you’re being rewired for glory. In this chapter, I walk through five pillars of your new identity — like being a ‘new creation,’ ‘righteous,’ ‘more than a conqueror,’ God’s ‘masterpiece,’ and ‘chosen and holy’ — and show how these truths are your anchor when the enemy whispers lies. But here’s the thing: knowing these truths isn’t enough. You’ve got to *wear them* daily, like armor. That’s where Chapter 18 kicks in, breaking down how to ‘put on the whole armor of God’ every morning — from the Belt of Truth to the Breastplate of Righteousness — so you’re not just fighting for freedom, but fighting *from* it. This isn’t about perfection; it’s about stepping into the reality of who you already are in Christ and letting that shape every choice. So if you’ve ever felt like your past defines you, or like you’re just ‘trying hard enough,’ this is for you. Let’s dig into how freedom becomes your default, not your exception. The Belt of Truth is like the foundation of your spiritual armor—it’s the thick leather strap that keeps everything in place so you can move freely without tripping. Think of it as a soldier’s first step before battle: you don’t just grab a sword and run in; you secure your gear so you’re ready to fight. This belt isn’t just about knowing facts or Bible verses. It’s about letting the truth of God’s Word sink into your mind and heart so deep that it becomes your reality. Every morning, you’re anchoring yourself in who God says you are, His perfect design for your life, and the promises He gives when temptation comes. It’s like saying, ‘I refuse to be tripped up by lies the enemy throws at me or the messed-up messages our culture shouts.’ You’re not just memorizing truth—you’re living from it. When you strap on this belt through prayer, you’re declaring, ‘My life is built on the solid ground of God’s Kingdom, not the shifting sand of my fears or the world’s distortions.’ It’s about training your brain to see the world through His eyes, so when the old lies try to creep in—like ‘You’re broken’ or ‘You’ll never change’—you already have the truth standing guard. This isn’t magic; it’s muscle memory. The more you practice it, the easier it becomes to reject lies and walk in the freedom He’s already given you. So every day, you’re not just putting on a belt. You’re putting on a lifetime of truth that keeps you steady, focused, and ready to fight from a place of peace, not panic. The breastplate of righteousness is your shield for the heart, lungs, and gut—those parts of you that hold your emotions, sense of self, and gut instincts. Think of it like this: when the enemy tries to hit you with shame, guilt, or comparisons, the breastplate is what stops those arrows cold. It’s not about pretending you’re perfect—it’s about remembering that in Christ, you’re already declared righteous. God sees you as holy and blameless because of Jesus’ work, not because of your performance. So when you start feeling like a failure or you compare yourself to others, that’s the enemy trying to chip away at your identity. The breastplate reminds you to say, ‘No, I’m covered in Christ’s victory.’ It’s like wearing a suit of armor that shouts, ‘My worth isn’t up for debate!’ Every time you declare that you’re God’s righteousness, you’re not just reciting a verse—you’re actively guarding your heart from lies that try to make you feel small. This isn’t about ignoring your struggles; it’s about fighting from a place of freedom. You don’t have to earn your value. You get to live from it. So when temptation comes, don’t ask, ‘Can I survive this?’ Ask, ‘How does this fit my new identity?’ That’s the power of the breastplate—keeping your core protected so you can walk in confidence, not fear. Here’s the thing about the Shoes of the Gospel of Peace—think of them like those ancient Roman boots with iron studs that let soldiers grip muddy battlefields. Your spiritual ‘terrain’ is messy, right? Life throws up all kinds of sludge—doubt, temptation, isolation, or just the daily grind that makes you want to retreat into old habits. Those shoes give you traction so you don’t slip back into the ditch of isolation or the grey areas you’re trying to leave behind. The peace here isn’t about ignoring problems or pretending everything’s perfect. It’s the deep, unshakable knowing that you’re ‘at one’ with God because of Jesus. That peace is your emotional anchor, keeping your feet steady even when the path feels shaky. And it’s not just about you—it’s about staying connected to your spiritual ‘Legion,’ the people walking this road with you. When you’re marching in peace, you’re not alone; you’re part of a team. That connection fuels your momentum, so you don’t get stuck in the muck of shame or the fog of loneliness. The enemy loves to isolate you, to make you think you’re fighting this battle solo. But these shoes remind you: you’re on solid ground, your feet secured by the Gospel. Every step you take is powered by the peace that says, ‘You’re already free, already loved, already part of something bigger.’ So when the terrain gets muddy—like when old patterns whisper, ‘Just one slip won’t hurt’—those iron-stud shoes dig in. They keep you moving forward, not because you’re perfect, but because you’re walking in the reality of peace that no lie can steal. It’s the difference between tripping over your past and marching boldly into the restoration God’s already writing for you. So here’s the deal with the Daily Armor Routine—it’s like your morning prep for spiritual combat. Every day, you spend five minutes intentionally ‘putting on’ the armor God gives in Ephesians 6, not just as a ritual but as a way to anchor yourself in who you are in Christ. Think of it like a soldier suiting up before a mission: you don’t show up in pajamas, right? You gear up because you know the fight’s real. The Belt of Truth is first—it’s your foundation. You declare, ‘Lord, I strap on the Belt of Truth!’ You’re reminding yourself that God’s Word is your ultimate truth, not the lies the enemy throws at you. Then comes the Breastplate of Righteousness, which guards your heart and emotions. You pray, ‘Father, I put on the Breastplate of Righteousness!’ Because you’re not fighting for righteousness; you’re fighting from the righteousness Jesus already gave you. Next, the Shoes of the Gospel of Peace give you stability. You say, ‘Lord, I lace up the Shoes of Peace!’ Those iron-stud boots keep you grounded on the rock of your salvation, so you don’t slip back into old habits or isolation. The whole routine is about re-focusing on God’s command, activating your awareness that you’re in a spiritual war, and securing your identity as His child. It’s not about being perfect—it’s about living from the victory you already have. You’re not trying to earn freedom; you’re stepping into the freedom you’ve been given. This daily practice turns your morning from just another day into a strategic move. It’s like a mental reset button that says, ‘Today, I’m not going to let lies or old patterns define me. I’m walking in the power of the new life God gave me.’ And when you do this consistently, it trains your brain to default to truth, peace, and righteousness, not fear or shame. Over time, the armor becomes second nature, and you start to see your choices shift because your identity is no longer up for debate. You’re not just surviving the battle—you’re living from the victory that’s already yours.
