The Healing Walk: Forgiveness, Self-Grace, and the Power of Professional Support

This episode explores the three movements of the Healing Walk—Forgiveness, Self-Forgiveness, and Recruiting Professional Help—to unlock integrated wholeness. Learn how to release bitterness, accept grace for yourself, and strategically build your support team to move from crisis to radiant freedom.

================================================================

In the book *fightlust*, Book 3, *Shoes of the Gospel of Peace*, we turn to Chapter 16 — *The Healing Journey* — where Jesus extends His invitation to all who are weary and burdened. This isn’t just about fixing what’s broken; it’s about walking a path where forgiveness, self-mercy, and professional help collide to heal deep wounds. Picture this: you’re lying on an operating table, but instead of a surgeon with a scalpel, it’s the Master Himself, offering rest for your soul. The chapter breaks down the Healing Walk into three movements — giving up the right to be angry, receiving grace for yourself, and recruiting help when the pain runs too deep. It’s not about pretending the hurt never happened or white-knuckling your way through shame. It’s about tearing up the ledger of bitterness, letting go of the mask you’ve been hiding behind, and trusting the Surgeon to bind up your wounds. If you’ve ever felt stuck in a cycle of guilt or used distraction to numb old pain, this lesson is your roadmap to freedom. We’re diving into how to forgive others without letting them back into your life, how to stop punishing yourself for sins already paid for, and why asking for help isn’t a weakness — it’s a strategic move. This isn’t a quick fix; it’s a journey where you trade microwave expectations for the slow, transformative work of the Great Physician. Let’s walk through the steps together — because healing isn’t just about surviving your past, it’s about stepping into a future where your heart is liquid with life, not frozen in fear. Forgiveness isn’t about pretending the hurt didn’t happen or letting someone off the hook—it’s about cutting yourself free from the emotional chains you’ve been carrying. Think of it like a debt: when someone hurts you, they steal a piece of you—your trust, your peace, maybe even your identity. You start keeping a mental ledger of all the ways they owe you. But holding onto that debt is like tying yourself to a person who’s already caused you pain. It creates this pressure in your soul that doesn’t let up, and for a lot of people, that pressure becomes a trigger for addiction or self-destructive habits. The truth is, forgiveness is a legal move. In the Bible, the word for forgiveness literally means ‘cancel a debt’ or ‘send it away.’ It’s not about feeling okay with what happened—it’s about saying, ‘I’m done letting this control me.’ You don’t have to forget the hurt, but you stop letting it define you. You tear up that ledger, hand the case over to God, and choose to walk in freedom. This doesn’t mean you have to reconcile with the person who hurt you or let them back into your life. Sometimes, forgiveness means setting a hard boundary while still releasing the emotional weight. And here’s the kicker: the hardest person to forgive is often yourself. You might believe God has already forgiven you, but if you’re still punishing yourself with shame, you’re basically saying your standards are higher than His. That’s not humility—it’s pride. True self-forgiveness means accepting that Jesus already paid your debt in full. It’s not about being weak or letting yourself off easy; it’s about trusting the One who created you to handle the mess you’ve made. When you do this, you stop fueling the pressure-cooker of bitterness and start creating space for healing. So how do you actually do it? Start by writing down the specific people or situations you’re still holding onto. Name the debt they owe you. Then, in Jesus’ name, declare it cancelled. Ask the Holy Spirit to fill the empty space with peace. It’s not a magic fix—it’s a step toward breaking the cycle. Because here’s the thing: you can’t keep living in the past and expect to walk free. Forgiveness is the key that unlocks the door. Here's a tough one: forgiving yourself. You know how some folks think they gotta punish themselves for past mistakes? Like, 'I did this bad thing, so I deserve to feel awful forever'? That's a lie. Let me tell you why. When you cling to shame, you're basically saying your standards are higher than God's. You're acting like Jesus' sacrifice on the cross wasn't enough for you—like your guilt is bigger than His grace. But that's not true. The Bible says Christ paid the full price for every sin, including yours. If the High King of Heaven declares you 'not guilty,' then your job isn't to keep prosecuting yourself. That's spiritual arrogance. It's like telling God, 'Nope, I know better than You.' So here's the deal: self-forgiveness isn't about letting yourself off the hook. It's about accepting the hook is already off. You’re not minimizing your past—you’re trusting that God’s mercy covers it. When you hold onto self-punishment, you’re keeping your heart open to shame, which just feeds the cycle of guilt and compulsion. But when you choose to forgive yourself, you’re sealing that wound with grace. You’re saying, 'I believe what Christ did for me is enough. I walk free.' It takes humility, but it’s the only way to stop letting your past define you. You’re not your worst mistake. You’re a beloved child of God, and your identity is in Him, not in what someone else did to you or what you did to yourself. So stop carrying that extra weight. Lay it down. The Surgeon already healed you—now let that truth sink in. Hey there, friend. Let’s talk about something real. You’ve been working hard on this healing journey, right? But here’s the truth: sometimes the wounds we carry are deeper than we can dig out alone. It’s not a weakness to admit that. Think of it like this—if you’re trying to fix a broken pipe underground, and it’s buried so deep you can’t reach it, you don’t keep digging with a shovel. You call in the pros. Christian counselors are like the specialists in your spiritual fire team. They’ve got the tools and training to untangle knots you can’t see, to help you navigate pain that feels too heavy or confusing. The enemy loves to lie to us, making us think asking for help is a sign of failure. But that’s the opposite of the truth. Building a Legion—your team of support—is actually the smart move. It’s how you turn isolation into strength. You’re not just relying on your own willpower anymore; you’re leveraging the expertise of people who’ve walked this path before. They don’t fix you, but they walk beside you, holding the light so you can see the roots of your hurt and how to heal them. And guess what? This isn’t about shame or pride. It’s about leadership. High-level leaders know when to bring in backup. So if you’re feeling stuck, or if your heart feels like it’s stuck in a loop of pain, don’t be afraid to reach out. That counselor isn’t here to judge you—they’re there to partner with you, to help you name the wounds, untie the knots, and keep moving toward freedom. Your healing isn’t a solo mission. It’s a team effort, and every strong warrior knows when to call in the reinforcements. So take that step. Your Legion is waiting to help you win. So, let’s break it down. First, grab a journal and write your Admissions Journal. Answer Jesus’ question from John 5:6—‘Do you want to be made well?’ Be honest about what you’re giving up, like clinging to victimhood or excuses. Then, identify your ‘old bandage’—maybe it’s busyness, cynicism, or overworking—and take a moment to rip it off and hand it to God. Next, schedule a ‘Rest Session’ where you spend 20 minutes meditating on Matthew 11:28. Picture yourself handing your heavy pack of shame and guilt to Jesus, feeling the weight lift off your shoulders. Memorize key verses like Psalm 147:3, which says, ‘He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds,’ and Colossians 3:13 about forgiving as Christ forgave you. Use the 3x5x7 system to make these truths stick in your mind. Finally, declare freedom over your past with phrases like, ‘I choose to be made well!’ or ‘My heart is liquid and alive!’—let these declarations replace old lies with truth. This isn’t about quick fixes or pretending everything’s perfect; it’s about trusting the Master Surgeon’s timing and taking one step of obedience at a time. When you stumble, don’t spiral into shame—say, ‘I’m forgiven! I rise and keep walking!’ and keep your focus on the healing journey, not the speed. By doing this, you’re not just managing your pain; you’re moving toward radiant wholeness, one honest moment at a time.
